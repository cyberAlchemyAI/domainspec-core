## Return R4: Christensen, Clayton - Wedge Read

The smallest buyer-visible wedge is: install one governed AI-dev workflow that makes agentic development trustworthy enough to use. Do not lead with Arcanum, Craft, validators, or a platform. The buyer sees a narrow service outcome: one workflow where intent is human-owned, agents execute inside a governed path, review is legible/unskippable, and the trail can later become auditor-grade attestation. Sources: `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:94`, `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:96`, `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:107-110`.

The first customer job is: "Help me put agents into my SDLC without multiplying intent bugs or breaking change-control/audit trust." The concrete first client already wants a governed agentic system embedded in his development lifecycle, and the stated risk is that raw agents at scale create intent bugs at scale. Sources: `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:105`, `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:107`, `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:111`.

The onboarding offer should be a fixed 2-4 week AI-dev readiness sprint: map the client's SDLC, pick one workflow, define "done correctly," then build the Phase 1 MVP: spec -> sandboxed agent implementation -> derived tests -> alignment audit -> human gate -> merge -> ledger entry. Sources: `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:97`, `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:109`.

The proof obligation is not "AI reduces review." It is: prove review time and escaped-intent-bug outcomes while showing the governed loop catching an intent bug. The synthesis explicitly says there is no evidence yet that these tools cut escaped intent bugs, so the offer must instrument escaped-intent-bug and review-time metrics; credibility comes from a reproducible demo, teardown essay, and first case studies. Sources: `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:81`, `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:89`, `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:101-102`.

What should remain free: validators/audits, spec-to-test derivation, extraction, taxonomies, Arcanum sigils, Necronomicon, Craft ledger schema/type/lane/blocker discipline, and intent tools. The synthesis is explicit that the adoption wedge is free, while the paid moat is governed enforcement, automation, metering, vertical deployment, and vendor-neutral immutable auditor-acceptable attestation. Sources: `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:35`, `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:37-42`, `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:44-50`.

Mandatory framing: "relocate and legibilize human intent review, never reduce it." The human owns the spec/intent ground truth, and gates add friction at intent-critical points as a feature, not a bug. Sources: `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:79-80`, `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:87`, `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:110`.

## Return R5: Simon, Herbert - Architecture Decomposition

The CyberAlchemy product architecture is already coherent. It is a governed agentic knowledge/execution stack whose product promise is to reduce residue between intent/schema and observed reality/evidence, then promote repeated operational learning through review gates rather than memory shortcuts. Sources: `cyberAlchemy/agentic-system-definition.md`, `cyberAlchemy/agentic-system-architecture.md`, `cyberAlchemy/agentic-system-knowledge-map.md`.

Components:

| Component | Boundary | Core capability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Philosophy/Intent Plane | Owns CyberAlchemy thesis: symmetry, drift/convergence, residue reduction. Does not own execution mechanics. | Gives the product its evaluation lens: intent must stay traceable to evidence. |
| Knowledge Governance Plane | Owns ontology branches, confidence, promotion, bridge discipline. Does not treat raw memory as authority. | Turns source-grounded knowledge into governed meaning. |
| Inventory Substrate | Owns source summaries, extracted claims, tags, contradictions, provenance, lookup indexes. Does not own final meaning or route authority. | Makes scattered corpus knowledge reusable without copying raw sources. |
| Operational Ontology | Candidate fourth ontology branch for agent-operation knowledge: routes, lessons, failures, context solutions, maintenance proposals. | Gives agents reusable, scoped operational knowledge with confidence and expiry. |
| Memory and Context Plane | Owns active interaction, session memory, route ledger, side notes, workflow hints. Does not become canonical without promotion. | Preserves continuity and emits candidates for review. |
| Context Solution Cards | Boundary layer between memory/ontology and execution; task-shaped retrieval, not a wiki dump. | Maps execution fingerprints to recommended route, evidence, pitfalls, confidence, expiry. |
| Execution Plane | Owns route selection and bounded work through Necronomicon, invoke, task-session, AEO, skills/sigils/spells/agents. | Executes selected lifecycle routes and emits outcome evidence. |
| Observability/Learning Plane | Owns telemetry, reflection, tuning signals, maintenance proposals. Does not mutate capabilities without owner approval. | Converts execution into candidate lessons and improvement proposals. |
| Research/Decision Plane | Owns MARS-style evidence lifecycle and MOGT-style objective-vector route policy for contested choices. | Validates whether the operating model improves route quality, cost, latency, safety, evidence completeness, or user burden. |

Dependency order: L0 Define Baseline, L1 Ontology Shape, L2 Memory Bridge, L3 Signal Bridge, L4 Retrieval Behavior, L5 Maintenance Loop, L6 Research Validation. Sources: `cyberAlchemy/agentic-system-definition.md`, `cyberAlchemy/agentic-system-implementation-layering.md`, `cyberAlchemy/agentic-system-ontology-entry-model.md`, `cyberAlchemy/agentic-system-architecture.md`, `cyberAlchemy/agentic-system-knowledge-map.md`.

The first working slice is the proof loop: source -> inventory -> ontology -> context -> execution -> telemetry/reflection. It deliberately excludes full repo ingest, canonical promotion, automated retrieval, runtime storage, and complete Operational Ontology. Sources: `cyberAlchemy/agentic-system-first-working-slice.md`, `cyberAlchemy/agentic-system-inventory-ontology-pipeline.md`.

Architecture gaps: Operational Ontology schema is not validated; storage authority is undecided; privacy policy for User Workflow Model is unresolved; cross-ledger signal vocabulary is not unified; Context Solution storage/retrieval mechanics are undefined; meta-model promotion criteria remain candidate-level; first slice has not yet produced artifacts/evidence.

## Return R7: Porter, Michael - External Market And Moat Scan

The paid moat is defensible only if it is cross-tool governed evidence, not "AI governance" in the abstract. The repo thesis is right: free wedge is legibility and method; paid moat is signed, vendor-neutral, auditor-usable evidence over AI-assisted work. That matches current pressure from AI governance regimes, secure SDLC expectations, and agentic AI security guidance, but the compliance claim must stay carefully bounded.

AI governance is now real buyer pressure: EU AI Act implementation is live in phases, GPAI obligations started in 2025, and high-risk rules now have a post-Omnibus timeline into 2027/2028; high-risk guidance is still draft/non-binding guidance, not settled law. The Commission's own summary emphasizes risk management, logging, documentation, human oversight, cybersecurity, and accuracy for high-risk AI systems.

NIST and ISO are pushing buyers toward management-system evidence: NIST AI RMF is voluntary but explicitly aimed at incorporating trustworthiness into AI design, development, use, and evaluation; NIST also launched an AI Agent Standards Initiative focused on secure, interoperable agents. ISO/IEC 42001 gives a certifiable AI management-system frame around risk, traceability, transparency, reliability, and continual improvement.

For SOC 2/ITGC/SOX-style pressure, the defensible claim is: existing control regimes already require evidence for change, control effectiveness, and reliable financial reporting. AICPA TSC is the SOC 2 control-criteria base; PCAOB AS 2201 governs ICFR audits; SEC 404 guidance centers management's internal-control assessment and supporting evidence. None of those primary sources says "AI-generated code requires cryptographic attestation."

Agentic AI specifically increases the need for identity, privilege, monitoring, and accountability evidence. The joint NSA/CISA/Five Eyes guidance names privilege, design/configuration, behavior, structural, and accountability risks, and recommends incremental deployment, strong governance, monitoring, and human oversight.

Generic AI governance is already crowded: Credo AI, IBM watsonx.governance, Drata, and Vanta all position around AI GRC, NIST/ISO/EU AI Act mappings, evidence collection, controls, and trust workflows.

Agent observability is also commoditizing: OpenTelemetry has GenAI semantic conventions in a dedicated repo; LangSmith, Langfuse, and Phoenix already sell or ship tracing, evals, token/cost tracking, and agent/tool-call visibility.

AI coding governance is moving inside incumbent platforms: GitHub Copilot has audit logs, cloud-agent access policy, branch/PR workflow, and agent activity records; GitLab Duo has AI audit event reports; Cursor and Microsoft also market enterprise security/audit controls.

The repo's internal split is coherent. `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:35` says validators/method are wedge while engine/governance/attestation/vertical are moat; the paid line is "cross-tool, vendor-neutral, cryptographically immutable, auditor-acceptable attestation" in `implementation/domainspec/development/COMMERCIALIZATION-SYNTHESIS.md:49`. The seam decision hardens that: public `arcanum` gets schema/method/plain-language concepts, while runtime, metering, attestation product, formal proof, and verticals remain private in `docs/decisions/domainspec-into-arcanum-wedge-moat-seams.md:21`.

Main seam risk: public `arcanum` already makes CyberAlchemy an open method, not a private commercial object, e.g. `arcanum/framework/CYBERALCHEMY-METHOD.md:5`. That is fine if the public claim is method/legibility. It becomes risky if the paid offer depends on "CyberAlchemy" as secret sauce rather than private control plane, runtime, attestation, and vertical deployment.

Second seam risk: generated Craft index schemas expose more than "ledger schema only." The index contract is still schema-level and says indexes are rebuildable, not authoritative, but it names generated `.craft/index.json` surfaces and lookup structures in `.agents/skills/craft/templates/schemas/index.schema.yml:6`. If the paid meter depends on index shape alone, it is leaking. If the paid meter is hosted signing, enforcement, role routing, auditor export, and cross-tool receipts, this is acceptable wedge.

Defensible paid moat: vendor-neutral attestation across mixed agents, IDEs, SCMs, CI, and humans; enforcement chokepoint plus signed receipts; human-owned intent review as the control object; regulated vertical templates only after evidence exists.

Commodity risks: validators, audits, spec templates, local YAML ledgers, prompt discipline, and observability dashboards are weak paid moats. Generic "AI governance mapping" is especially weak unless DomainSpec is anchored to the SDLC change artifact itself: who/what changed code, what intent governed it, what tests derived from that intent, who approved, and what immutable evidence survives.

Do not overstate: do not claim SOC 2, ITGC, or SOX legally requires AI-code attestation; do not claim EU AI Act applies to all coding agents; do not claim ISO 42001 proves a product is safe; do not claim OpenTelemetry GenAI or observability vendors solve auditor-grade attestation; do not claim the tools reduce escaped intent bugs or review burden until measured.
