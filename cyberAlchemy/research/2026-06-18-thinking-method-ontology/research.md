Status: collected research dispatch receipts. This file is evidence and synthesis input, not a canonical ontology mutation.

## Goal

Create a research strategy for formalizing CyberAlchemy methods of thinking into a taxonomy and schema-first ontology. The target vocabulary includes define, research, design, plan, discipline, constitution, axiom, confidence, ergonomics, governance, distilling, and adjacent method primitives worth mapping.

The run used `cyberAlchemy/` as the active workspace substrate and `research/cyber-alchemy-space/` plus `projects/ontologize/` as pressure surfaces.

## Dispatch Shape

Explorers:

- Peirce: abductive and semiotic method mining.
- Ashby: cybernetic control and feedback mining.
- Simon: bounded-rational action architecture mining.
- Alexander: pattern-language and ergonomic morphology mining.

Synthesis:

- Hofstadter: compact family map and schema-first primitive set.

Review:

- Lakatos: precedent gate.
- Meadows: non-vacuity gate.
- Wittgenstein: definitional-soundness gate.

Resulting stance:

- The method ontology should not invent a large new vocabulary.
- Most useful entries are already owned CyberAlchemy or Arcanum primitives.
- The new work is to create a reuse map, schema-backed witnesses, and promotion gates.
- Proposed labels should not guide agents unless they have an ontology entry, schema, register, fixture, card, or explicit source witness.

## Research Conclusion For Findings

The final findings should use the strictest combined position:

- Use Meadows' non-vacuity rule as the authority gate.
- Use Wittgenstein's collapse map to avoid vocabulary inflation.
- Use Lakatos' precedent gate to mark most work as build-from-owned.
- Treat Ontologize as the schema/product pressure surface for B3 run context, B4 bridge evidence, branch materialization, DAG edge rules, optimization chains, and card-level proof.
- Treat CyberAlchemy as the method primitive and promotion-governance surface.
- Treat Observed Invocation Loop and Context Builder as owned runtime witnesses, not new ontology ideas.
