How `goal` Decides When To Continue Or Stop

`goal` is a fail-closed router for a Craft-backed work graph. Its job is to keep moving only when the scope, risk, route, receipt, audit, staged delta, and approval evidence are clear. When evidence or authority is unclear, it stops instead of guessing.

What it is

A control loop that reads the current frontier, classifies risk, routes eligible work to an owner, and stages proposed ledger changes.

How it decides

It asks a sequence of gate questions: is the source bound, is risk allowed, is the route valid, is the receipt terminal, did audit pass, and is approval exact?

When it stops

It stops on protected risk, unknown authority, missing approval, audit veto, missing closeout, direct ledger mutation, budget ceiling, or promotion without evidence.

Decision Switchboard

This table turns the graph into plain questions. It is the fastest way to understand how `goal` chooses PASS, STOP, BLOCK, or a next route.

Question Continue when... Stop or block when... Next route
Is the goal bound to a Craft scope and source ledger? Yes: read the frontier. No: BLOCK with source-authority. decision-gate
Is each frontier node safely classified? Allowed or read-only work can route. Unknown or protected work becomes T3 and STOPs with t3-node. operator-approval
Does the route name owner, technique, receipt, gate, and fallback? Dispatch through the owning capability. Invalid route blocks before execution. dispatch-spec
Did every delegated lane return a terminal receipt? Audit the receipt evidence. Open, missing, or unjoined lane blocks. task-session or reroute
Did audit accept the evidence? Stage a proposed delta. Audit veto blocks, even if work looks done. audit-gate
Would the change mutate active ledger state? Stage a delta and framed diff only. Direct active ledger mutation blocks. craft after approval
Is there an exact approval token and durable decision record? Ready for Craft apply. Missing token is held; ambient approval or mismatch blocks. decision-gate
Is the frontier empty and gap discovery enabled? Run bounded gap discovery and queue proposals. Stop at duplicate loop or budget ceiling. next-slice proposal queue

Stop Wall

A stop is not always bad. In `goal`, a stop often means the control loop protected the ledger, the human approval boundary, or the source-authority boundary.

source-authority

Goal cannot identify the Craft scope, source ledger, or selected context. It blocks before any round work.

t3-node

A node is protected or unknown risk. Neutral defaults treat unknown work as protected.

route-validation

The dispatch route does not name enough owner, technique, receipt, gate, or fallback evidence.

receipt-closeout

A delegated lane did not return terminal evidence: closed, blocked, timed out with reroute, or handed off.

audit-veto

Review evidence vetoes apparent progress. Audit can override the urge to close.

approval-required

A staged batch exists, but no exact approval token and durable decision record exist yet.

ambient-approval

General approval is not enough. The token must match the batch and carry a decision record.

budget-ceiling

The proportionality guard stops before exceeding configured turns, spawns, tokens, or no-progress budget.

promotion-readiness

Reusable promotion blocks without Experiment Harness evidence. Telemetry alone is not promotion.

Dependency Boundary

Goal decides the control path, but other capabilities own major parts of the work. This boundary is what prevents the router from pretending to be every tool.

Inside `goal`

  • Bind goal and source authority.
  • Read and freeze a frontier snapshot.
  • Classify risk fail-closed.
  • Assemble and validate route shape.
  • Join receipts and run audit gates.
  • Stage deltas as proposals.
  • Emit result and telemetry fields.

Outside `goal`

  • arcana/craft owns ledger reads and approved applies.
  • dispatch-spec validates owner, technique, and receipt routes.
  • task-session owns bounded execution evidence.
  • decision-gate records approval or blocker decisions.
  • observability records runtime signals without promotion.

Round Lifecycle

A goal round is not "keep working forever." It is a bounded pass through gates. Gap discovery runs only after the active frontier is empty and the caller opts in.

  1. Bind
  2. Read
  3. Classify
  4. Route
  5. Audit
  6. Stage / approve
Normal progress

Read-only or allowed work routes to an owner and returns terminal evidence. Audit passes, then a staged delta can be proposed.

Batch ready

Goal stops with a framed batch diff and waits. Promotion requires an exact approval token and decision record.

Frontier empty

If enabled, bounded gap discovery mines residue, dedupes proposals, and stops at the round budget or duplicate loop.

Reader Terms

Reader terms are explanatory, not new canonical definitions. Each term says why it matters and what misuse to avoid.

Fail-closed

When unsure, stop or block instead of guessing permission.

Why it matters: unknown risk, unclear authority, missing receipts, and missing approvals cannot slip through.

Frontier snapshot

The frozen set of open nodes for one round.

Why it matters: decisions are made against a bounded context, not a vague repo-wide feeling.

Risk tier

A safety label for whether a node can route or must stop.

Why it matters: unknown defaults to T3, so the system does not improvise protected work.

Staged delta

A proposed ledger change, not an applied active row.

Why it matters: work can be reviewed before it changes the source ledger.
