# CyberAlchemy Plug-in Platform Product-Thesis Research Strategy

This is a non-authority Inventory read model of a confirmation-ready research
strategy. It does not establish a canonical plug-in definition, adopt a
product thesis, admit an ontology or Product Capability, implement IDE or
Workspace behavior, alter the active ARE/DSH worktree, or authorize
publication, release, deployment, or production.

## Design Effect

The thesis under test is narrower than “everything is a plug-in.” It asks
whether CyberAlchemy can be both an owner-preserving guest plug-in in many
hosts and a typed host for plug-ins such as Product Capabilities and harnesses,
while keeping adapter, projection, ownership, authority, admission, and
execution distinct. The IDE hypothesis is that a recognized definition or
ontology-path event produces a typed, source-linked candidate projection in
Workspace—not silent admission or mutation.
