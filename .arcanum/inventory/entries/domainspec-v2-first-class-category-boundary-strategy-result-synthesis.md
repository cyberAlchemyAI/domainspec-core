## Result

The auditor accepted `findings.md`. The central result is:

> A DomainSpec relation becomes first-class only when it is a stable domain
> distinction that changes root-owned validation, composition, derivation,
> projection, or owner decisions; broad recurrence is evidence for review, not
> proof of authority.

The dispatch classifies the overall rubric as **build-from-owned**, not novel.
It consolidates existing DomainSpec precedent around root authority,
edge-law admissibility, executable typed graphs, spec-as-ontology boundaries,
category-theory rationale, and domain-language practice.

## Accepted R3 Consequences

- The Entity-to-Entity semantic relation family survives as first-class R3
  work, but individual verbs still need examples, non-examples, endpoint law,
  inverse policy, and validation consequences.
- `owns` and `belongs-to` must not be collapsed naively. A generated inverse of
  `owns` should likely be `owned-by`; `belongs-to` may be a distinct
  membership, classification, or home-assignment relation if witnessed.
- `depends-on` is killed for current promotion and deferred until a concrete
  witness proves different reject/allow behavior from `references` or a
  field-level dependency.
- Saga signatures remain conditional: promote only if cross-feature,
  compensation, participant, temporal-safety, or residue obligations are not
  reducible to Workflow plus metadata.
- `produces` / `emits` survives as an endpoint-signature split:
  `Operation --[produces]--> Event` and `Entity --[emits]--> Event`.
- `Source --[verb]--> Target` is useful notation, not authority. Authority is
  the finite root-owned signature and its obligations.
- Category-theory language should be phrased as "typed composable graph
  fragment" in R3 unless DomainSpec formalizes the full category laws.
