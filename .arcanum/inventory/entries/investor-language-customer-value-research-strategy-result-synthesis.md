## Accepted Findings

### Commercial framing

The clearest bounded experiment is:

> We are testing whether engineering leaders will pay for an external,
> fixed-scope review of one consequential software release.

The candidate offer is a **fixed-scope release review sprint**. The deliverable
is a **versioned release review** for one identified release, boundary, evidence
cutoff, and customer-authorized release owner.

This is a coherent service specification. It is not a validated wedge,
differentiated product, category, moat, or source of demonstrated customer
value.

### Role boundary

- CyberAlchemy assembles, source-traces, classifies, and flags.
- Customer specialists confirm applicable requirements and interpret
  specialist results.
- The customer-authorized release owner approves the deployment boundary,
  accepts or rejects exceptions, and assigns response actors.
- Customer authority never moves to CyberAlchemy.

### Customer-value proof

Candidate value mechanisms are lower review or reconstruction labor, faster
open-item resolution, fewer material decision revisions, less deployment
rework, less later reconstruction, or a warranted deployment-boundary change.
Every mechanism remains a hypothesis until a paid pilot compares it with a
competent internal process and discloses total customer plus CyberAlchemy
burden.

The monetary taxonomy is closed:

- `CUSTOMER-BASELINE`
- `MEASURED-PILOT`
- `EXTERNAL-BENCHMARK`
- `HYPOTHESIS`
- `PROHIBITED`

### Commercial falsification

The accepted proposed test protocol is:

1. Freeze prospect, buyer-authority, release, access, price, and
   disqualification criteria.
2. Present the same offer to ten qualifying prospects.
3. Commercial-entry pass: at least two sign and pay.
4. Complete three paid pilots across at least two customers.
5. Customer-value pass: at least two preregistered operational improvements
   without unacceptable burden or a material missed in-scope requirement.
6. Continuation pass: at least one non-promotional repurchase.

These thresholds are test design, not market facts.

### Three scenario result

- **Technology-company release review:** `GO TO TEST`; coherent and measurable,
  commercially unwitnessed.
- **Live-premises operating window:** `DILIGENCE HOLD`; incumbent sufficiency,
  payor, access, and measured value are unresolved.
- **Confidential professional workflow:** `DILIGENCE HOLD`; authority,
  confidentiality, custody, retention, procurement, and measured value are
  unresolved.

The two transfer hypotheses remain fully researched in diligence, but the
primary investor narrative should not present them as customer markets or
evidence of reach.

### Language result

Investor-facing language should use `fixed-scope release review sprint`,
`versioned release review`, `customer-confirmed requirements`,
`customer-authorized release owner`, and `observable stop or rollback
triggers`.

Terms such as decision-readiness, receipt, derived obligations, supported
obligations, residual-risk ownership, reusable residue, substrate,
transportability, promotion/demotion, and fail-closed require replacement or
demotion to technical diligence.

## Rewrite Route

The approved findings govern a later mutation step for the investor package.
That step should:

1. lead with one buyer hypothesis, one release event, one current alternative,
   one fixed-scope service, one versioned deliverable, and missing validation;
2. keep the technology release workflow in primary copy;
3. move full live-premises and confidential-workflow hypotheses to diligence;
4. add the two commercial gates and empty transaction-witness schema;
5. place customer economics beside seller economics;
6. move DomainSpec/CAV2/Arcanum names behind the understandable transaction;
7. rename the financing section and state that no completed ask exists;
8. preserve the forbidden-claim register.
