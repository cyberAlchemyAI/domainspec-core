# AI Assurance / Certification Vocabulary Research Findings

Status: research substrate for business-plan construction. This is not final
investor prose. This is vocabulary and evidence mapping only, not legal advice,
certification advice, compliance advice, or an external assurance opinion.

## 1. Executive Result

The strongest safe vocabulary is **governed AI work readiness package**.

The repository can support a bounded story: DomainSpec/CyberAlchemy can help
turn AI-assisted work into source-linked, owner-routed, gate-checked evidence
packages. The current evidence supports readiness language, receipt discipline,
scoped deterministic validation, claim-boundary governance, and private verifier
specimens.

It does **not** support certification, conformity assessment, ISO/IEC 42001
compliance, EU AI Act compliance, third-party audit readiness, formal
correctness of the platform, or a completed assurance-case product.

The best scoped deliverable phrase is **receipt-backed assurance case**, but
only as an intended package shape: a structured claim/evidence/non-claim bundle
assembled for a bounded workflow. The reviewer gate is decisive: **no complete
end-to-end receipt-backed assurance case specimen exists yet**. Current evidence
is a set of strong components, not a fully witnessed assurance-case pipeline.

Investor-safe one-liner:

> We produce governed AI work readiness packages: source-linked evidence,
> owner-routed decisions, readiness gates, and receipt-backed assurance-case
> materials for bounded AI workflows.

## 3. External Vocabulary Sources

Use these as vocabulary pressure and alignment references only:

- [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework)
  - NIST describes the AI RMF as voluntary risk management for improving
    trustworthiness considerations in AI design, development, use, and
    evaluation.
- [ISO/IEC 42001](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001)
  - ISO/IEC 42001 specifies requirements for establishing, implementing,
    maintaining, and improving an AI management system.
- [EU AI Act overview](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)
  - The EU AI Act uses a risk-based framework and describes high-risk
    obligations such as risk mitigation, data quality, logging, documentation,
    human oversight, robustness, cybersecurity, and accuracy.
- [AI Act Article 43: Conformity assessment](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-43)
  - Article 43 confirms that conformity assessment is a formal procedure for
    high-risk AI systems, with internal-control and notified-body routes in
    named cases. This is why `conformity assessed` is not BP-safe language here.

## 5. Core Thesis

DomainSpec/CyberAlchemy should be framed as a **governed AI work readiness
package**: a way to organize AI-assisted work into reviewable evidence, explicit
ownership, gated decisions, and bounded claims.

The compounding effect across POCs is not that the portfolio is already
certified or fully assured. The compounding effect is that each POC leaves
controlled residue: receipts, gate results, owner decisions, trace links,
telemetry rows, readiness verdicts, formal-methods residue, or verifier outputs.
Those residues can become the material for later assurance cases.

## 9. Mini Glossary

**Receipt:** A traceable record of evidence, route, gate, owner decision,
validation result, telemetry event, or verifier output. A receipt records what
happened; it does not prove truth.

**Assurance case:** A structured claim-evidence-warrant package for a bounded
claim. In this repository, `receipt-backed assurance case` is a scoped
deliverable target, not a current certification claim.

**Proof package:** Internal term for bundled receipts, gates, decisions, traces,
bounded formal artifacts, and verifier outputs. Public use requires explicit
scope and non-claims.

**Attestation:** Scoped integrity/verifier evidence. Current evidence supports
L1 valid/tampered discrimination, not external signature, public verifier
status, truth, or third-party approval.

**Audit-readiness:** Evidence organized for inspection or audit preparation. Not
auditor acceptance and not third-party audit readiness.

**Readiness:** Bounded preparedness against named gates and scope. Not
production correctness, legal compliance, or deployment approval.

**Certification:** Formal determination by an authorized external process or
body. Not currently claimable.

**Conformity:** Satisfaction of a specified standard or regulatory requirement.
Avoid unless the exact requirement, evidence, and assessment route are named.

**Claim <= proof:** Every claim must be no broader than its evidence class,
scope, verifier strength, and known non-claims.

## 10. Missing End-To-End Witness

The major gap is the absence of one complete assurance case specimen.

Current state: individual witnesses exist. The repo has governance discipline,
deterministic validation specimens, formal-methods residue, private L1
attestation, and receipt/gate vocabulary.

Missing state: one bounded workflow that connects those pieces end to end.

Required next specimen:

1. Choose one governed artifact, preferably a DomainSpec v2 rule or validation
   fixture.
2. Derive deterministic obligations/tests.
3. Run the test and capture pass/fail output.
4. Attach CAV2 `claim <= proof` classification.
5. Record an owner decision.
6. Emit a goal/Craft gate receipt.
7. Seal the artifact/result bundle with the L1 attestation specimen.
8. Include positive and negative mutation verification.
9. Add a NIST-informed standards vocabulary note.
10. Add a "what this does not prove" page.

Only after that specimen exists should the project claim a completed
**receipt-backed assurance case specimen**.
