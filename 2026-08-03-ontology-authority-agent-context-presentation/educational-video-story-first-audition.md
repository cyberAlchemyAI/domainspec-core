# Story-First Language Audition

This is the smallest representative sample of the proposed revision. It contains exactly three audience-facing moments. It does not replace the current approved text package.

## Moment 1 — Opening

### On-screen copy

`ARE WE ACTUALLY ALLOWED TO GO AHEAD?`

### Spoken copy

A team is about to announce a new community program.

The partners are ready. The message is scheduled. People have already begun arranging their work around it.

Then, just before the announcement, someone asks a simple question:

“Are we actually allowed to go ahead?”

So the agent searches. Meeting notes. Decision logs. Old conversations. Project records.

And there it is: an official record from a genuine source, carrying one very reassuring word.

Approved.

## Moment 2 — Tension

### On-screen copy

`PERMISSION TODAY — OR EVIDENCE FROM THE PAST?`

### Spoken copy

For a moment, this feels like success. The agent found the answer.

But look a little closer.

The approval is real. It was not fabricated. It also belongs to an earlier moment. Its validity period has ended. A later review may supersede it. And nothing in the evidence we selected establishes what the current owner has decided.

The record has not become false. But the action it once supported may no longer be justified.

So what did the agent actually find?

Permission for today—or evidence from the past?

## Moment 3 — Reveal

### On-screen copy

`ONTOLOGIES ARE KNOWLEDGE SPACES`

### Spoken copy

This is the gap an ontology helps us see.

The agent remembered the information, but not enough of the world around it.

An ontology puts that world back.

Ontologies are knowledge spaces. They are bounded world models that describe not only types, classes, and definitions, but how concepts interact; when a relationship is meant to hold; how strongly the evidence supports it; and who has the authority to change the governed state.

The ontology does not vote yes or no. It makes the missing premises visible.

And now the agent can give a better answer:

“I found a genuine earlier approval. I cannot establish that it is current. I should ask the responsible owner—or stop.”

## Audition gate

The full rewrite remains blocked until the operator decides whether this voice and movement should become the new narrative spine.

Review questions:

1. Does the opening feel like a story rather than a lecture?
2. Does `Approved` feel genuinely reassuring before the doubt appears?
3. Does the ontology definition feel earned when it arrives?
4. Can the spoken copy be read naturally without performing a keynote voice?
5. Does the reveal preserve the distinction between understanding and authority?

Gate values: `approve`, `revise`, or `reject`.
