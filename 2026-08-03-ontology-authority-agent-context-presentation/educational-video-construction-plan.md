## The change in medium

The presentation is organized around moments a speaker advances. The video must be organized around visible change: every important spoken claim causes something in the image to appear, move, connect, expire, stop, or become unresolved.

This derivative therefore has no slides, presenter notes, audience prompts, or click-to-reveal states. It has continuous voiceover, shots, motion cues, edit points, restrained on-screen language, captions, and sound.

## Distill result

- **Target context:** a twelve-to-thirteen-minute educational video that can be understood without a live presenter.
- **Objective and output artifact:** transform the approved podcast narrative into a production-ready voice-and-picture script using the existing eight illustrations as visual worlds.
- **Mode and budget:** Standard by assumption; one proposal track, inline Proposer/Balancer simulation, two recursive rounds, one reconciliation.
- **Proposal tracks:** one story-led explanatory-film track.
- **Recursive rounds:** two of two.
- **Verdict:** pass for a three-scene transport audition; full script remains gated by video-rhythm approval.
- **Current smallest coherent unit:** a **meaning-change beat**—one spoken claim, one corresponding visible transformation, one held consequence, and one transition into the next question.
- **Optimization point:** a spoken sentence alone is audio; a moving image alone is illustration. The meaning-change beat is the smallest unit in which voice and image explain one another.

## Role conversation trace

### Round 1

- **Proposer claim:** preserve the single earlier-approval story and replace twelve slides with one continuous chain of visual transformations.
- **Evidence or assumption:** the approved voice already has a stable narrative anchor and all eight illustrations belong to the same visual grammar.
- **Balancer objection — medium mismatch:** slow zooms over still images could reproduce a slideshow with narration.
- **Reconciliation:** every shot must have a semantic reason to change. Use masks, crop movement, parallax, edge drawing, state replacement, and match cuts; never hold a completely unchanged image beneath a new idea.

### Round 2

- **Proposer claim:** synchronize every phrase to a visual action for production precision.
- **Evidence or assumption:** the user wants the voice and images to follow what is being said.
- **Balancer objection — cognitive-load risk:** literal motion on every word would compete with comprehension and feel mechanical.
- **Reconciliation:** animate only the semantic anchor of each sentence. After the change, hold the frame long enough for the consequence to register. One primary motion per beat; secondary ambience remains subtle.

## Technique trace

- **Abstraction-level guard:** pass — changes transport grammar, not ontology meaning or authority.
- **Recomposition proof:** pass — meaning-change beats compose into scenes; scenes compose into the approval investigation; the investigation composes into the complete educational film.
- **Evolution profile:** pass — the shot table can later feed an editor, motion designer, Remotion timeline, caption file, or voice generator without changing the narration contract.
- **Cognitive-load check:** pass with guardrail — one visual change per claim; on-screen text names only anchors, never duplicates paragraphs.
- **Requisite-variety check:** pass — narration carries the human story, diagrams carry structure, typography carries only decision words and contrasts.
- **Boundary-object check:** pass — the earlier approval record remains visually stable across memory, time, authority, context, and Augmentor scenes.
- **Concept-vs-knowledge status:** pass — motion choices are editorial proposals; evidence ceilings remain unchanged.
- **Premortem:** likely failure is “a slideshow that happens to have a voice.” Guardrail: if removing the motion does not weaken the explanation, the shot has not earned its place.
- **Frame-expiry note:** this plan expires if the target becomes a talking-head video, two-host conversation, vertical short, or under-eight-minute cut.
- **Navigation result:** review the three-scene audition; approve or correct the video rhythm; then generate the complete production script and caption derivative.

## Video surface contract

| Surface | Responsibility | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Voiceover | Carries the investigation and emotional continuity | One reflective narrator; natural paragraphs; no slide language |
| Primary picture | Shows the world changing because of the narrated claim | One semantic motion per meaning-change beat |
| On-screen text | Names anchors the eye must retain | Prefer 1–5 words; never display the full voiceover |
| Captions | Provide complete accessible transcription | Separate caption derivative; verbatim voiceover |
| Sound | Marks discovery, tension, gates, and resolution | Quiet and structural; never simulate alarm or certainty |
| Edit notes | Specify cuts, holds, masks, parallax, and transitions | Production-facing only |
| Claim boundary | Prevents visual implication beyond evidence | Owner gate never opens automatically; candidates never self-promote |

## Visual grammar

- Treat each existing illustration as a **world**, not a slide.
- Build shots from close crops, wide reveals, foreground/background separation, edge traces, and object isolation.
- Use source cards as the recurring match-cut object between scenes.
- Use cyan motion for typed meaning, amber for unresolved candidate state, and violet for owner authority.
- Let green confirm only the genuineness of the earlier record—not current permission.
- When authority is missing, illuminate the absent connection rather than showing a generic error screen.
- Never animate a candidate crossing the violet owner gate.

## On-screen language policy

- Use full-screen text only twice: `Ontologies are knowledge spaces` and the final sentence.
- Use short anchors elsewhere: `APPROVED`, `valid then`, `current owner?`, `available ≠ promoted`, `authority effect: none`.
- Do not project repository paths, ontology IDs, hashes, validation counts, or source selectors.
- Burned-in subtitles are optional; the production script should generate a separate caption file by default.
