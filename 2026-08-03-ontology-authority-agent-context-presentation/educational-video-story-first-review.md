# Story-First Editorial Review

## Verdict

The current script is semantically careful and production-ready as a text package, but its opening explains the subject before the audience has a reason to care. The best revision is **case-first, delayed-definition**: begin with the genuine old approval, let the audience experience the apparent success and hidden gap, then reveal the ontology as the model that restores the missing world around the record.

This pass approves that direction for a language audition. It does not replace the current voiceover, captions, timing, production script, or prior approvals.

## What already works

- The old approval is genuine rather than a fabricated trap.
- Time, confidence, governed status, and authority remain distinct.
- The ontology exposes missing premises without deciding permission.
- Memory, context selection, ontology meaning, owner authority, and runtime action remain separate roles.
- The Augmentor boundary remains explicit: `authority_effect: none`.
- The closing line, “the map does not grant permission,” is memorable and exact.
- The eight illustrations already support semantic motion rather than slide narration.

## Text-intent substrate

### Resonance core

- Voice: intimate, intelligent, reflective, and conversational.
- Felt movement: reassurance → doubt → understanding → responsible restraint.
- Desired residue: a true memory can still be unsafe when the world around it is missing.
- Forbidden feel: lecture opening, corporate keynote, product advertisement, ominous cautionary tale, or schema recitation.

### Relevance core

- Audience: mixed business, product, technical, and agent-system listeners who may not know ontology terminology.
- Starting state: they understand search and memory as useful capabilities.
- Needed shift: retrieval is not interpretation, and interpretation is not authority.
- Authority mode: source-bounded explanation; no runtime, promotion, deployment, or production claim.

### Trajectory core

1. A team needs a current decision.
2. The agent finds a genuine approval.
3. The answer appears complete.
4. Time, possible supersession, and current ownership reveal the gap.
5. The ontology is named as the knowledge space that models the missing world.
6. Existing ontology examples show that relations, confidence, and governed status answer different questions.
7. Context selection carries only the right slice of that world.
8. Augmentor brings a candidate to the owner gate but cannot turn it into permission.
9. The agent returns a better answer and stops at the correct boundary.

## Distill role trace

### Round 1

- **Proposer claim:** move the approval story to the first frame and make it the single narrative anchor.
- **Evidence/assumption:** the current story already contains the film's clearest stakes, emotional turn, and authority distinction.
- **Balancer objection — source-fidelity risk:** a dramatic cold open could imply that `APPROVED` is false or that the agent made an unauthorized decision.
- **Reconciliation:** let `APPROVED` feel genuinely reassuring; preserve its provenance and reveal only that its current applicability is not established.

### Round 2

- **Proposer claim:** delay “Ontologies are knowledge spaces” until the audience can see what the retrieved record is missing.
- **Evidence/assumption:** formal vocabulary becomes memorable when it names a problem the audience has already encountered.
- **Balancer objection — orientation risk:** delaying the subject too long could make the film feel like a generic AI-governance story.
- **Reconciliation:** reveal the definition within roughly the first two minutes and let the title/visual world provide light orientation before that point.

## Smallest coherent unit

**A genuine approval can be retrieved correctly and still be unsafe to use when its time, relationships, and current authority are missing.**

This is the smallest unit that carries the film's felt meaning and technical responsibility. Split further, and either the emotional turn or the authority distinction disappears.

## Recomposition proof

The one approval record can carry the complete model:

- its kind introduces type;
- its application to a program introduces scope and relation;
- its source introduces provenance;
- its ended validity introduces time;
- its supporting evidence introduces confidence;
- the later review introduces lifecycle and possible supersession;
- the current decision-maker introduces ownership and authority;
- the selected packet introduces bounded context;
- the stopped request introduces the owner gate;
- Augmentor introduces a bridge that preserves this path without becoming authority.

No second narrative anchor is required. Body War remains a short transfer example proving that confidence and promotion are separate outside the approval story.
