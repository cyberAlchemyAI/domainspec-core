# Paper Lane: Cognitive Tools, Representation, and Reflective Correctness

## Lane thesis

Cognitive navigability is a distinct correctness lens. A representation is not sufficient because it is formally coherent or executable; readers must also be able to move among its forms while preserving the claim's limits. Alternative views preserve one governed meaning horizontally. Reflection-tower escalation begins only when those views cannot name a required distinction. Comprehension is then tested as a residue-tracked round trip back to the concrete case.

This is an **architecture and evaluation proposal**. It is not a result about human cognition.

## Where the lane belongs

Insert the lane after the paper's derivation/check pipeline and before its general evidence method. It supplies a fourth correctness lens alongside structural, transactional/temporal, and experimental/adversarial checks:

1. **Structural correctness** — are the objects and relations well formed?
2. **Transactional or temporal correctness** — does the state transition preserve the intended invariant?
3. **Experimental or adversarial correctness** — does execution discriminate the claimed mechanism from simpler alternatives?
4. **Cognitive navigability** — can a reader traverse representations, recover the concrete case, and preserve the claim ceiling?

## Proposed section argument

### 1. Horizontal representations

A cognitive tool may expose the same bounded claim as plain language, operator expression, executive summary, contract, causal-careful statement, dashboard view, or ontology entry. These are not increasing levels of truth. They are audience-specific surfaces whose invariant is the same claim label, limits, governance route, and approval boundary.

### 2. Vertical residue promotion

When a reader cannot resolve a mismatch by improving an existing view, the failure is typed as residue. Only missing-vocabulary residue justifies a richer explanatory layer. The new layer must preserve prior distinctions, name what was previously unavailable, and define how the result returns to the lower-level case.

### 3. Round-trip comprehension

The reader must ascend from a concrete case to the richer representation and descend again. The return is successful only when the reader can:

- restate the bounded claim;
- retain its interpretation and authority limits;
- apply it to the original and an unfamiliar case;
- distinguish demonstrated, inferred, proposed, and untested content;
- identify when the representation itself is insufficient.

### 4. Evidence boundary

The formal reflection tower supports the structure of faithful promotion in its formal domain. It does not establish a psychological mechanism or an educational benefit. The existing novice test supplies a useful baseline/treatment protocol shape, but its executed arm is an LLM structural pretest; a human arm is required for claims about human comprehension.

## Public-safe paragraph candidate

> We treat cognitive navigability as a separate correctness lens. A claim may be exposed through several audience-specific representations, but those views remain horizontal: each must preserve the same evidential and authority limits. A richer explanatory layer is introduced only when the current vocabulary cannot name an observed mismatch and local repair fails. This reflection-tower analogy organizes the instrument; it is not evidence about cognition. We therefore define comprehension operationally as a round trip: a reader must use the richer representation and then reconstruct the concrete case without losing distinctions or enlarging the claim. Whether the instrument improves comprehension is a falsifiable empirical question, not a consequence of the formal analogy.
