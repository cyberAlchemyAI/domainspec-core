## 1. Deep Per-Book Maps

### Book 1 — Map and Territory

**Core thesis.** `[local-inference, per lens-E1]` Beliefs are maps of reality; the only legitimate purpose of a belief is accurate correspondence to the territory, and a belief that does not "pay rent in anticipated experiences" is not a genuine belief but a social performance.

**Load-bearing claims.**
1. `[primary-source, per lens-E1]` Beliefs should pay rent in anticipated experiences — a belief that yields identical predictions whether true or false carries zero information and should be discarded.
2. `[primary-source, per lens-E2]` Evidence is defined as an event "entangled" via causal links with the target of inquiry; rational belief must be causally entangled with reality, else it is "blind faith." (E2 calls this the strongest, hardest-to-attack part of the corpus.)
3. `[primary-source, per lens-E1]` Three-way distinction: *believing* a proposition vs. *believing one ought to believe* it (belief in belief, after Dennett) vs. publicly *professing* it (tribal cheering, not an epistemic act).
4. `[primary-source, per lens-E1]` A real explanation must constrain anticipation; phrases that "explain" any outcome equally well are *semantic stopsigns*, not knowledge.
5. `[primary-source, per lens-E1]` Epistemic rationality = "systematically increasing map–territory correspondence"; instrumental rationality = "systematically finding a better path to goals."

**Key vocabulary.** Map/Territory `[open-residue, per lens-E1]` (title essay 404'd); beliefs-pay-rent; belief-in-belief; semantic stopsign; cheering/professing; anticipation-controllers; epistemic vs. instrumental rationality.

**Where E2 qualifies it.**
- `[related-source, per lens-E2]` "Beliefs pay rent" = the verification principle of logical positivism (Ayer, Carnap), substantially abandoned as self-refuting and over-exclusionary (Quine). E2's verdict: *partial divergence* — Yudkowsky is more careful (says "useless," not "meaningless," allows inference to unobservables) but presents a patched re-tread of a defeated program as fresh.
- `[related-source, per lens-E2]` Correspondence theory is one live option among several (deflationary/Tarskian, coherence, pragmatist); the corpus presents a contested metaphysics as near-tautology. The *attitude* (face reality) is defensible; the *metaphysics* is asserted, not argued.
- `[related-source, per lens-E2]` Solomonoff induction is presented as *the* correct formalization of simplicity, but it is uncomputable and UTM-dependent (constant factor unbounded). E2 verdict: **overclaim of rigor.**

**Where E3's lineage qualifies it.**
- `[local-inference, per lens-E3]` Map/territory **predates Yudkowsky** — Korzybski, *Science and Sanity* (1931). The Sequences apply and popularize, do not coin it. Mis-attribution flag.
- `[primary-source, per lens-E3]` "Beliefs Pay Rent" belongs to the *Mysterious Answers* sequence (Jul–Aug 2007), often mis-attributed to "Fake Beliefs." "What Is Evidence?" is *Noticing Confusion* (Sep 22, 2007).

### Book 2 — How to Actually Change Your Mind

**Core thesis.** `[local-inference, per lens-E1]` Human cognition systematically writes a conclusion first and recruits supporting arguments afterward (rationalization); genuine belief revision requires interrupting this at the algorithmic level, not just rejecting individual conclusions.

**Load-bearing claims.**
1. `[primary-source, per lens-E1]` The *bottom line*: what matters is the algorithm that actually produced the conclusion, not the arguments built afterward; a pre-written conclusion is "evidence only of which owner paid the higher bid."
2. `[primary-source, per lens-E1]` Conservation of Expected Evidence: each anticipated piece of support must be balanced by an equal-and-opposite anticipated counterevidence; any strategy expecting a net confidence gain before seeing evidence violates Bayes.
3. `[primary-source, per lens-E1]` Rationalization ≠ reasoning: rationalization selects evidence post-hoc; its ink is entangled only with the desired conclusion, not with reality.
4. `[primary-source, per lens-E1]` *True rejection*: the stated objection is often not the real cause of disagreement (esp. tribal/political).
5. `[local-inference, per lens-E1]` Motivated stopping and motivated continuation are symmetric failures.

**Key vocabulary.** The bottom line; rationalization; Conservation of Expected Evidence; affective death spiral `[open-residue, per lens-E1]`; true rejection; motivated stopping/continuation; applause lights `[primary-source, per lens-E1]`.

**Where E2 qualifies it.**
- `[related-source, per lens-E2]` Core Kahneman–Tversky biases (availability, representativeness, anchoring, conjunction fallacy) genuinely replicate — "this part the literature backs."
- `[related-source, per lens-E2]` BUT the corpus (~2006–09) predates the replication crisis; adjacent social-priming / ego-depletion results the milieu absorbed have failed to replicate (Hagger multi-lab ego-depletion RRR). Verdict: **time-stamped overconfidence** — a reader treating the corpus as a current cognitive-science map would over-trust retracted effects.
- `[related-source, per lens-E2]` One-sided uptake: Gigerenzer's ecological-rationality counter-program ("Homo Heuristicus," natural frequencies) gets little airtime; "biases as defects" is a contestable framing sold as consensus. Practical advice (notice rationalization, seek disconfirmation) survives either way.
- `[related-source, per lens-E2]` Bayes-as-uniquely-correct overclaims uniqueness; frequentism has a coherent error-statistical philosophy (Mayo); problem-of-priors, logical omniscience, old-evidence remain open. `[open-residue, per lens-E2]` canonical Bayes essay URL misresolved; reconstructed from sequence framing.

**Where E3's lineage qualifies it.**
- `[primary-source, per lens-E3]` Affective Death Spirals = *Death Spirals* sequence (Dec 2, 2007), Book 2 — often mis-placed in Book 1 because precursors (halo effect, confirmation bias) live there. The fission metaphor originates here.

### Book 3 — The Machine in the Ghost

**Core thesis.** `[local-inference, per lens-E1]` Minds are optimization processes shaped by blind evolutionary history, not designer intent; this explains why human values feel natural-yet-arbitrary, and why extrapolating human psychology to other minds (or AI) is a catastrophic error.

**Load-bearing claims.**
1. `[primary-source, per lens-E1; corroborated per lens-E2]` Organisms are adaptation-executors, not fitness-maximizers (the phrase borrowed from Tooby & Cosmides per E2); evolved mechanisms execute without regard to current fitness (sugar craving).
2. `[primary-source, per lens-E2]` Evolution is a foresightless hill-climbing optimizer; population-genetics math (allele-frequency change) is the real content behind "adaptation."
3. `[open-residue, per lens-E1]` Evolution is an "alien god" — no human ethical concept maps onto it ("An Alien God" URL 404'd).
4. `[local-inference, per lens-E1]` "Godshatter": evolution's single optimization pressure shatters into many proximate human desires `[open-residue]`.
5. `[primary-source, per lens-E1; open-residue on owning essay]` Terminal vs. instrumental values distinction; evolution itself "had" no terminal values.

**Key vocabulary.** Adaptation-executor; fitness-maximizer; Godshatter `[open-residue]`; terminal vs. instrumental values `[open-residue on origin]`; Detached Lever Fallacy `[open-residue]`; superstimuli `[local-inference, per lens-E1]`.

**Where E2 qualifies it.**
- `[related-source, per lens-E2]` Foresightless evolution and adaptation-executors are **strongly backed** (Williams, Fisher/Price, Dawkins, Tooby & Cosmides).
- `[local-inference, per lens-E2]` **Reductive overreach in framing.** "Optimization process" as a unifying abstraction across evolution/markets/minds is an *analogy, not a theorem*; the corpus's "evolution optimizes inclusive fitness, full stop" elides levels-of-selection and spandrel/exaptation debates (Sober & Wilson, Gould–Dawkins).
- `[local-inference, per lens-E2]` **Internal tension:** the "mind as expected-utility maximizer" abstraction (load-bearing for later AI material) is contradicted by Book 2's own biases literature.

**Where E3's lineage qualifies it.**
- `[primary-source, per lens-E3]` "How an Algorithm Feels From Inside" (Feb 11, 2008) belongs here (*A Human's Guide to Words*), often mis-placed in Book 4. It is the mechanism behind why cognitive processes mislead us about their own nature.
- `[open-residue, per lens-E3]` Terminal/Instrumental original essay 404'd on 4 attempts; likely first home is *Fragile Purposes* (Book 3) or *Value Theory* (Book 5) — owning book unconfirmed.

### Book 4 — Mere Reality

**Core thesis.** `[local-inference, per lens-E1]` Physical reality is lawful, fully reductionist, and already largely explained; accepting this without treating it as ugly or threatening is both epistemically required and aesthetically rewarding ("Joy in the Merely Real").

**Load-bearing claims.**
1. `[local-inference, per lens-E1; open-residue]` Reductionism is presented as the established track record of science, not a mere philosophical thesis.
2. `[primary-source, per lens-E1; open-residue on dedicated essay]` Mind Projection Fallacy: reading map-properties (probability, mystery) into the territory.
3. `[primary-source, per lens-E1]` Dissolving the question / "taboo your words": many philosophical problems are confused framings dissolved by restating in lower-level empirical terms.
4. `[primary-source, per lens-E2]` Many-Worlds Interpretation "wins outright"; collapse is "phlogiston-like"; any single-world theory "must violate Special Relativity."

**Key vocabulary.** Mind Projection Fallacy `[open-residue]`; dissolving the question `[primary-source]`; taboo your words `[primary-source]`; reductionism `[open-residue]`; explaining vs. explaining away `[open-residue]`; decoherence/Many-Worlds `[open-residue]`.

**Where E2 qualifies it (this is the corpus's most-attacked book).**
- `[related-source, per lens-E2]` **MWI is the single biggest overclaim.** Physicists are not near consensus — a 2025 survey (~1,100 respondents): Copenhagen 36%, information-based ~17%, **MWI ~15%**, Bohm ~7%; only 24% confident. MWI is a respected *minority* view.
- `[related-source, per lens-E2]` The "collapse violates SR" claim is too strong — standard QM is non-signaling; relativistic collapse models are active research (GRW, Tumulka).
- `[local-inference, per lens-E2]` **Internal contradiction:** Yudkowsky champions MWI on simplicity grounds *precisely because it is empirically equivalent* to collapse — i.e. it "pays no differential rent," violating his own Book 1 criterion. (E2's strongest single cross-book attack — see §1 Book 1 and cross-cutting note.)
- `[related-source, per lens-E2]` "Dissolving" the hard problem of consciousness (Chalmers, Jackson's Mary) is a *stance, not a refutation*; calling a hard problem "confused" does not dispatch it.

**Where E3's lineage qualifies it.**
- `[primary-source, per lens-E3]` Reductionism is *named and defended* here (*Reductionism 101*, ~2008) but was *implicit* in Book 1 and *applied* in Book 3; progression runs methodological → ontological.

### Book 5 — Mere Goodness

**Core thesis.** `[local-inference, per lens-E1]` Human values are neither arbitrary nor infinitely flexible; they are evolutionary products (hence fragile), but they are the *actual* values that matter — any system that overrides them (fake preferences, proxy-optimizing AI) destroys what it claims to protect.

**Load-bearing claims.**
1. `[local-inference, per lens-E1; open-residue]` "Value is fragile": the space of possible value systems is vast; the human-endorsed region is tiny; a slightly-off optimizer yields catastrophe.
2. `[primary-source, per lens-E2]` No Universally Compelling Arguments: a paperclip maximizer is unmoved by human moral arguments; "morality" names a specific human computation with relative truth-values.
3. `[local-inference, per lens-E1; open-residue]` Fake preferences diverge from what a fully-informed agent would prefer; moral reasoning must distinguish them.
4. `[local-inference, per lens-E1; open-residue]` Morality as fixed computation — moral intuitions are compressed outputs of a complex human computation, treated as data, not noise.
5. `[open-residue, per lens-E1]` "Something to protect": a real stake grounds rationality as more than intellectual performance.

**Key vocabulary.** Value is fragile `[open-residue]`; fake preferences `[open-residue]`; no universally compelling arguments; morality as fixed computation `[open-residue]`; something to protect `[open-residue]`; Coherent Extrapolated Volition (CEV).

**Where E2 qualifies it.**
- `[related-source, per lens-E2]` "No universally compelling arguments" is **sound and modest** — basically Hume's no-ought-from-is + orthogonality of motivation. The recognizable position is moral functionalism (Jackson & Pettit); Yudkowsky is *not* a robust moral realist.
- `[local-inference, per lens-E2]` **Overclaim:** the *leap* is that human morality is a well-defined "fixed computation" with determinate truth-values. The metaethics literature (constructivism; Street's evolutionary debunking) suggests values may be plural, incoherent, partly indeterminate — "the computation" may not exist as a single object. The framework borrows the *confidence* of realism while keeping the *metaphysics* of anti-realism.
- `[related-source, per lens-E2]` Evolutionary debunking (Street) is **not engaged** and cuts both ways: if values are evolved computations, why trust their *extrapolation* (CEV) as authoritative? CEV-convergence is an unargued bet, not a result.

**Where E3's lineage qualifies it.**
- `[primary-source, per lens-E3]` "Value is Fragile" (Jan 29, 2009, *Value Theory*) is the verified discussion point for terminal/instrumental, but may not be the concept's first appearance (see Book 3 open-residue).

### Book 6 — Becoming Stronger

**Core thesis.** `[local-inference, per lens-E1]` Rationality is an art that must be deliberately practiced, socially instantiated in communities with honest error-correction, and grounded in precise vocabulary; its payoff is the precondition for humanity navigating hard problems (incl. aligned AI).

**Load-bearing claims.**
1. `[primary-source, per lens-E1]` Words are "mental paintbrush handles" — each invokes a cluster of associations; many disputes are over word definitions, not facts. Solution: taboo contested words.
2. `[primary-source, per lens-E1; open-residue on body]` "37 Ways That Words Can Be Wrong" catalogs categorization failure modes (sneaking connotations, arguing by definition).
3. `[primary-source, per lens-E1]` The Twelve Virtues of Rationality (curiosity, relinquishment, lightness, evenness, argument, empiricism, simplicity, humility, perfectionism, precision, scholarship, and "the void") describe rationalist *character*, not just technique.
4. `[local-inference, per lens-E1]` "Yudkowsky's Coming of Age" is a worked example — his own early errors show rationality is a skill even self-identified rationalists fail at.
5. `[local-inference, per lens-E1]` Communities of intelligent people can fail systematically — "cult attractor," epistemic viciousness.
6. `[primary-source, per lens-E1; open-residue on body]` Goal: "raise the sanity waterline."

**Key vocabulary.** Words as mental paintbrush handles `[open-residue]`; taboo your words `[primary-source]`; the Twelve Virtues `[primary-source]`; raising the sanity waterline `[open-residue]`; epistemic viciousness `[open-residue]`; cult attractor `[local-inference]`; the void `[primary-source]`.

**Where E2 qualifies it.**
- `[related-source, per lens-E2]` **Overclaim of trainability.** The debiasing/transfer literature is mixed-to-skeptical: interventions show some effect but poor cross-domain/time transfer (Lilienfeld; Kahneman & Klein require high-validity fast-feedback environments, which "general rationality" lacks). This is the corpus's *most testable and least-tested* promise — framed as exhortation/memoir.
- `[open-residue, per lens-E2]` CFAR-style outcome data exists but was not retrieved; empirical case unconfirmed.

**Where E3's lineage qualifies it.**
- `[local-inference, per lens-E3]` Community/cultishness material (*The Craft and the Community*) frames a community-level death spiral — the same Book 2 dynamic scaled up.

## 2. Cross-Book Spine — How the 6 Books Build

`[local-inference, per lens-E1, with lineage per lens-E3]`

1. **Book 1 sets the standard:** beliefs are maps; a map that does not track the territory is not merely wrong, it is not a belief — only social performance. Formal criterion: anticipation-control.
2. **Book 2 diagnoses the obstacle:** the human algorithm writes the bottom line first, then recruits arguments. A structural feature of motivated cognition, to be caught algorithmically.
3. **Book 3 grounds the obstacle in history:** minds are adaptation-executors from blind optimization; values/heuristics are ancestrally tuned, not truth-tracking, not coherent.
4. **Book 4 supplies the worldview the map must converge on:** physicalism, reductionism, Bayesian epistemology, dissolution of mind-projection pseudo-problems. Response: joy, not mysticism.
5. **Book 5 applies this to value:** human values are the fragile output of Book 3's machine; fake preferences, detached levers, and universally-compelling arguments are traps that destroy value while claiming to serve it.
6. **Book 6 closes the loop:** the art must be practiced, vocabulary precise, communities error-correcting, motivation grounded in something real to protect — else rationality becomes the epistemic viciousness it was meant to cure.

**Recurrence (per lens-E3 matrix):** Map/Territory (B1 → 3,4,5); Beliefs-Pay-Rent (B1 → 2,4,5); Bayesian Evidence (B1 → 2,4,5); Mysterious Answers (B1 → 3,4,5); Semantic Stopsigns (B1 → 2,3,5); Reductionism (implicit B1 → named B4 → applied B3,5). The epistemological tool of Book 1 is *sharpened by each new application domain.*

**Adversarial counter-spine `[local-inference, per lens-E2]`:** the same spine has a structural fault line — Book 1's verificationist criterion is *contradicted* by Book 4's MWI verdict (empirically-equivalent interpretation chosen on simplicity = pays no differential rent). The "optimization process" abstraction unifying Books 3/5 is analogy sold as theorem. Idealizations (Solomonoff uncomputable, Bayesian logical omniscience, EU-maximizer minds vs. Book 2's biases) are presented as the normative skeleton with their inapplicability downplayed.
