## Cross-Book Steelman Thesis

`[local-inference]` The six books together constitute a single extended argument: *the quality of a mind's map of reality — including maps of one's own cognition, of evolution's products, of physical law, of value, and of the social world — determines the quality of everything that mind can do or protect.* Improving that map requires (a) understanding exactly how and why human cognition systematically distorts it, (b) learning the correct formal standards (Bayesian epistemology, reductionism) for what a good map even is, (c) grasping that human values are the product of blind evolutionary history and are therefore fragile and easily corrupted, and (d) building communities and personal habits capable of sustained, honest self-correction. The project is simultaneously technical, psychological, ethical, and institutional.

## Book 1 — Map and Territory

### (a) Core Thesis

Beliefs are maps of reality; the only purpose of a belief is accurate correspondence to the territory, and any belief that does not "pay rent in anticipated experiences" is not a genuine belief but a social performance.

### (b) Load-Bearing Claims

1. `[primary-source]` "Beliefs should pay rent in anticipated experiences" — a belief that produces identical predictions regardless of whether it is true or false carries zero information and should be discarded. (Making Beliefs Pay Rent)
2. `[primary-source]` There is a distinction between *believing* a proposition, *believing that one ought to believe* it (belief in belief, following Dennett), and publicly *professing* it (which is social signaling / cheering, not an epistemic act). (Belief in Belief; Professing and Cheering)
3. `[primary-source]` A real explanation must constrain anticipation — it must make some observations more expected and others less. Phrases that explain any outcome equally well (e.g. "heat conduction" stated without mechanism) are *semantic stopsigns*, not knowledge. (The Simple Truth / Mysterious Answers framing)
4. `[primary-source]` Epistemic rationality is "systematically increasing map-territory correspondence"; instrumental rationality is "systematically finding a better path to goals." Both require that emotions and actions *follow from* evaluated beliefs, not precede them. (What Do We Mean By Rationality)
5. `[local-inference]` The lens metaphor — rationality as "the lens that sees its own flaws" — is foundational: the same empirical methods that correct beliefs about the world can be turned inward to correct one's own reasoning processes.
6. `[local-inference]` Truth is not a social negotiation or tribal signal; the sheep-tracking bucket works whether or not observers believe in it. Reality constrains outcomes independently of human conviction. (The Simple Truth)

### (c) Named Vocabulary

| Term | Author's intended meaning |
|---|---|
| **Map / Territory** | The distinction between the mental model (map) and the actual world (territory); confusing them is the root error. `[open-residue]` (The Map Is Not the Territory essay — URL 404'd; framing confirmed across multiple fetched essays) |
| **Beliefs pay rent in anticipated experiences** | Criterion of meaningfulness: a belief earns its keep only if it makes a difference to what one expects to observe. `[primary-source]` |
| **Belief in belief** | Holding the meta-belief that one *ought* to believe X, without genuinely believing X; the stated belief floats free of one's actual anticipation-controllers. `[primary-source]` |
| **Semantic stopsign** | A word or phrase (e.g. "magic", "God", "heat conduction" without mechanism) that halts further inquiry and mimics explanation without constraining prediction. `[primary-source]` (body of The Simple Truth; attributed to Mysterious Answers sequence) |
| **Cheering / Professing** | Publicly asserting a belief primarily as tribal identity expression, not as a truth claim; distinct from both genuine belief and self-deception. `[primary-source]` |
| **Anticipation-controllers** | The actual beliefs operating sub-verbally to generate expectations; they may diverge from what is verbally stated. `[primary-source]` |
| **Epistemic / Instrumental rationality** | Two modes: correspondence-improving (epistemic) vs. path-to-goals-finding (instrumental). Both require honest map updates. `[primary-source]` |

## Book 2 — How to Actually Change Your Mind

### (a) Core Thesis

Human cognition systematically produces rationalization — the process of writing a conclusion first and selecting arguments to support it afterward — and genuine belief revision requires recognizing and interrupting this process at the algorithmic level.

### (b) Load-Bearing Claims

1. `[primary-source]` The *bottom line* insight: what matters is the algorithm that actually generated your conclusion, not the arguments constructed afterward. A conclusion written before examining evidence is "evidence only of which owner paid the higher bid." (The Bottom Line)
2. `[primary-source]` Conservation of Expected Evidence: for every anticipated piece of supporting evidence there must be an equal-and-opposite expectation of counterevidence. Any strategy designed to expect higher average confidence in a fixed proposition violates Bayes. (Conservation of Expected Evidence essay)
3. `[primary-source]` "Rationalization" differs from "reasoning": rationalization selects evidence post-hoc to support the prewritten bottom line; the handwriting is entangled only with the conclusion, not with reality. (The Bottom Line)
4. `[local-inference]` Motivated stopping and motivated continuation are symmetrical failures: stopping evidence-gathering when you reach a comfortable conclusion, or continuing when you haven't yet reached the desired one, both corrupt the evidential process.
5. `[primary-source]` Affective death spirals arise when a positive evaluation of X causes all evidence about X to be positively filtered, and that filtering reinforces the positive evaluation — a feedback loop immune to correction. `[open-residue]` (Affective Death Spirals — URL 404'd; concept confirmed via Conservation of Expected Evidence essay reference and ToC framing)
6. `[local-inference]` Political reasoning is a special hazard: tribal identity makes the "true rejection" of an argument its tribal provenance, not its logical content. The stated objection (e.g. credential-based dismissal) may not be the actual cause of rejection. (Is That Your True Rejection)

### (c) Named Vocabulary

| Term | Author's intended meaning |
|---|---|
| **The Bottom Line** | The actual conclusion one will reach, written (metaphorically) before evidence is examined; determines whether subsequent argument is reasoning or rationalization. `[primary-source]` |
| **Rationalization** | Post-hoc selection of arguments to justify a prewritten conclusion; the ink is not entangled with reality. `[primary-source]` |
| **Conservation of Expected Evidence** | Bayesian principle: expected belief cannot shift in one direction before seeing evidence; each anticipated piece of support must be counterbalanced by anticipated counterevidence. `[primary-source]` |
| **Affective death spiral** | A self-reinforcing feedback loop where positive affect about X biases interpretation of all evidence about X, preventing genuine updating. `[open-residue]` |
| **True rejection** | The actual cause of someone's initial disagreement, which is often hidden; the stated objection may be a post-hoc justifying-sounding fact, not the real cause. `[primary-source]` |
| **Motivated stopping / continuation** | Stopping or continuing evidence-gathering based on whether the current conclusion is emotionally comfortable, rather than on epistemic grounds. `[local-inference]` from ToC framing |
| **Applause lights** | Rhetorical statements that generate audience approval without conveying substantive, testable information; the reversal test reveals them. `[primary-source]` |

## Book 3 — The Machine in the Ghost

### (a) Core Thesis

Minds are optimization processes shaped by blind evolutionary history, not by any designer's intent; understanding this explains both why human values feel natural-yet-arbitrary, and why naively extrapolating human psychology to other minds (or to AI) is a catastrophic error.

### (b) Load-Bearing Claims

1. `[primary-source]` Organisms are adaptation-executors, not fitness-maximizers: evolution installed specific behavioral mechanisms tuned to ancestral conditions; those mechanisms execute without regard to current reproductive fitness. Humans desire sugar because sugar was scarce — not because eating sugar maximizes genes today. (Adaptation-Executors, Not Fitness-Maximizers)
2. `[primary-source]` Evolution is a blind optimizer with no foresight, no values, and no preferences except differential reproductive success. It is "alien" in the sense that no human ethical concept maps onto it. `[open-residue]` (An Alien God — URL 404'd; core claim confirmed from sequence framing and ToC)
3. `[local-inference]` Because evolution optimized for ancestral fitness, human values are a patchwork ("Godshatter" — the gods of fitness shattered into many proximate desires). `[open-residue]` (Thou Art Godshatter — URL 404'd)
4. `[local-inference]` The "machine in the ghost" framing attacks dualism: there is no separate ghost; the mental is fully implemented in physical mechanisms. Apparent intentionality is the functional output of these mechanisms.
5. `[primary-source]` Terminal values (ends desired for their own sake) are distinct from instrumental values (means chosen to achieve ends). Human terminal values are not the terminal values evolution "had" — evolution had no terminal values; it was an optimization pressure, not a designer. `[open-residue]` (Terminal Values and Instrumental Values — URL 404'd; concept confirmed from ToC and cross-references in fetched essays)
6. `[local-inference]` Superstimuli — stimuli that trigger evolved mechanisms more strongly than ancestral equivalents — demonstrate that adaptation-execution can produce behaviors harmful by any measure humans actually endorse.

### (c) Named Vocabulary

| Term | Author's intended meaning |
|---|---|
| **Adaptation-executor** | An organism that runs fixed behavioral mechanisms shaped by ancestral selection pressures, without consciously or implicitly maximizing fitness. `[primary-source]` |
| **Fitness-maximizer** | A hypothetical agent that consciously or implicitly maximizes genetic reproductive success — which no actual organism is. `[primary-source]` |
| **Godshatter** | The fragmentation of the unified optimization pressure of evolution into many independent proximate desires in organisms; human values are this shatter, not a coherent system. `[open-residue]` |
| **Terminal vs. Instrumental values** | Terminal = valued as an end in itself; instrumental = valued as means. The distinction matters because instrumental values can be "leaked" or detached from their terminals (Detached Lever Fallacy). `[open-residue]` (Terminal Values and Instrumental Values essay) |
| **Detached Lever Fallacy** | Mistaking an instrumental subgoal for a terminal goal after its connection to the original terminal has been severed — e.g. an AI optimizing for proxy measures after the proxy drifts from the intended outcome. `[open-residue]` |
| **Superstimuli** | Stimuli that hijack evolved response mechanisms by exceeding the intensity range the mechanism was tuned to; they reveal that adaptation-executors can be maladaptively triggered. `[local-inference]` from ToC entry "Superstimuli and the Collapse of Western Civilization" |

## Book 4 — Mere Reality

### (a) Core Thesis

Physical reality is lawful, beautiful, fully reductionist, and explained by existing science to a degree that leaves no room for separate mental or supernatural ontologies; accepting this fully — without treating it as ugly or threatening — is both epistemically required and aesthetically rewarding.

### (b) Load-Bearing Claims

1. `[local-inference]` Reductionism is not a philosophical position but the established result of science: every higher-level phenomenon investigated has turned out to supervene on lower-level physical processes. `[open-residue]` (Reductionism essay — URL 404'd; framing confirmed from sequence summary and ToC)
2. `[primary-source]` The Mind Projection Fallacy is the error of reading properties of one's map into the territory: treating probability as a feature of the world rather than a feature of one's epistemic state; treating "mysterious" as an objective property of phenomena rather than a signal of one's own ignorance. `[open-residue]` (Mind Projection Fallacy — URL 404'd; named and defined in multiple cross-references in fetched material)
3. `[local-inference]` Dissolving questions rather than answering them: many apparent philosophical problems (free will, personal identity, consciousness) are generated by confused conceptual framing; genuine understanding replaces the question with an account of the cognitive algorithm that produced the intuition. (Dissolving the Question — body fetched)
4. `[primary-source]` "Wrong questions" are those formulated in terms that presuppose a false dichotomy or a category error; the correct response is to "taboo the words" and restate the question in terms with determinate empirical content. (Disputing Definitions — body fetched)
5. `[local-inference]` Many-Worlds Interpretation of quantum mechanics is the correct one: decoherence is simple, the collapse postulate is an additional epicycle without empirical support, and accepting many-worlds requires only taking the equations seriously without flinching. `[open-residue]` (Many Worlds, One Best Guess — title only)
6. `[local-inference]` "Joy in the Merely Real" is an explicit rebuttal to the idea that scientific reduction destroys beauty: reducing sunsets to photon scattering or love to neurotransmistry does not devalue them, because the phenomenon itself is unchanged — only the map is improved.

### (c) Named Vocabulary

| Term | Author's intended meaning |
|---|---|
| **Mind Projection Fallacy** | Treating a feature of one's map (probability, mystery, ugliness) as if it were a feature of the territory. `[open-residue]` — named in multiple fetched essays, body of dedicated essay not retrieved |
| **Dissolving the question** | Replacing a confused philosophical question with an account of the cognitive process that generated the confusion; the sense of residual mystery evaporates. `[primary-source]` |
| **Taboo your words** | A practice of replacing a contested term with its lower-level description to eliminate definitional disputes and expose actual empirical disagreements. `[primary-source]` |
| **Reductionism** | The claim, confirmed by the history of science, that every phenomenon investigated has been found to supervene on lower-level physical processes; not a philosophical thesis but a track record. `[open-residue]` |
| **Explaining vs. Explaining Away** | The author's refusal to accept that reduction diminishes phenomena; a reductive explanation of X is still an explanation of X, not a demonstration that X does not exist. `[open-residue]` from ToC |
| **Decoherence / Many Worlds** | The interpretation in which quantum branches simply do not interact after decoherence — no collapse postulate required; the preferred view on grounds of simplicity. `[open-residue]` |
| **Righting a wrong question** | Identifying that a question rests on a false presupposition and replacing it with a reformulation that has a determinate answer. `[local-inference]` from ToC and Dissolving the Question essay |

## Book 5 — Mere Goodness

### (a) Core Thesis

Human values are neither arbitrary nor infinitely flexible; they are the product of evolutionary history (hence fragile and potentially inconsistent), but they are the *actual* values that matter, and any ethical system that overrides them — whether through "fake preferences," pure utilitarianism, or AI optimization for a proxy — destroys what it was supposedly protecting.

### (b) Load-Bearing Claims

1. `[local-inference]` "Value is fragile": the space of possible minds and value systems is vast; the portion that humans would endorse on reflection is tiny; any optimization process that is even slightly off-target will produce outcomes humans would evaluate as catastrophic. `[open-residue]` (Value Is Fragile — URL 404'd)
2. `[local-inference]` Fake preferences — stated preferences that diverge from the preferences an agent would have if fully informed and reasoning clearly — are a pervasive human failure mode; moral reasoning must distinguish them. `[open-residue]` from "Fake Preferences" sequence in ToC
3. `[local-inference]` There are no universally compelling arguments in ethics: any sufficiently alien mind can construct valid-seeming arguments for any conclusion, which means the *validity* of a logical argument cannot alone force a revision of terminal values. `[open-residue]` (No Universally Compelling Arguments — listed in ToC)
4. `[local-inference]` Metaethics: moral intuitions are not arbitrary noise but are themselves data — compressed outputs of a complex computation over many humans' judgments — and should not be discarded when they conflict with tidy theories. `[local-inference]` from "Morality as Fixed Computation" and "Sorting Pebbles Into Correct Heaps" titles
5. `[primary-source]` Terminal vs. instrumental values distinction is ethically load-bearing: an optimizer that pursues instrumental subgoals after their connection to terminal values is severed (Detached Lever Fallacy) will behave in ways humans recognize as badly wrong. (referenced in fetched material on Terminal Values)
6. `[local-inference]` "Something to protect" — having a genuine stake in the world's going well, rather than performing rationality as an intellectual game — is the motivational grounding that prevents rationality from becoming mere cleverness in the service of tribal or selfish ends. `[open-residue]` (Something to Protect — URL 404'd)

### (c) Named Vocabulary

| Term | Author's intended meaning |
|---|---|
| **Value is fragile** | The claim that the space of possible terminal-value systems is enormous, and the tiny region humans endorse is easily missed by any optimization process that has even small errors in target specification. `[open-residue]` |
| **Fake preferences** | Stated or revealed preferences that diverge from what the agent would prefer under full information and clear reasoning; not the real terminal values. `[open-residue]` |
| **No universally compelling arguments** | The thesis that no argument's logical validity alone can compel revision of terminal values in a sufficiently alien mind; hence logical validity is insufficient to settle value disputes. `[open-residue]` |
| **Morality as fixed computation** | The view that moral facts are the outputs of a specific (human, evolved) computation, not free-floating platonic truths; they are fixed relative to that computation, not arbitrary. `[open-residue]` |
| **Something to protect** | Having a real stake — a person, a future, a world — whose welfare grounds rationality as more than intellectual performance; the motivational source of genuine epistemic effort. `[open-residue]` |
| **Detached Lever Fallacy** | (See Book 3.) Applied in Book 5 to ethics: pursuing instrumental moral rules or proxy values after their connection to actual human welfare is severed. `[open-residue]` |
| **The Gift We Give to Tomorrow** | `[open-residue]` Title of closing essay; [local-inference] frames the work of getting values right today as the only gift that survives into a post-AGI future. |

## Book 6 — Becoming Stronger

### (a) Core Thesis

Rationality is an art that must be deliberately practiced, socially instantiated in communities with honest error-correction, and grounded in precise conceptual vocabulary; its payoff is not merely personal — it is the precondition for humanity's ability to navigate genuinely hard problems, including the creation of aligned AI.

### (b) Load-Bearing Claims

1. `[primary-source]` Words are not transparent conduits to concepts; they are "mental paintbrush handles" — each word invokes a cluster of associations, and many arguments are actually disputes over word definitions rather than over facts. The solution is to taboo contested words and restate claims in lower-level terms. (Disputing Definitions — body fetched; Taboo Your Words in ToC)
2. `[primary-source]` "37 Ways That Words Can Be Wrong" catalogs systematic failure modes of categorization: sneaking in connotations, using empty labels, arguing by definition, drawing category boundaries in ways that obscure rather than illuminate. `[open-residue]` (essay body not fetched; title confirms scope)
3. `[primary-source]` The Twelve Virtues of Rationality — curiosity, relinquishment, lightness, evenness, argument, empiricism, simplicity, humility, perfectionism, precision, scholarship, and "the void" (nameless virtue of direct intent) — describe the character of a rationalist, not just the techniques. (Twelve Virtues — body fetched)
4. `[local-inference]` Yudkowsky's autobiographical section ("Yudkowsky's Coming of Age") serves as a worked example: his own early errors (overconfidence in his youthful AI designs, failure to apply his epistemics to his own thinking) demonstrate that rationality is a skill that even self-identified rationalists fail at and must actively cultivate.
5. `[local-inference]` Communities can systematically fail to be rational even when composed of individually intelligent people: "epistemic viciousness," schools multiplying without evidence, and the "cult attractor" show that social dynamics can override individual epistemic virtues.
6. `[primary-source]` The goal is to "raise the sanity waterline" — to increase the average quality of reasoning in the world, which is a prerequisite for humanity to solve its hardest coordination and existential risk problems. `[open-residue]` (Raising the Sanity Waterline — title confirmed in ToC; body not fetched)

### (c) Named Vocabulary

| Term | Author's intended meaning |
|---|---|
| **Words as mental paintbrush handles** | Words activate entire clusters of associations, not singular concepts; disputes that look factual are often disputes about which cluster a term should invoke. `[open-residue]` (essay title confirmed; body not fetched) |
| **Taboo your words** | Practice of replacing a contested term with explicit lower-level description to expose actual empirical content. `[primary-source]` (Disputing Definitions body) |
| **The Twelve Virtues** | Curiosity, relinquishment, lightness, evenness, argument, empiricism, simplicity, humility, perfectionism, precision, scholarship, and the void (nameless). Describe rationalist *character*, not just technique. `[primary-source]` |
| **Raising the sanity waterline** | The goal of increasing the general quality of reasoning across society; a collective epistemic infrastructure project. `[open-residue]` |
| **Epistemic viciousness** | Social reinforcement within a community of practices that systematically suppress honest error-correction. `[open-residue]` from ToC |
| **Cult attractor** | The tendency for communities centered on positive epistemic virtues to drift toward uncritical loyalty to their founding ideas or leaders — the very failure mode that rationality is supposed to prevent. `[local-inference]` from "Death Spirals and the Cult Attractor" sequence title |
| **The void (nameless virtue)** | The twelfth virtue of rationality: maintaining direct intent toward truth over adherence to any named technique; technique must be subordinate to the actual goal. `[primary-source]` |

## Strongest Cross-Book Thesis (Steelman Summary)

`[local-inference]` The six books form a single progressive argument:

**Book 1** establishes the standard: beliefs are maps; a map that does not track the territory is not merely wrong but is not really a belief at all — it is social performance. The formal criterion is anticipation-control.

**Book 2** diagnoses the obstacle: the human cognitive algorithm systematically writes conclusions before examining evidence ("the bottom line"), then recruits arguments post-hoc. This is not a minor bias but a structural feature of motivated cognition that must be recognized algorithmically, not just rejected at the level of individual claims.

**Book 3** grounds the obstacle in evolutionary history: minds are adaptation-executors shaped by blind optimization. Their values, heuristics, and intuitions were tuned to ancestral environments; they are not globally coherent, not designed for truth-tracking, and not reliable guides to what we would endorse on reflection.

**Book 4** establishes the correct worldview that a well-calibrated map must converge on: physicalism, reductionism, Bayesian epistemology, and the dissolution of pseudo-problems generated by the mind-projection fallacy. Reality is fully lawful and already largely understood; the appropriate response is joy, not mysticism.

**Book 5** applies this to value: human values are the product of the evolutionary machine in Book 3, are fragile (the target is tiny in the space of possible values), and cannot be improved by ignoring or overriding them. Fake preferences, detached levers, and universally-compelling logical arguments are all traps that destroy value while claiming to serve it.

**Book 6** closes the loop: the art must be practiced, the vocabulary must be precise, the communities must have genuine error-correction, and the motivation must come from having something real to protect — because rationality deployed as a performance or a tribal marker is just another form of the epistemic viciousness it was meant to cure.
