# Governed Definitions

Stable definitions for the highest-reasoning-load concepts later artifacts depend on. Each keeps
**precise statement**, **intuition**, **worked example**, and **anti-misuse** together. These are
the concepts that carry the most weight across the tower's spine.

### 1. Beliefs pay rent (in anticipated experience)
- **source kind:** `primary-source` (B1)
- **precise statement:** A genuine belief must alter the probabilities you assign to some future
  observation. If two beliefs (B and ¬B) imply *exactly* the same anticipations, the belief
  carries zero information and is epistemically empty.
- **intuition:** A belief is a bet on what you'll see; a belief that pays out identically whether
  true or false is not a bet at all.
- **worked example:** "The tree falling makes acoustic vibrations but no auditory experience" vs.
  "...makes a sound" — if both predict the same instrument readings and the same absence of a
  listener's experience, the dispute is verbal, not factual.
- **anti-misuse:** This is a *heuristic test for empty belief*, **not** a theory of meaning. The
  positivist version (R1) over-excluded and was abandoned; do not infer "unobservable ⇒
  meaningless". Arcanum already enforces the operational core via *claim ≤ proof* (P10).

### 2. The bottom line / rationalization
- **source kind:** `primary-source` (B2)
- **precise statement:** The epistemic validity of a conclusion is fixed by the *process* that
  produced it. If the conclusion was fixed first and arguments assembled after, the arguments are
  rationalization — entangled with the desired answer, not with reality — regardless of how good
  they sound.
- **intuition:** "The bottom line is already written; the ink above it is decoration."
- **worked example:** A reviewer who decides a PR is good, then lists reasons, has produced
  rationalization; a reviewer whose verdict moves with each finding has reasoned.
- **anti-misuse:** A post-hoc *explanation* of a correctly-derived conclusion isn't automatically
  rationalization — the test is whether the conclusion *could have moved* given contrary evidence.

### 3. Conservation of Expected Evidence
- **source kind:** `primary-source` (B2)
- **precise statement:** E[posterior | before seeing evidence] = prior. For every outcome that
  would raise P(H), there must be a complementary outcome that lowers it, weighted so the
  expectation is unchanged. No experiment can be *expected* (in advance) to confirm a hypothesis.
- **intuition:** You cannot rig a test you already know will support you; if success would
  confirm H, failure must disconfirm it by the matching amount.
- **worked example:** "If the witch floats she's guilty; if she sinks she's innocent (but drowns)"
  — a test where every outcome confirms guilt violates conservation: it has zero evidential value.
- **anti-misuse:** Governs *expected* (pre-observation) evidence; an actual observed update can of
  course be net-positive. A rubric that can *only* raise confidence violates it (Arcanum bridge B4).

### 4. Adaptation-executors, not fitness-maximizers
- **source kind:** `primary-source` (B3); corroborated `related-source` (R6: Williams; Tooby & Cosmides)
- **precise statement:** Evolved organisms execute mechanisms that *were* selected for fitness in
  the ancestral environment; they do not compute or maximize current inclusive fitness. The
  adaptation runs even when it no longer serves fitness.
- **intuition:** Evolution wrote the code long ago; the organism just runs it, bugs and all.
- **worked example:** Craving sugar/fat — adaptive under ancestral scarcity, maladaptive amid
  modern abundance; the craving fires regardless.
- **anti-misuse:** Do **not** inflate the related "optimization process" metaphor into a unifying
  theorem across evolution, markets, and minds — that is analogy, not proof (L3; blocked as B7).

### 5. Map / territory correspondence
- **source kind:** `local-inference` on the corpus framing; **origin `related-source`** (Korzybski 1931, R11)
- **precise statement:** A belief (map) is accurate to the degree its structure corresponds to the
  structure of reality (territory). Properties of the *map* (uncertainty, mystery, probability)
  must not be projected onto the *territory* (the Mind Projection Fallacy).
- **intuition:** The map is not the territory; confusing the two — e.g. treating your own
  uncertainty as a property of the thing — is a recurring error.
- **worked example:** "This phenomenon is *inherently* mysterious" projects the map-state
  (your confusion) onto the territory; the mystery is in the observer, not the object.
- **anti-misuse:** Credit Korzybski for the phrase, not the corpus. As an Arcanum bridge it is
  *analogy-only* (spec↔reality), largely already covered by definitions-governance drift checks.
