# DomainSpec Core project-history revision blueprint

## Scope and evidence discipline

Revise the history around repository formation, owner transitions, committed mechanisms, bounded validation, formal correction, and current claim boundaries.

- Git dates identify repository events, not idea origins, implementation success, release, or operation.
- Changelogs record declared release surfaces, not deployment or production use.
- Documentation records a contract, design, or status assertion; it does not by itself prove execution.
- Source-reported tests remain source-reported when not rerun.
- A candidate, plan, receipt, or passing bounded validation does not establish authority, promotion, publication, deployment, adoption, or production readiness.
- Distinguish legacy DomainSpec implementation, DomainSpec Core, the private DomainSpec pillar, and the DomainSpec v2 candidate program.
- Distinguish historical CyberAlchemy naming in Arcanum history, public Arcanum, and private CyberAlchemy v2.
- Treat Saturn only as private Saturn-facing architecture/specification, never as a shipped component, repository, implemented loop, public surface, or production capability.
- Exclude Business Philosopher as source spine and as an unsourced prehistory note.

## Diagnosis

The existing history overweights its philosophical/formal lineage and underrepresents parent formation, early governance mechanisms, Arcanum’s separate public ownership and runtime development, validation-repository activity, selective public export, CAV2 authority routing, DomainSpec v2’s candidate program, deterministic build–fail–repair evidence, and bounded local witnesses.

Formalization remains a major correction lens, but only where a proved invalidation or distinction changes claims or architecture.
