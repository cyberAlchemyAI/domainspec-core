This artifact is research synthesis, not legal advice.

## CAS Status

The “Cognitive Act Sovereignty License Addendum” in the corpus is a proposed
policy text. It is not shown to be enacted law, an approved standard, a reviewed
contract, an OSI-approved license, or an accepted data license.

As written, its behavioral restrictions, required remuneration, mandatory local
processing, automatic revocation, and inheritance across consuming systems
conflict with multiple Open Source Definition criteria, including free
redistribution, no discrimination against fields of endeavor, and technology
neutrality. OSI specifically identifies ethical-use restrictions as a common
rejection reason. It must not be called an open-source license.

Sources: [OSI OSD](https://opensource.org/osd) and
[common rejection reasons](https://opensource.org/licenses/common-reasons-for-rejection-of-licenses).

## Distinct Protected Objects

Any future legal route must decide which object it addresses:

- human prompt expression;
- idea, method, or discovery;
- AI-assisted output or human arrangement/edit;
- code;
- invention;
- confidential know-how or trade secret;
- telemetry, biometric data, or inferred mental state;
- dataset or database investment;
- employment contribution;
- contractually recognized contribution;
- community or Indigenous knowledge.

These objects do not share one automatic ownership rule.

## Authorship Boundary

The U.S. Copyright Office's 2025 AI copyrightability report is one
jurisdiction-specific reference. It concludes that prompts alone ordinarily do
not provide enough control to make the prompting user the author of generated
output, while sufficiently creative prompt text, perceptible human expression,
or human selection, arrangement, and modification can require separate
analysis. Other jurisdictions and employment/contract settings can differ.

Source: [U.S. Copyright Office AI Report Part 2](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf).

## Privacy and Erasure Boundary

Precise location, timestamps, biometrics, emotional signals, and persistent
identifiers can be personal or sensitive data and can enable correlation.
Brazil's LGPD provides rights including access, correction, anonymization,
blocking, deletion in specified cases, and consent withdrawal. GDPR and other
regimes add their own territorial and category rules. Public immutability can
conflict with those duties and with personal safety even when a digest is used.

Source: [Brazilian government summary of LGPD data-subject rights](https://www.gov.br/mcti/pt-br/acesso-a-informacao/lei-geral-de-protecao-de-dados-pessoais-lgpd/direito-dos-titulares).

## Minimum Next Legal Packet

Before any license or contract draft:

1. choose governing jurisdiction and forum;
2. define covered parties, assets, acts, and exclusions;
3. distinguish software license, data license, contribution agreement, and
   community governance charter;
4. define acceptance, grant, obligations, termination, survival, conflict, and
   remedy;
5. test privacy, employment, consumer, copyright, patent, trade-secret, and
   database-law interactions;
6. decide whether OSI open-source compatibility is a requirement;
7. obtain qualified legal review.

Until then the corpus language remains a values manifesto and design input.
