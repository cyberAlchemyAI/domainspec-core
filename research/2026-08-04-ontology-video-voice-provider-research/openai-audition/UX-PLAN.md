# UX Plan: Blind Voice Audition

## Human question

Does this one admitted voice configuration sound like the reflective, intimate narrator this ontology story needs—especially when reassurance turns into doubt and when time, confidence, authority, and “Body War” must remain distinct?

This is not a preference carousel. The owner first freezes one exact stock voice/configuration. Blind listening then tests its two repetitions against the approved narration contract; a later comparison provider would use the same protocol.

## Owner preselection surface

Before rendering, the owner records:

- one common accent/locale or explicit permission for provider-native variation;
- one exact stock voice ID;
- the official demo/source heard and date;
- why that voice is plausible for reflective investigation;
- one named production operator.

The current official guide recommends `marin` and `cedar` for best quality. That recommendation narrows where to listen; it is not voice-fit evidence and does not choose for the owner.

## Blind-listening surface

| Surface | Contract |
|---|---|
| Asset label | Opaque UUID; no provider, model, voice, or repetition name. |
| Player | Same local player/version, no enhancement, normalization, or variable speed. |
| Environment | Same headphones, output level, quiet room, and at most one replay. |
| Repetition order | Balanced per listener; the two repetitions are separated. |
| Script access | Same frozen text available to every listener for fidelity context. |
| Score input | Six 1–5 criteria plus optional bounded notes. |
| Lock | Each listener signs/locks before unblinding. |

## Rubric

| Criterion | Weight | Anchor question |
|---|---:|---|
| Reflective warmth/intimacy | 20% | Does it feel like a thoughtful companion, not a keynote or ad? |
| Naturalness/artifact freedom | 20% | Does speech breathe and connect without synthetic distraction? |
| Reassurance-to-doubt semantic arc | 20% | Does “approved” reassure before the question changes the meaning? |
| Time/confidence/authority distinction | 15% | Do the three ideas sound deliberately separate? |
| Pacing, breath, ask-or-stop cadence | 15% | Does the narration stay near 120–130 WPM with restrained emphasis? |
| Product-name/general pronunciation | 10% | Is “Body War” ordinary English with stress on “Body,” and are all key phrases clear? |

Score anchors are `1 = unacceptable/clear failure`, `3 = adequate`, `5 = excellent`; 2 and 4 are intermediate. Weighted points are `Σ(weight × score / 5)` on a 0–100 scale.

## Bias and accessibility controls

- Do not show provider branding, marketing copy, voice gender labels, price, or official quality recommendations during scoring.
- Do not let one listener hear another's score before locking.
- Keep the exact transcript available visually; offer a screen-readable score form and keyboard-only operation.
- If a listener cannot use the common headphones/player condition, record the deviation and exclude that sheet from the primary aggregate rather than hiding the variance.
- Preserve listener/repetition spread and median; do not compress disagreement into one mean.

## Decision boundary

A score sheet can describe fit. It cannot close fidelity, rights, public-use, privacy, retention, security, disclosure, cost, release, or production gates. The named production operator reviews the locked/unblinded evidence and records `approve`, `reject`, or `defer`.
