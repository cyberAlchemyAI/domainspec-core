# Agentic Music Studio — Presentation Review

## S01 · opening

**S01 · projected_copy** — Agentic Music Studio You know how the track should feel. Turning that feeling into sound is the hard part.

**S01 · spoken_copy** — You know when a track needs to feel darker, stranger, sharper, or more alive. But finding the synth settings, effects, and automation that get you there can pull you out of the music.

**S01 · speaker_notes** — Hold after the headline. Do not define the product yet. Let the room recognize the familiar gap between hearing a direction and reaching it.

## S02 · tension

**S02 · projected_copy** — The shortcut The sound arrives fast. But can you still shape it? Change one choice. Replay it. Perform it.

**S02 · spoken_copy** — Prompt-to-audio can give you a result quickly. But when you want to change one choice, replay it exactly, or perform it, you may have only a finished waveform — not something you can keep shaping.

**S02 · speaker_notes** — This is a limitation statement, not a blanket rejection of generative audio. Keep the contrast on inspectability and authorship.

**S02 · interaction_prompt** — What is the last sound you could describe but could not reach quickly?

## S03 · reveal

**S03 · projected_copy** — The proposition Describe it. Hear it. Shape it. Keep it yours. Your sound comes from editable code — not generated audio.

**S03 · spoken_copy** — Agentic Music Studio starts with the sound you’re reaching for — “darker,” “more bite,” “stranger before the drop” — and turns it into a playable result with code you can inspect, edit, and reproduce. We have a working proof, and we are looking for musicians, tool builders, and audio researchers to help shape what comes next.

**S03 · speaker_notes** — This is the approved reveal. Stress “keep it yours.” The no-model claim applies to the sound path; a model may parse intent into an inspectable spec.

## S04 · mechanism

**S04 · projected_copy** — How it works A sound description becomes code you can hear and edit. Every step leaves something the musician can inspect or change.

**S04 · spoken_copy** — The musician describes a direction. The studio makes that direction explicit in a shared spec, writes readable code, renders sound from a fixed seed, and shows what changed. The musician hears it, edits it, or asks for another move.

**S04 · speaker_notes** — “Spec” is now earned because the audience has seen why a black box is insufficient. Avoid explaining package architecture on this slide.

## S05 · proof

**S05 · projected_copy** — Working proof The prototype makes sound today. Real WAV audio. Seeded code. A loop you can steer.

**S05 · spoken_copy** — The current proof renders playable audio from the example “dark violin stochastic synth, bring strangeness.” It has a small intent engine, a deterministic synthesis and measurement core, and a steering loop. This is a proof of the motion, not yet a finished DAW product.

**S05 · speaker_notes** — Play the audio if the room permits. The 14-test count belongs to the dated PDF snapshot; do not present it as a current repository-wide total.

**S05 · interaction_prompt** — What would you try to change first after hearing this texture?

## S06 · measurement

**S06 · projected_copy** — What the proof taught us A meter is not a feeling. Measure the sound. Ask listeners about the experience.

**S06 · spoken_copy** — The first analyzer moved by 6.1 percent when its settings changed. The improved method brought that to 1.3 percent. That makes the measurement more stable. It does not prove that a listener will call the result dark or strange. We want the studio to show that difference honestly.

**S06 · speaker_notes** — Keep analyzer stability and felt experience separate. The listener-study question remains deliberately open.

## S07 · steering

**S07 · projected_copy** — Steering Every creative move has a trade-off. “More bite” changed the sound — and changed what was sacrificed.

**S07 · spoken_copy** — “More bite” was not a universal improvement. It raised dissonance and brightness, which moved the texture away from darkness. A useful studio should show that cost so the musician can choose the trade-off rather than accept a hidden optimization.

**S07 · speaker_notes** — Do not introduce “Pareto navigation” unless the room asks for the formal term.

## S08 · workday

**S08 · projected_copy** — A day in the studio The work stays musical. Sketch. Audition. Sculpt. Perform. Keep the trail.

**S08 · spoken_copy** — A musician begins with a direction, auditions a render, says “less mud, more bite,” and can still open the code to make a precise change. Later, the same movement can be performed live and saved with its seed, measurements, audio, and notes.

**S08 · speaker_notes** — This is a product-use inference from the proven loop, not evidence of a completed DAW integration or validated daily workflow.

**S08 · interaction_prompt** — Where would this loop fit — or break — in your current practice?

## S09 · collaborators

**S09 · projected_copy** — Where you can change the work Three kinds of collaborators can test what matters next. Bring practice, interfaces, or evidence.

**S09 · spoken_copy** — Musicians can tell us whether the interaction respects real creative practice. Tool builders can test how the shared spec connects to DAWs, live coding, and controllers. Audio researchers can help separate stable measurements from felt effects with listening evidence.

**S09 · speaker_notes** — These are invitations, not validated adoption segments. Ask the room which contribution matches its practice.

**S09 · interaction_prompt** — Which of these questions is already yours?

## S10 · invitation

**S10 · projected_copy** — The invitation Bring one sound your current setup makes hard to reach. We will start there.

**S10 · spoken_copy** — If you work in a DAW, build audio tools, live code, or study perception, bring one sound direction that is hard to reach without losing the thread. We can use it to test the loop together and decide what this studio needs to become next.

**S10 · speaker_notes** — End on the invitation. Do not repeat the architecture or claim product readiness.

**S10 · interaction_prompt** — What sound direction would you bring?
