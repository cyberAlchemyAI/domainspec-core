## Direct decision

**Advance two local comparison candidates over the same project records:
decision-lens-first and project-index-first. Component precedent supports
plausibility; no source, explorer result, reviewer result, or local outcome
evidence selects a winner.** This is the exact gate correction required by
Quine’s final review ([“Exact synthesis revisions required,” lines

([“Project-index-first must remain a genuine control,” lines

([“Comparative entrances,” lines
126–136](robot-talks/reviewer-taleb-final.md#L126)).

Both candidates expose the same complete corpus index, project-owned previews,
durable detail routes, and separately governed project contribution states.
Candidate A makes a reversible editorial decision lens the first control.
Candidate B makes the complete project index the first control and retains
lenses as secondary navigation. A surface earns the stronger name **project
map** only after it demonstrates corpus completeness, current/lens position,
direct jumps, return to prior state, and interruption recovery.

This preserves rather than averages the lanes. Lane Z supplies durable
navigation and room recovery; Lane A supplies cold-entry decisions,
project-owned truth, and the independent welcome clock. Lane Z still warns
that live room orientation may require the complete corpus earlier than a cold
self-guided route, while Lane A still warns that project names impose decoding
work on newcomers. [Ahrens, “Final Recommendation and Boundary,” research.md
lines 257–279](research.md#L257); [Senge, “Final Recommendation and Boundary,”
lines 537–553](research.md#L537); [Alexander, “Direct conclusion,” lines
821–829](research.md#L821).

The strongest unresolved issue is not layout. It is authority: project names,
canonical roots, public status, and current asks are not owner-admitted across
the corpus. A project can be real without being open to help, and a plausible
missing witness is not proof of welcome. [Alexander, “Proof-of-project versus
proof-of-welcome,” lines 874–890](research.md#L874); [Christensen,
“Proof-of-project versus proof-of-welcome,” lines
1367–1396](research.md#L1367).

Gate ceiling: this is substrate for Whisper and later Invoke only. Until the
fixed comparison witness runs, it is not admitted information architecture,

## Operational definitions

These meanings govern every later section and the writer handoff. They preserve
Quine’s repaired terms rather than relying on familiar but weaker meanings.
[Quine final, “Repaired terms,” lines
95–114](robot-talks/reviewer-quine-final.md#L95).

| Term | Operational definition | Claim boundary / rejection test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Portfolio opening** | One collection-level tension with simultaneous visible exits to a reversible decision lens and the complete corpus index. | It claims neither personal fit nor a hidden reveal. Reject it if reusable unchanged for an unrelated AI gallery. |
| **Project opening** | The first project-specific statement of felt friction, native verb-object act, current maturity, and adjacent limit. | It is not a slogan, preview synonym, assistant coaching, or miniature specification. |
| **Detail opening** | The independently orienting first view at a durable project route: identity, native act, maturity, proof, adjacent limit, and project contribution state, followed by addressable deeper sections. | It must make sense after direct load and cannot depend on the prior preview, presenter, or hidden state. |
| **Preview** | One bounded comparison object containing recognizable act, current artifact/status, important limit, project contribution state, inclusion reason when lensed, and a durable detail link. | The 10- and 30-second points are reader observations over this one hierarchy, not separate authored objects or guaranteed reading times. |
| **Detail** | The canonical addressable project account expanding mechanism, artifacts, dated proof, limits, authority conflicts, trajectory, distinctions, and conditional welcome evidence. | Depth means inspectability, not length. A repository dump or animated specification is not detail. |
| **Corpus index** | A complete, directly navigable representation of all admitted projects. | It claims completeness and addressability only. |
| **Project map** | A corpus index that also represents current position or lens context, direct jumps, prior-state return, and interruption recovery. | Without every property, call it an index; spatial card arrangement is not a map. |
| **Decision lens** | A reversible editorial projection whose inclusions trace to exact project language and keep the complete corpus one action away. | It is not matching, recommendation, ranking, assessment, or canonical taxonomy. Result count is a test parameter. |
| **Live mode** | A reversible projection-density and traversal policy over the same canonical routes and records. | It may alter size, density, order, and controls only. No factual sentence may exist solely in speech, notes, fragments, or a second document. |
| **Project contribution state** | Project-side `none`, `possibility`, or `open ask`. | `Possibility` is conceivable useful work without invitation. `Open ask` requires a current owner, wanted artifact, prerequisites/scope, recipient, public route, visibility, freshness/expiry behavior, and supportable response expectation. |
| **Recruitment ask** | An owner-originated, current `open ask` record satisfying the preceding obligations. | Project proof, a roadmap gap, issue, repository, next witness, or external contribution precedent cannot populate it. |
| **Interest** | A visitor’s private or weakly expressed inclination to keep considering a project. | The page cannot infer or certify interest from clicks, saves, follows, analytics, or navigation; interest is not an observable rung. |
| **Offer** | A visitor-authored, non-binding statement that they may contribute a named act or artifact through an authorized route. | It is not the owner’s ask and implies no receipt, fit, acceptance, or commitment. |
| **Contact** | A transmitted message to a bound recipient through a disclosed route and visibility policy. | It proves outreach only, not receipt, response, welcome, fit, or acceptance. |
| **Commitment** | A mutually confirmed bounded agreement after human clarification of scope, ownership, artifact, review, and next action. | It occurs outside the showcase and cannot be inferred or emitted by it. |
| **Brief** | Every retained sentence changes recognition, comparison, trust, or responsible next action. | Shortness may not remove maturity, proof, or limit; delete a sentence when its removal changes no decision. |
| **Substantial** | A cold reader can accurately retell the concrete act, present proof/maturity, important limit, and distinction from an adjacent project. | Word count and density are not substitutes for the retell. |
| **Clever** | Optional precision, contrast, or rhythm that makes a true project-owned act memorable without adding or hiding a claim. | Remove it and retest. If accuracy or navigation depends on it, or memorability rises while retell accuracy falls, reject it. |

## 2. Audiences and decision journey

Both a technical builder and a domain practitioner must be able to move through:

1. **Recognize:** “Is a concrete practice or problem here mine?” Use ordinary
   acts before framework vocabulary.
2. **Orient:** choose a shallow lens or see all projects; routes overlap and do
   not assess fit.
3. **Select:** compare a small project set by distinct act, maturity, proof, and
   limit. Begin the witness with three; result cardinality remains a test
   parameter, not a design fact.
4. **Trust:** inspect the project-owned artifact and its claim ceiling.
5. **Approach:** distinguish no ask, a possible next witness, and an owner-bound
   open ask.
6. **Act safely:** save/follow, make a tentative offer, or contact a bound
   recipient; commitment happens only after human clarification.

The decision stages and their minimum information are supported by
Christensen’s “Decision journey,” lines 1147–1158. Domain-specific preconditions
for writers, musicians, designers, developers, researchers, and curious
listeners appear at lines 1160–1171. The technical builder additionally needs
implementation boundary, reproduction surface, and acceptance check; the
domain practitioner needs native verbs, material, authority, and how their
output will be used—not the label “non-technical help.” [Christensen, lines
1162–1171](research.md#L1162).

## 3. Two landing-hierarchy comparison candidates

### Candidate A: decision-lens-first

1. One brief, concrete portfolio opening.
2. Simultaneous exits: a shallow decision lens and **See all projects**.
3. Lens result: a small non-exclusive preview set, each explaining why it
   appeared; three is the initial witness parameter, not a fixed product rule.
4. Complete corpus index, always one action away; it becomes a project map only
   when the stronger recovery behaviors are present.
5. Durable project detail.
6. Project contribution state after project truth.
7. Related projects and return to the previous lens/index state.

This is the decision-to-map-to-route loop proposed after Lane Z exchange
([Senge, lines 521–535](research.md#L521)) joined to Lane A’s asymmetric
decision→truth→contribution sequence ([Alexander, lines
831–849](research.md#L831)).

### Candidate B: project-index-first control

Open with a semantic, nonuniform all-project index organized by project
identity, current maturity, and distinctive act. Decision lenses remain
secondary controls. Explorer evidence identifies a plausible benefit for
returning visitors and a plausible cold-entry cost, but neither is a measured
local outcome. It remains a genuine control over identical records, not a
rejected strawman. [Christensen, “Entry-route comparison,” lines
1110–1119](research.md#L1110); [Taleb final, lines
126–136](robot-talks/reviewer-taleb-final.md#L126). No current source or reviewer
evidence establishes decision-lens-first superiority.

## 4. Index-to-detail and conditional-map route model

Canonical routes should support:

- `/projects` for the complete semantic index;
- reversible query or path state for a decision lens, with reset and “all”
  visible;
- one owner-admitted stable slug per project;
- addressable `evidence`, `limits`, and `contribute` sections;
- native links, breadcrumbs, browser Back/Forward, restoration of lens and
  scroll/focus state, and direct sharing;
- a reversible live-view parameter or control over the same project identity,
  not a separate factual document.

The `/projects` surface is a **corpus index** by default. Call it a **project
map** only when it visibly adds current project/lens position, direct labeled
jumps, return to the prior lens/index state, and recovery after interruption.
Completeness alone does not earn “map.” [Quine final, lines
89–93](robot-talks/reviewer-quine-final.md#L89).

On mobile, comparison reflows without horizontal card dependency; filters do
not become a questionnaire; headings and links remain usable without
enhancement. Keyboard users can reach, operate, reset, and leave lenses;
focus moves to the result heading after an explicit filter action and returns
predictably after detail navigation. Reduced motion and semantic fallback are
mandatory. Long project stories do not live in modals. These requirements
follow the route/history and accessibility evidence at Ahrens lines 43–61 and
Senge’s presenter/self-guided contract and failure table at lines 414–447.

## 5. One preview hierarchy, observed at two depths

### 10-second observation — recognition and claim ceiling

Project-owned felt problem; recognizable act; admitted name/necessary alias;
maturity; one paired honest limit; optional welcome **state**, never an inferred
ask. Decision: “Does this matter to me, and what kind of reality is it?”

### 30-second observation — distinction and trust

What exists now; dated/status-bounded proof; why it may matter; future-labeled
trajectory; why the chosen lens surfaced it; and exactly one project
contribution state: `none`, `possibility`, or owner-backed `open ask`; only
`open ask` may expose an authorized offer/contact route.
Decision: “Why this project, what can I trust, and may I safely approach?”

These are observation points over one preview object, not guaranteed reading
times and not separate authored introductions. [Taleb final, lines
52–70](robot-talks/reviewer-taleb-final.md#L52).

### Detail — inspectability

Project-owned story and vocabulary; mechanism and artifacts; proof receipts and
limits; authority/name/root/publication conflicts; present versus proposed;
safe and forbidden claims; related-project distinctions; next witness; and,
only when open, expected artifact, prerequisites, scope, receiver, review path,
visibility, freshness, and supportable response expectation.

The final grammar is Alexander lines 919–957. Shared questions must not produce
uniform prose: if names can be swapped without rewriting the preview, the work
has been flattened. [Christensen, lines 1300–1308](research.md#L1300).

## 8. Project contribution state and visitor approach

These are orthogonal structures, not one CTA ladder. [Quine final, lines
156–160](robot-talks/reviewer-quine-final.md#L156).

### Project-side contribution state

| State | Meaning | Available public action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `none` | No public contribution invitation or safely publishable possibility is bound. | Inspect/save the project only. |
| `possibility` | A useful conceivable act or next witness is source-bounded, but no invitation, recipient, or submission route is authorized. | Inspect/save; no volunteer submission. |
| `open ask` | A current project owner binds the wanted artifact, prerequisites/scope, recipient, public route, visibility, freshness/expiry, and supportable response expectation. | Inspect/save and optionally author a non-binding offer through the authorized route. |

Proof-of-project fields cannot populate proof-of-welcome. External contribution
precedent, repositories, roadmaps, issues, TODOs, or missing witnesses cannot
populate an `open ask`. [Christensen, lines 1367–1381](research.md#L1367);
[Popper final, lines 165–169](robot-talks/reviewer-popper-final.md#L165).

### Visitor-side approach

1. **Inspect/save:** open evidence, copy a stable link, or follow a real public
   channel.
2. **Non-binding offer:** the visitor states a possible named act/artifact
   through an authorized route.
3. **Contact:** the message is transmitted to a bound recipient under disclosed
   visibility; this proves outreach only.
4. **Human clarification:** people discuss fit, scope, ownership, and review
   outside page automation.
5. **Commitment:** people mutually confirm bounded work outside the showcase.

Interest remains private or weakly expressed. Clicks, saves, follows, dwell
time, or navigation do not prove interest, fit, willingness, welcome, response,
acceptance, or commitment. The split repairs Christensen’s progressive ladder
([research.md lines 1173–1182](research.md#L1173)) without collapsing project
ask and visitor offer.

### Two-clock degradation

The truth clock changes only when project identity, maturity, proof, limit,
authority, or public boundary changes. The welcome clock changes when an owner
opens, changes, pauses, expires, or closes an ask. [Alexander, lines
906–917](research.md#L906).

Witness this with the same Poker record rendered first as `possibility`, then
as `none/unbound`: project truth must remain byte-equivalent or field-equivalent
while only contribution language and controls change. A future owner-confirmed
`open ask` must have an independent last-confirmed value and expiry/stale
behavior; expiry degrades the contribution state without changing project
truth. [Taleb final, lines
213–225](robot-talks/reviewer-taleb-final.md#L213).

## 9. Visual and interaction direction

- Let typography, headings, and project-owned nouns carry distinction before
  card chrome.
- Use nonuniform but comparable previews; do not encode maturity or priority
  only through size, color, position, or animation.
- Keep proof adjacent to limit and contribution state visually independent.
- Make the corpus index—and, only when its recovery contract is met, the project
  map—legible at projection distance and semantically readable when
  styling/enhancement fails.
- Prefer direct links, persistent orientation, visible reset/return, and
  labeled jumps.
- Motion is manual, pausable, nonessential, reduced-motion aware, and never
  carries truth.
- No autoplay, carousel dependency, long-form modal, presenter-dependent
  fragments, or full-screen deck as the default website.
- Preserve each domain’s verb/object pair; “clever” means precise contrast or a
  memorable concrete act, not hype, mystery, puns, anthropomorphic AI, or
  jargon. [Alexander, lines 616–621](research.md#L616).

Composition acceptance applies three edits to every portfolio opening, project
opening, and preview:

1. delete each sentence; cut it if recognition, comparison, trust, or a
   responsible next action does not change;
2. swap two project names while leaving prose unchanged; if either remains
   plausible, reject both previews and restore project-owned act, material,
   proof, maturity, and limit;
3. remove each clever phrase and compare accurate retelling; reject it when
   memorability increases while factual accuracy falls.

Public candidate copy also bans these verb families unless independently
admitted: `matches/recommends` for editorial lenses;
`understands/thinks/wants` for AI behavior; `joins/enrolls/accepts` for contact;
`proves` for screenshots or clicks; and unqualified `ships/works` for candidate,
research, scaffold, or specification maturity. [Quine initial, “Exact
actionable challenges,” lines
91–106](robot-talks/reviewer-quine-initial.md#L91).

These are evidence-bounded principles, not screenshot taste or a selected
visual style. Accessibility and failure constraints are supported by Ahrens
lines 43–61 and Senge lines 338–344, 433–447.

### Evidence lane B — conversational-AI/chat terminology and copy

Audit assistant coaching, anthropomorphism, unexplained internal terms, generic
AI claims, name-substitutable prose, hidden authority changes,
future-as-present, inferred welcome, misleading verb families, and
meta-narration. Apply sentence deletion, project-name substitution,
clever-phrase removal, and reveal-on versus resolved-static comparison. These
are definitional/copy checks, not browser or precedent evidence.

### Evidence lane C — human comprehension, coherence, and decision clarity

Use exactly two uncoached cold readers:

- one technical builder unfamiliar with the internal framework;
- one domain practitioner, preferably a musician working with AI without being
  required to write framework code.

After portfolio opening and preview scan ask:

1. Which project would you inspect next, and which content caused the choice?
2. What exists now?
3. What important thing is not claimed?
4. How does it differ from an adjacent preview?

After detail and contribution state ask: “What, if anything, can you responsibly
do next from this page, and what still requires an owner or human to confirm?”

Each reader must independently choose for a content-bearing reason, accurately
retell native act, proof/maturity, limit, and adjacent distinction, and state
that Poker’s possibility provides no current volunteer route. They must not
convert possibility into welcome, contact into response, or contact into
commitment. Different project preferences are allowed. A perfect browser path
with inaccurate retelling fails. [Taleb final, “Human evidence,” lines
186–211](robot-talks/reviewer-taleb-final.md#L186).

### Two-clock witness

Run the same Poker truth record once with `possibility`, then with
`none/unbound`. Project identity, act, maturity, proof, limit, detail, and route
must remain byte-equivalent or field-equivalent; only contribution copy and
controls change. Both readers must understand that the project remains real and
inspectable while no open ask exists. A later `open ask` needs a separate
last-confirmed value, expiry, and degradation rule. [Taleb final, lines
213–225](robot-talks/reviewer-taleb-final.md#L213).

### Mandatory known-bad and false-positive fixtures

1. **Known bad — impressive equal-card gallery:** fourteen equal cards,
   interchangeable “AI-powered” summaries, green `Active` badges, repository
   links, modal detail, generic `Join us`, and autoplay live mode. It must fail
   even if visually excellent.
2. **False positive A — mechanics pass, decision fails:** semantic headings,
   stable URLs, perfect history/keyboard/reflow/reduced-motion/live mechanics,
   but all three previews say “working AI tool” and derive `Contribute` from
   next witnesses. Fail if Music Studio becomes usable, Sonar becomes
   implemented, or Poker becomes welcoming in either retell.
3. **False positive B — parity appears green:** both modes render the same
   compared fields, but notes/speech alone explain Kuhn-only scope or unbound
   welcome. Fail: material truth is presenter-dependent.
4. **False positive C — clever recall harms truth:** a reveal improves Poker
   name recall but readers retell it as a hosted competitive arena. Kill the
   reveal mechanism.

These fixtures and expected failures are specified by Taleb final lines
234–269. Screenshots, clicks, schemas, polish, or mechanical passes cannot
launder false maturity, welcome, or understanding.

### Explicit non-vacuity and definitional KILL emitters

Precedent never emits `KILL`. The **non-vacuity gate** emits
`KILL_UNLESS_COMPARATIVE_GAIN` for the reveal when reveal-on does not improve
correct route choice or proof/limit retelling; it emits `KILL` for generic
`Join`, inferred asks, route-hostile long dialogs, truth-bearing fragments,
forced scrollytelling, presenter-dependent truth, and Exchange machinery when
they cannot support the required responsible decision. [Taleb final, lines
213–232](robot-talks/reviewer-taleb-final.md#L213).

The **definitional-soundness gate** emits `KILL` for an ordinary equal-card grid
called a map, filter UI described as matching/recommendation, a slide deck
called live mode, a jobs board called recruitment, Exchange states called
contact, generic AI prose called project voice, long specification called
detail, animation called progressive disclosure, roadmap gap called open ask,
click called interest, mechanics called understanding, or a dramatic reveal
called comprehension when it is retained as an architectural mechanism without
improving correct route choice or proof/limit retelling over the resolved-static
control. A removable static contrast may still survive as copy craft under the
`clever` test; it does not survive as a comprehension mechanism. [Quine
adjudication, lines
36–46](robot-talks/reviewer-quine-adjudication.md#L36); [Quine final,
“Exact reskinned KILLs,” lines
116–133](robot-talks/reviewer-quine-final.md#L116).
