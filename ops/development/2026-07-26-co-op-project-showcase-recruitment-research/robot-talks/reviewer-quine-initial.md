# INITIAL REVIEWER POSITION — Quine, Willard Van Orman

Gate: definitional soundness only.

Scope: candidate synthesis, tested against the explorer transcript and the
registered reviewer prompt. This review does not establish precedent ownership,
implementation feasibility, usability, recruitment efficacy, or public
authorization.

## Position

**CONDITIONAL PASS.** The synthesis does not presently reduce to an ordinary
portfolio grid, hidden slide deck, jobs board, generic AI gallery, or animated
specification. Its meaningful difference is the conjunction of:

1. a reversible decision lens that does not claim to match people to projects;
2. a complete, map-like corpus view that remains immediately recoverable;
3. durable project routes carrying proof beside limit;
4. a separate, owner-confirmed welcome record; and
5. a live view that changes projection density and traversal, not facts or
   document identity.

Remove any one of the last three constraints and the candidate becomes an
ordinary reskin. The synthesis is therefore sound only if the writer preserves
the repaired definitions below. The current “CTA ladder” also confuses a
publisher's recruitment ask with a visitor's offer and places “interest” too
close to observable actions. That is repairable, but it must not pass into
`findings.md` unchanged.

## Repaired term and claim table

| Term | Operational definition | Necessary distinction / claim ceiling | Collapse test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Portfolio opening** | The first compact statement of the collection-level tension, followed immediately by at least two explicit exits: a reversible decision lens and the complete project map. | It frames why these projects are shown together. It does not summarize fourteen projects, promise a reveal, coach the reader, or claim personal fit. | If it can precede any unrelated AI collection unchanged, it is generic gallery copy. If it withholds scope until a dramatic reveal, it is presentation theater. |
| **Project opening** | The first project-specific unit that names one felt friction, one native verb-object act, current maturity, and one adjacent limit. | It establishes recognition and claim ceiling. It is not a slogan, teaser, generic mission, or miniature specification. | If another project name can be substituted without rewriting the nouns and verbs, KILL it as uniform AI prose. |
| **Preview** | A bounded comparison unit that answers: what practice is affected, what the project lets someone do, what exists now, what important limit applies, and why this preview appeared under the selected lens. It links to one durable detail route. | A preview supports selection among projects. It need not contain mechanism, history, every proof receipt, trajectory, or recruitment workflow. “10-second” and “30-second” are test conditions over the same preview hierarchy, not two different content objects. | If every preview is an equal card with title, blurb, image, and “learn more,” the proposal is an ordinary portfolio grid. |
| **Detail** | The canonical, addressable project account that expands the preview into project-owned story, mechanism, current artifacts, dated/status-bounded proof, limits, authority conflicts, trajectory, related-project distinctions, and conditional welcome evidence. | Detail means inspectability, not length. It separates present, proposed, private, and public claims. It is not a repository dump or a specification pasted into a page. | If animation, accordions, or scrolling merely reveal specification sections without changing what a visitor can verify or decide, KILL it as an animated specification. |
| **Live mode** | A reversible projection policy over the same canonical records and routes: larger type, lower density, deliberate sequence, visible map/current position, direct jumps, and interruption recovery. | It may suppress secondary detail but may not change name, maturity, proof, limit, ask state, or claim ceiling. Presenter notes are non-authority. Self-guided URLs and browser navigation remain valid. | If meaning depends on next/previous slides, spoken transitions, hidden presenter context, or a second factual document, KILL it as a slide deck hidden in a website. |
| **Recruitment ask** | A publisher-side, current, owner-confirmed record that names the wanted contribution, expected artifact, prerequisites/scope, recipient, public contact route, freshness, visibility, and supportable response expectation. | An ask is evidence that help is wanted now. A plausible next witness, repository issue, project proof, or general “we welcome contributors” statement is not an ask. | If projects are primarily rows of openings, roles, deadlines, or application controls, KILL it as a jobs board. |
| **Interest** | A visitor's private or weakly expressed inclination to keep considering a project. Inspecting, saving, copying a link, or following a real channel may indicate continued attention but does not prove willingness, fit, contact, or commitment. | Interest is not a page-authored lifecycle state and should not be inferred from clicks. The surface supplies interest-preserving affordances; it does not certify interest. | If analytics or a button converts browsing into “interested candidate,” the term is unsound. |
| **Offer** | A visitor-authored, non-binding statement that they may be able to contribute a named act or artifact against an open ask or authorized inquiry route. | An offer originates with the visitor; an ask originates with the project owner. Neither implies acceptance. | If “I can help” immediately enrolls, matches, reserves, or assigns work, the showcase has become an exchange/application system. |
| **Contact** | A transmitted message through a named public route to a bound recipient, with disclosed information and visibility expectations. | Contact proves only that outreach was sent through the stated route. It does not prove receipt, response, fit, welcome, or acceptance. | “Contact us” without recipient, route, purpose, and consequence is decorative CTA copy. |
| **Commitment** | A mutually confirmed, bounded agreement reached after human clarification of scope, ownership, expected artifact, review, and next action. | Commitment occurs outside the showcase. The page may describe this boundary but cannot generate or infer commitment. | Any “join,” “claim,” “matched,” or “you are in” state emitted by the showcase is prohibited exchange machinery. |
| **Brief** | The opening/preview contains no field that does not change recognition, comparison, trust, or next action; secondary evidence is deferred to detail. | Brief is information economy, not word-count minimalism or omission of proof/limit. | Remove each sentence in turn. If no decision changes, cut it. |
| **Detailed / substantial** | The unit includes enough discriminating particulars to state what one can do, what exists, and what cannot yet be claimed; detail is available at a stable route. | “Substantial” does not mean dense. A short preview can be substantial when its concrete act, maturity, proof, and limit are specific. | Ask a cold reader to retell act, present reality, and one limit. Failure means the copy is thin regardless of length. |
| **Clever** | Precision that compresses a true contrast or makes a concrete domain act memorable without adding a claim. It is optional and removable. | Cleverness may live in exact framing, juxtaposition, rhythm, or project-owned concrete language. It excludes hype, mystery, puns, jargon, canned transitions, anthropomorphic AI, and meta-narration. | Remove the clever phrase. If truth, navigation, or comprehension breaks, it was concealment rather than craft. If memorability rises while retell accuracy falls, reject it. |

## Candidate verdicts

| Candidate / mechanism | Definitional verdict | Reason and required boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decision-first, map-recoverable, route-durable showcase | **PASS WITH REPAIR** | It is more than a portfolio grid only when lenses are reversible editorial views, the complete map is one action away, and all routes converge on the same project truth. Never call lens output a match or recommendation. |
| Project-index-first alternative | **PASS AS CONTROL** | A semantic index is intentionally close to a portfolio. Its value is corpus visibility and comparison, not novelty. It becomes a KILL candidate only if equal cards erase maturity, acts, proof, and limits. |
| Fourteen-project map | **PASS** | “Map” means complete orientation, current position, and recovery. If it is merely fourteen cards arranged spatially, rename it “index”; do not borrow the stronger term. |
| Shared 10-second / 30-second / detail grammar | **PASS WITH REPAIR** | Treat 10 and 30 seconds as comprehension depths over one structured preview, not content species or guaranteed reading times. Preserve project-owned prose and maturity differences. |
| Durable project detail | **PASS** | It changes the visitor's verification capacity and supports direct address, Back, and return. Do not equate depth with length or expose a raw long-form specification as “story.” |
| Live mode over shared facts | **PASS WITH HARD INVARIANT** | It is a view policy, not a second ontology or deck. Stable routes, map recovery, direct jumps, and factual parity are constitutive, not optional enhancements. |
| Conditional recruitment ladder | **PASS WITH TERMINOLOGY REPAIR** | Recast it as two sides: owner **ask** and visitor **approach** (`inspect/save → offer → contact → human clarification → commitment`). Interest is not an observable rung; contact is not commitment. |
| Portfolio-level narrative reveal | **ANALOGY ONLY** | A resolved contrast can sharpen the opening, but no fact or route may depend on staged revelation. Remove it if it produces mystery or presenter dependence. |
| Equal-card project grid | **KILL** | It normalizes incomparable maturity and domain acts into title/blurb/image/link and supplies no meaningful decision beyond “open a card.” |
| Full-screen sequence or slide ontology | **KILL** | A website that depends on ordered frames, spoken transitions, or presenter-only context is a deck with browser chrome. |
| Ask-first opportunity board / application flow | **KILL** | It makes vacancies rather than project truth the primary object and imports matching, intake, or commitment states excluded by the goal. |
| Generic “AI-powered projects” gallery | **KILL** | “AI” becomes the only shared noun while writers', musicians', designers', researchers', and developers' acts disappear. No project distinction survives name substitution. |
| Animated long specification | **KILL** | Progressive reveal, scroll choreography, or accordions do not constitute progressive disclosure when the visitor receives the same undigested specification and gains no clearer decision. |

## Exact actionable challenges

1. **Repair the CTA ladder everywhere.** Replace `Curiosity → Interest → Offer →
   Contact → Clarification → Commitment` with two explicitly different records:
   project-side `none | possibility | open ask`, and visitor-side
   `inspect/save | non-binding offer | contact | human clarification |
   commitment`. State that interest is neither reliably observable nor
   inferred from interaction.

2. **Disambiguate every use of “opening.”** Label it either `portfolio opening`,
   `project opening`, or `detail opening`. Define one preview object whose
   hierarchy is tested at 10 and 30 seconds; do not imply that every project
   needs three separately authored introductions.

3. **Make “map” earn its name.** Require completeness, current-position
   indication, direct project jumps, return to the prior lens/index state, and
   interruption recovery. If the proposed artifact cannot satisfy these, call
   it an index rather than a map.

4. **State the live-mode identity invariant as an acceptance condition.** The
   same stable project URL and factual record must remain usable outside live
   mode; projection may alter density, order, and controls only. Add a
   known-bad fixture where browser mechanics pass but a claim appears only in
   presenter notes or spoken narration.

5. **Operationalize “brief but detailed and clever.”** For each opening and
   preview, run three edits/tests: sentence-deletion for brevity; cold-reader
   retell of concrete act, present proof/maturity, and one limit for substance;
   and clever-phrase removal plus retell-accuracy comparison. Cleverness fails
   if it increases memorability by decreasing factual accuracy.

6. **Add the name-substitution kill test to composition acceptance.** Swap two
   project names while leaving their preview prose unchanged. If either still
   reads plausibly, reject both previews and restore project-owned verb-object
   pairs, materials, proof, and limits.

7. **Ban the five misleading verb families in public candidate copy unless
   independently admitted:** `matches/recommends` for editorial lenses,
   `understands/thinks/wants` for AI behavior, `joins/enrolls/accepts` for
   contact, `proves` for screenshots or clicks, and `ships/works` for candidate,
   research, scaffold, or specification maturity.

## Closing gate statement

No synthesized candidate is killed wholesale. The architecture remains
definitionally non-tautological because it changes three visitor decisions:
how to enter without knowing project names, how to inspect reality and limits
at a durable destination, and how to distinguish possible help from an
owner-backed invitation. The listed collapse candidates are killed, and the
conditional pass expires if the repaired meanings are weakened in the writer
artifact.
