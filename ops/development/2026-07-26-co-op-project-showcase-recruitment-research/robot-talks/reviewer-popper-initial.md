## Weak analogies

1. **NASA/JPL → fourteen Co-Op projects.** Mission collections are much larger,
   use mature public taxonomies, and serve visitors who may already know mission
   status/type. They support durable collection/detail mechanics, not the local
   lens vocabulary or decision-first ordering.
2. **reveal.js → governed showcase IA.** reveal.js demonstrates controls and
   multiple views, not shared factual authority, route semantics, comprehension,
   or presenter-independent truth.
3. **WAI component patterns → content suitability.** APG explains how to make a
   chosen widget operable. It does not itself establish that accordions are good
   supplementary containers or dialogs bad project containers; those are
   obligation-fit inferences.
4. **Mozilla/Creative Commons → local recruitment readiness.** Act/skill entry
   is deployed, but those organizations have explicit public participation
   programs. Their invitation cannot be borrowed to fill missing local owners,
   asks, routes, or response capacity.
5. **Outreachy → lightweight CTA ladder.** Outreachy’s clarity comes from a
   staffed, scheduled operational program. Removing that machinery preserves
   only a useful analogy, not evidence that a six-step local ladder will work.
6. **CHAOSS guidance → observed welcome.** Expectation-setting can reduce
   uncertainty, but a published response target is not proof of actual response
   behavior.
7. **GOV.UK service demo → project comprehension.** Actor/action/payoff is a
   credible presentation grammar, but service-demo guidance does not validate
   recall across fourteen heterogeneous projects.
8. **Misconception-to-reveal / Veritasium.** No current primary source was
   supplied or independently established for the claimed movement. Retain it as
   removable local editorial technique, not sourced precedent.
