# Findings — Co-Op Project Showcase and Recruitment

## Direct answer

The Co-Op project page should be one browser-native truth surface with two
genuine entrances over the same records: **decision-lens-first** and
**project-index-first**. Both must lead to project-owned previews, durable
project routes, adjacent proof and limit, and a separately governed
contribution state. The same routes and facts should support the existing
45-minute meeting through a reversible low-density live view; live mode may
change size, order, density, and controls, but never facts. No evidence yet
selects either entrance, proves comprehension, or proves recruitment.
[Ahrens, final recommendation, `research.md` lines 257–279](research.md#L257);
[Senge, final recommendation, lines 537–553](research.md#L537);
[Alexander, direct conclusion, lines 821–829](research.md#L821).

This is research substrate for a Whisper composition experiment and later
Invoke design. It is not final copy, implementation, public admission, a
redesign of the meeting, or proof that anyone understands, feels welcome, or
will contribute.

## Corrected scope and solution-independent problem

**Fact.** The Co-Op Engine is already a weekly meeting. The researched object
is its dual-use project showcase and recruitment artifact, not an Experiment
Exchange, jobs board, matching service, application flow, or new meeting
mechanism. The earlier Exchange is a typed negative for this corrected goal.
[Christensen, recruitment/presentation counterexample, lines
1215–1221](research.md#L1215).

**Problem.** A cold visitor needs to understand which of fourteen projects
matters to them, trust what exists and what does not, and see a responsible
next action without reading fourteen mini-specifications or being sold finished
products. Technical builders and domain practitioners must both enter through
recognizable acts before internal framework vocabulary. [Christensen, decision
journey and audience needs, lines 1147–1171](research.md#L1147).

**Proposal.** Preserve one factual corpus and compare two entrance orders.
Do not infer fit, welcome, interest, response, or commitment from navigation.

## Audience and decision requirements

Both audiences must recognize a native practice, orient without assessment,
select by content, inspect proof and limit, distinguish project reality from
welcome, and act without invented commitment. A technical builder additionally
needs the implementation boundary, reproduction surface, and acceptance check.
A writer, musician, designer, researcher, or curious listener needs native
verbs, material, authority, and how an output would be used—not “non-technical
help.” [Christensen, lines 1147–1171](research.md#L1147).

The page must let a visitor answer:

1. Which project would I inspect, and which content caused that choice?
2. What exists now?
3. What important adjacent claim is excluded?
4. How does it differ from another project?
5. What, if anything, may I responsibly do next?

## Two comparison candidates

**Candidate A — decision-lens-first.** Opening; simultaneous “find by what I
want to do” and “see all projects”; a small, non-exclusive result set explaining
each inclusion; complete index one action away; detail; contribution state;
return to prior lens/index state. Lens result count is a test parameter.
[Alexander, asymmetric sequence, lines 831–849](research.md#L831).

**Candidate B — project-index-first control.** Opening; complete semantic,
nonuniform index by identity, act, maturity, proof/limit; lenses as secondary
controls; same previews and detail routes. It is not a deliberately weak grid.
[Christensen, entry-route comparison, lines 1110–1119](research.md#L1110).

## Overview, navigation, and route model

**Proposal.** `/projects` is the complete semantic corpus index. A reversible
query/path carries lens state. Each owner-admitted slug has addressable
`evidence`, `limits`, and `contribute` sections. Native links, breadcrumbs,
Back/Forward, direct load, refresh, prior-state return, and scroll/focus
restoration are required. Live view is a reversible parameter over the same
identity and facts.

Call the overview a **project map** only after it witnesses completeness,
current project/lens position, direct labeled jumps, return to prior
lens/index state, and interruption recovery. Otherwise it is a corpus index.
Mobile reflows without horizontal comparison; keyboard users can operate,
reset, and leave lenses; reduced-motion and semantic no-enhancement fallbacks
retain truth and routes. [Ahrens, lines 43–61](research.md#L43);
[Senge, lines 414–447](research.md#L414).

## The 10-second / 30-second / deep grammar

These are observation depths over one hierarchy, not guaranteed reading times
or separately authored summaries.

- **10-second:** project-owned felt friction; recognizable native act; admitted
  name/alias; maturity; adjacent limit; optional contribution **state**.
- **30-second:** what exists; dated/status-bounded proof; why it may matter;
  future-labeled trajectory; lens inclusion reason; exactly one of `none`,
  `possibility`, or owner-backed `open ask`. Only `open ask` exposes an
  authorized offer/contact route.
- **Deep:** mechanism, artifacts, receipts, limitations, authority/name/root
  conflicts, present/proposed distinction, safe/forbidden claims, adjacent
  projects, next witness, and—only for an open ask—artifact, prerequisites,
  scope, receiver, review, visibility, freshness, and response expectation.

The source grammar is Alexander lines 919–957; shared questions must normalize
structure, not voice. [Christensen, lines 1300–1308](research.md#L1300).

## Project-side welcome and visitor-side approach

Project-side:

- `none`: inspect/save only.
- `possibility`: inspect/save only; no volunteer submission.
- `open ask`: an owner has bound the artifact, scope, recipient, route,
  visibility, freshness/expiry, and response expectation; a visitor may author
  a non-binding offer.

Visitor-side:

1. inspect/save;
2. make a non-binding named offer through an authorized route;
3. contact a disclosed recipient;
4. clarify fit/scope/review with humans;
5. mutually commit outside the showcase.

Project proof, repositories, issues, roadmaps, or missing witnesses cannot
populate an open ask. Clicks cannot populate interest. [Christensen, lines
1367–1381](research.md#L1367). Truth and welcome have independent clocks;
expiry changes contribution controls without rewriting project truth.
[Alexander, lines 906–917](research.md#L906).

## Visual and interaction principles

- Let typography, headings, native nouns, and verb-object acts differentiate
  projects before card chrome.
- Use nonuniform but comparable previews; never encode maturity/priority only
  through size, color, position, or animation.
- Keep proof adjacent to limit and contribution state visually independent.
- Keep index/map readable at room distance and semantic without styling.
- Prefer native links, persistent orientation, visible reset/return, and
  labeled jumps.
- Motion is manual, pausable, nonessential, and reduced-motion aware.
- Reject autoplay, carousel dependency, long modal stories, presenter-only
  fragments, and deck-as-default-site.
- “Clever” means precise, removable contrast—not hype, mystery, puns,
  anthropomorphic AI, or jargon. [Alexander, lines 616–621](research.md#L616).
