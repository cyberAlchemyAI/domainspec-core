# WaveDB For Craft Runtime Research Strategy

## Direct Strategy

Research whether WaveDB can safely add runtime capabilities to Craft without
silently changing Craft's current source-of-truth, derived-view, evidence, or
owner contracts. Compare three postures and issue a separate verdict for each:

1. WaveDB as a rebuildable projection or query cache.
2. WaveDB as a transactional runtime sidecar while `.craft/ledger.yml` remains
   authoritative.
3. WaveDB as the replacement canonical Craft store, which is inadmissible
   without a separately owned Craft contract and migration decision.

The result is an exact-bound architecture and falsifiable prototype plan. This
proposal does not implement, install, promote, publish, or deploy anything.

## P1 Trigger Decision

Dispatch is required. Synthesis combines at least four independent source and

the WaveDB, Craft-contract, failure/migration, and fixture-design lanes can run

The live type is `research`, and the candidate sheet uses schema `0.8.0`.

## Dependency Flow

```text
explorers
  -> synthesizer
  <-> reviewers (zig-zag cap 1)
  -> exact-binding evidence closure
  -> writer
  -> auditor
  -> parent final approval

synthesizer -> explorers (feedback cap 1, exact-source repair only)
auditor -> writer (feedback cap 1, writing repair only)
```

Feedback edges are repair routes, not initial dependencies. The writer stays
blocked until every load-bearing row is bound, deferred, or killed.

## Research Obligations

The dispatch must return:

- an immutable WaveDB source identity and exact source selectors;
- a current-versus-roadmap claim matrix for relevant WaveDB features;
- canonical Craft invariants that no adapter may silently change;
- a row, link, index, artifact, workspace, and operation-to-key mapping;
- separate verdicts for projection/cache, YAML-authoritative sidecar, and
  canonical-store replacement;
- adapter and transaction boundaries, including snapshot and batch semantics;
- migration, rollback, rebuild, crash, concurrency, corruption, downgrade,
  encryption/key, license, dependency, and packaging treatment;
- one finite synthetic fixture with positive, negative, no-op, and recovery
  cases;
- deterministic, integration, recovery, migration, concurrency, and bounded
  benchmark hypotheses;
- ordered implementation slices and their owner decisions;
- explicit claim ceilings separating README, source inspection, planned
  fixture, executed fixture, promotion, and production evidence.
