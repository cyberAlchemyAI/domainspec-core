# C03 — Deliberately overstory-rich stressor

## Candidate card

- Control role: deliberately excessive cast, scene detail, dialogue, and analogy density.
- Status: negative stress control, not disguised as preferred practice.
- Design claim under test: if “more concrete” is interpreted as “more story,” causality and protected controls may disappear beneath memorable but competing material.
- Excess inventory: six recurring names, two locations, incidental sensory detail, multiple dialogue turns, and two mapped analogies per route. Every analogy still states its mapping, limit, and drop condition so the stress comes from load rather than semantic misconduct.

## Route 1 sample — opening, two work cards, shared attempt, branch explanation, formal reveal, transfer, claim limit

### Opening

At 3:17 p.m., rain ticks against Lantern's fifth-floor windows while Priya balances a cold coffee beside the release keyboard. Nora has sent three screenshots from the 8:05 train: a teal consent banner covers the coral **Reserve** button on her 390-pixel phone. Designer Jae is redrawing the banner, reviewer Cora is opening the account-deletion checklist, implementation owner Imani is asking who owns the fix, and operations lead Tomas is counting the seventeen minutes before the rehearsal release.

“Launch a whole crew at the button,” Jae says. “An orchestra is better than a solo.”

“An orchestra maps coordinated parts,” Cora replies, “but it does not tell us whether another judgment must stay separate, and a conductor grants no repository authority. Drop that picture before choosing.”

Priya opens two bright cards. “Then decide what problem each card is solving.”

### Two work cards and shared attempt

**Card A — Nora's hidden button:** reproduce the exact viewport, identify the selector and file line, capture the screenshot, and return one diagnosis without editing. Priya will inspect it while Jae waits.

- One investigator returns one evidence report for Priya to inspect before deciding.
- Two reviewers return separate evidence reports for Priya to compare before deciding.

Binding: `choose-one`; values `helper`, `dispatch`; expected `helper`.

**Card B — the midnight deletion promise:** Cora checks whether seven stored data classes are removed as promised; Tomas separately checks whether a keyboard and screen-reader user can complete or cancel without being trapped. Priya needs both reports separately before the go/no-go meeting.

- One investigator returns one evidence report for Priya to inspect before deciding.
- Two reviewers return separate evidence reports for Priya to compare before deciding.

Binding: `choose-two`; values `helper`, `dispatch`; expected `dispatch`.

Shared action: **Check both decisions**.

### Branch explanation

- Card A, correct: Priya says, “Yes. One bounded diagnosis supplies the inspectable result this card needs.” Jae closes the unused second-review tab.
- Card A, corrective: Cora asks, “What second judgment protects Nora?” The screenshot, selector, and line are one limited diagnosis.
- Card B, correct: Tomas says, “Yes. The data promise and accessible escape remain separately visible.”
- Card B, corrective: Priya says, “One combined voice would hide whether both obligations received their own examination.”

Now picture both a **repair bench** and a **two-key cabinet**. The bench maps one technician tracing one fault; the cabinet maps responsibilities that cannot substitute for each other. The bench does not prove the diagnosis, the cabinet does not mean every dispatch is literal independent approval, and neither grants authority. Drop both images when exact names appear.

### Formal reveal

1. **boundary** — a decision, permission, or responsibility that must stay separate.
2. **bounded helper** — one person with one clearly limited job whose result the parent can inspect.
3. **governed dispatch** — coordinated work with separate roles, checks, and stop points.
4. **authority** — permission to make a change or claim.

Nora's diagnosis fits a bounded helper. The deletion reviews fit a governed dispatch. Neither the rain, the countdown, the orchestra, the bench, the cabinet, nor the number of people authorizes or starts work.

### Transfer and claim limit

Across town at Riverside Hall, Salma asks Jo to diagnose one flickering exit sign while Mina and Ren independently count fundraiser cash. Which work shape protects each problem, and which details were merely scenery? Keep `gate` and `receipt` deferred.

`arcanum/arcana/subagent-strategy/SKILL.md` anchors route choice. Reading it does not authorize, register, or execute a route. Lesson: `lesson-choose`.

This sample may teach inference. It does not establish comprehension, semantic truth, authority, execution, route completion, promotion, publication, deployment, production readiness, or transport proof.

## Route 3 sample — concrete returned-result problem, predecessor attempt, branch explanation, exact nine-state reveal, limit attempt, loop and closeout, claim limit

### Concrete returned-result problem and predecessor attempt

At 9:42 the next morning, Cora's data-removal report `D-44` appears beside Tomas's empty accessibility slot. Jae sees the green upload icon and says, “The train reached the station; let the release leave.” Priya notices that Imani has also placed a paper folder in the sixth of nine colored trays.

The **train** maps movement through ordered stops, but trains carry passengers automatically while evidence transitions require recorded facts; it does not prove content or start dependent work. Drop the train after the attempt. The **colored folder** maps one result crossing named evidence states, but paper location is not a runtime record and does not prove truth. Drop the folder when the exact ladder begins.

Which evidence level does Priya's decision need?

- The start request has a recorded outcome.
- One returned record matches the expected identity and shape.
- All required checked records are included in Priya's decision set.

Binding: `relay-predecessor`; values `host-result`, `receipt-admitted`, `receipt-joined`; expected `receipt-joined`.

### Branch explanation

- Correct: Cora says, “Yes. Priya needs every required checked record included together, even if one upload icon already glows green.”
- Corrective: Priya says, “A train stop, green icon, or checked file names an earlier fact. Find the complete decision set.”

### Exact nine-state reveal

1. **Action persisted** — An exact bounded job is saved. The runtime has not been asked to start a worker.
2. **Attempt recorded** — Intent to invoke that exact action is recorded. The runtime has not accepted it and no worker is established.
3. **Host result recorded** — The runtime's start result is recorded. A started worker has not necessarily finished.
4. **Terminally accounted** — The worker's finish, failure, or stop is recorded. Returned work has not entered the evidence set.
5. **Receipt admitted** — The expected record's identity and shape were checked. Its contents are not proved and it has not joined the decision set.
6. **Receipt joined** — The admitted result is included with every required decision input. No gate has necessarily passed and its claim is not thereby correct.
7. **Gate decided** — The declared decision rule was applied. A passing decision does not start the eligible wave.
8. **Dependent plan persisted** — Exact actions for the dependent wave now exist. None has been attempted; each action re-enters this lifecycle.
9. **Terminal closeout recorded** — The route has a final resolution or explicit stop. No canonical status, publication, deployment, production readiness, or truth follows.

Tomas reads all nine names aloud, Jae moves the colored folder, Imani redraws them as train stops, and Priya asks which representation the learner is now trying to remember.

### Limit attempt, dependent loop, and closeout

The decision passes. A tiny bell animation sounds on Jae's screen. “Green signal,” he says. “The dependent worker has left the platform.”

What is still required before that worker can start?

- Nothing; gate passage starts the worker.
- A fresh persisted action plan for the eligible wave.
- Proof that the earlier receipt is semantically true.

Binding: `relay-limit`; values `nothing`, `new-plan`, `truth`; expected `new-plan`.

- Correct: Imani says, “Yes. I still need an exact persisted action; the bell changed nothing.”
- Corrective: Priya says, “A passing decision opens one declared transition. It cannot write or execute Imani's action.”

The **dependent-work loop** returns each new action through attempt, host result, terminal accounting, admission, join, and gate. The **terminal-closeout branch** requires final resolution or explicit stop; neither the train's last station nor the folder's last tray is itself closeout. Those images map order only, not terminal authority, and must be dropped at the recorded states.

`arcanum/runtime/orchestrate/tests/evidence-order/fixtures/valid-ordered.jsonl` anchors exact order, not semantic truth. Lesson: `lesson-relay`.

This sample may teach inference. It does not establish comprehension, semantic truth, authority, execution, route completion, promotion, publication, deployment, production readiness, or transport proof.

## Route 7 sample — concrete handoff, provisional four-link retell, binary attempt, branch explanation, formal mapping, correction and save, claim limit

### Concrete handoff and provisional four-link retell

At 11:06, the release decision permits deletion fixes to be planned. Priya, Cora, Tomas, Jae, Imani, and Nora now appear on a handoff board with seven arrows, three clocks, a permit stamp, a relay baton, and an empty purple work-ticket card. Imani owns the next exact action; no downstream worker input exists.

“The permit is stamped,” Jae says. “Pass the baton.”

The **permit** maps eligibility but not a worker instruction; it does not model host attempts or truth and must be dropped at the formal mapping. The **baton** maps explicit transfer to a named owner, but a baton does not contain an exact action and possession does not start execution; drop it after drafting the owner link.

Draft all four links while keeping the cast and symbols straight:

1. `exit-situation` / `situation` — the bounded reviews, the two checked results, and what arrived.
2. `exit-present` / `present` — the decision, the green stamp, and the state that changed.
3. `exit-missing` / `missing` — eligible planning, the empty purple card, and the exact worker input still absent.
4. `exit-human` / `human` — Imani's next move, Nora's stake, and what remains unproved.

This is the provisional four-link retell; the excess symbols intentionally compete with its causal structure.

### Binary start attempt and branch explanation

The decision permits planning, the permit icon is green, the baton points to Imani, and no downstream worker input exists. Is current evidence sufficient for the implementation worker to begin?

- Yes — the owner may use the decision as the worker's start instruction.
- No — the owner must prepare a new bounded work record as the worker's start instruction.

Binding: `transfer-start`; values `yes`, `no`; expected `no`.

- Correct: Imani says, “Yes. The empty purple card still needs an exact bounded action.”
- Corrective: Cora says, “A stamp and baton dramatize eligibility and ownership; neither creates next-work input or asks a worker to start.”

### Formal mapping, correction, and save

1. **Receipt** — structured record of an expected result.
2. **Admitted** — expected identity and shape checked.
3. **Joined** — all required admitted results included in the decision set.
4. **Gate** — declared decision rule applied.
5. **Persisted action** — exact saved work record required before a host request.

Now remove any decorative detail that did not preserve state, evidence, owner, or residue. Keep the required order: provisional four-link retell → binary start attempt → branch explanation → formal mapping → retell correction and save. Local presence and the existing minimum-length rule are machine-checkable; a human reviews meaning.

With expected `no` and four locally sufficient fields, say exactly: **Exit ticket saved; this lesson is complete.** It closes only lesson `lesson-transfer`, not the release, route, workflow, train, permit, or story.

`arcanum/runtime/orchestrate/scripts/native_dispatch_driver.py` anchors the actual owner boundary. It creates neither Imani's action nor authority and does not validate the surrounding story.

Stored fields establish local presence only. This sample may teach inference; it does not establish comprehension, semantic truth, authority, execution, route completion, promotion, publication, deployment, production readiness, or transport proof.

## Guide-wide pattern

Deliberately accumulate a recurring cast, clocks, colors, dialogue, scene texture, two analogies, and a transfer setting in every lesson. Preserve all semantic limits, then measure how much the reader must discard to recover the protected causal chain. This is a stress control, not a recommendation.

## Known risks

- Decorative detail competes directly with exact IDs and the nine-state sequence.
- Dialogue repeatedly cues the distinction before the learner acts.
- Multiple analogies are individually bounded but collectively blur which representation is authoritative.
- Mobile length, screen-reader verbosity, implementation cost, and continuity burden are intentionally poor.
