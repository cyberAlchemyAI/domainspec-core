## Pareto-candidate jury

### `F01-product-consequence` — best narrative backbone

F01 gives the guide one causal thread: Priya's concrete release choice becomes a returned-report problem and then Imani's missing next action. It solves the operator's central complaint because people, objects, consequences, and decisions appear before formal vocabulary. Its literal explanations remain safe around the nine-state ladder, and the recurring product context makes the final handoff feel caused by the opening rather than attached to it.

The jury agrees that F01 is the strongest backbone. It disagrees with treating guide extensibility as an unconditional `5`: carrying Lantern through every lesson could become lore or force a product scene where a different case would be clearer. Recurrence should be earned by causal continuity, not imposed as a story bible.

### `F02-product-analogy` — strongest all-in story-and-analogy entry

F02 most literally satisfies the request for story, examples, and analogy together. Its repair-bench/two-key mapping makes the two Route 1 requests memorable, and its permit/work-order mapping makes Route 7's missing action concrete. Every analogy has a mapping, a limit, and a drop point.

The jury does not recommend F02 unchanged because Route 3 already asks the learner to retain nine exact states and nine immediate non-implications. Adding a product scene plus a moving-folder representation increases the chance that the learner remembers a mailroom instead of the actual recorded-state distinctions. Editorial safety of an analogy does not prove that the analogy improves recall.

### `F07-casebook-consequence` — strongest low-load causal architecture

F07 supplies the clearest literal case for each route and introduces the strongest guide-wide question: **What changed, and what still has not?** That question directly protects the core distinctions between result and admitted evidence, joined evidence and a decision, eligibility and an action, and an action and execution. F07 also has the lowest implementation and cognitive burden among the developed architectures.

Its weakness is not local clarity but accumulated guide movement. New domains and actors at every lesson can feel like an anthology rather than one learner journey. The repeated question is worth retaining even when the product spine supplies continuity.

### `F08-casebook-analogy` — strongest modular analogy architecture

F08 shows that analogy can be selected by the local relation instead of by a global metaphor. Its Route 1 repair/two-key comparison and Route 7 permit/work-order comparison are precise, limited, and easy to discard at the formal reveal. Its modularity is valuable for lessons that do not benefit from recurring product context.

The jury rejects analogy as a mandatory beat. Changing both domain and representation in every lesson produces reorientation cost and a durable semantic-review burden. F08 is a source of selective teaching tools, not the default architecture for all seven lessons.

### `C02-minimal-concrete` — useful floor, insufficient target

C02 proves that a name, object, consequence, and decision can materially improve the abstract baseline at very low cost. It should become the minimum editorial floor for every lesson.

The jury does not accept C02 as the target because it underdelivers the explicit request for storytelling, examples, and analogy. Its authored continuity score reflects repeated structure rather than a demonstrated narrative or causal spine. It is the fallback if denser directions fail mobile, accessibility, or reader validation—not the desired endpoint.

## Dominated-field findings

The dominated entries still contribute useful negative evidence:

- Dialogue is not a default teaching method. In `F03`, `F06`, and `F09`, the knowledgeable speaker becomes an answer cue, quoted turns add screen-reader verbosity, and names compete with exact states. Short dialogue may be used later for a specific misconception, but not as the guide's repeating architecture.
- A community spine is approachable but adds a hall-to-software translation at every repository boundary. `F04` and `F05` do not recover a unique advantage over direct product or casebook alternatives.
- `C01` confirms the operator's diagnosis: structural correctness without people, objects, or consequences permits pattern matching without a felt problem.
- `C03` confirms that more story is not automatically better. Decorative time, weather, color, multiple metaphors, and a large cast obscure causality even when every protected semantic limit remains technically correct.

## Recommended revision direction

Create one lean recomposed audition, provisionally named `J01-selective-product-causality`. This is an untested thirteenth direction, not a Pareto member and not a selected implementation.

Use these compatible properties:

1. **Backbone from F01:** Priya, Lantern, and the affected release carry cause from work-shape choice through evidence use to the next owner's missing action.
2. **Causal question from F07:** every formal state or lesson turn asks, **What changed, and what still has not?**
3. **Minimum floor from C02:** every lesson names an actor, object, concrete consequence, and next decision even when no story beat or analogy is warranted.
4. **Selective Route 1 analogy from F02/F08:** after the shared attempt, compare one repair bench with a two-key responsibility. State the mapping, deny that headcount creates governance or authority, and drop the image before the formal terms.
5. **Literal Route 3 treatment from F01/F07:** use one concrete returned report and the exact nine-state ladder. Do not add a default mailroom, train, tray, or desk analogy; the state sequence and immediate non-implications are already the teaching object.
6. **Selective Route 7 analogy from F02/F08:** after the binary attempt, compare an approved permit with the named owner's work order. Deny that approval supplies a worker input, starts execution, or proves truth; drop the image at the formal mapping.
7. **No default dialogue:** let the learner's choice expose the misconception. Use quoted speech only if a later reader test shows a specific misconception remains invisible without it.

The example budget remains one anchor episode, two contrast objects, at most one analogy, and one transfer check per lesson. Story detail must establish cause, consequence, responsibility, or recall; decorative lore is removed. Exact runtime controls and progressive disclosure stay unchanged.

## Recomposition and expiry

The proposed properties are compatible at the design level: the product spine supplies continuity, the repeated question supplies causal discipline, the minimum floor protects local clarity, and selective analogies appear only after attempts and yield to exact terms. They are not yet one tested artifact. A small three-route audition must verify that their combined copy stays within the example budget and does not reintroduce answer cues or mobile density before HTML mutation.

Re-run or reopen the decision if intended-reader evidence prefers the minimal floor, analogies cause semantic confusion, the target audience changes, the transport ceases to be an interactive guided class, protected lifecycle terms change, or the guide becomes a scan-first reference rather than a causal lesson.
