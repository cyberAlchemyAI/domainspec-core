# Direct research — beginner orchestration language audition

- Date: 2026-08-20
- Status: concluded for language direction
- Recommended direction: Q movement with a bounded M lifecycle-map reuse
- Exact candidate ready to integrate unchanged: none

## Research question

Which of the three frozen audition strategies best reduces beginner terminology
friction across the Route 1 opening, Route 3 lifecycle tension, and Route 7
transfer reveal without weakening causal order, evidence-state boundaries,
authority limits, or claim limits?

This is a direct editorial study of the exact M, E, and Q candidates bound in
the candidate-set manifest. It combines a parent semantic review with one
read-only beginner-flow review performed without the parent's verdict. It is not
a governed Recovery 004 join or closeout, a Human Gate A decision, a rendered
surface test, or an intended-reader comprehension study.

## Conclusion

Use **Q as the voice and reader-movement direction**, then reuse **M's exact
nine-state lifecycle map after the learner's first attempt**. Do not select E as
the baseline. Do not integrate any candidate unchanged.

Q best follows the required progression: ordinary question, attempt,
explanation, formal names, and a changed next question. It keeps the visible
formal-term set to 4, 6, and 5 terms across the three proposed entry movements.
Its Route 7 four-link retell is the clearest transfer structure.

M supplies the strongest semantic teaching component: its Route 3 map names all
nine states in order and pairs each state with what still does not follow. That
component should follow, not precede, Q's plain checkpoint attempt. M is not a
viable whole-candidate winner as authored because its questions are often
preceded by wording that nearly or exactly states their answers, and its Route 7
feedback says an unqualified “route is complete.”

E's concrete examples are useful raw material, but E is not on the selection
frontier. It is the longest candidate in every moment, introduces `receipt` and
`gate` too early in Route 1, repeats conclusions before the learner acts, and
does not produce a semantic or interaction advantage unavailable from Q or M.

The beginner-flow reviewer applied the answer-leak gate more strictly than the
candidate self-checks: explaining a rule before a question can turn the question
into recall rather than diagnosis even when no option is visually marked. The
semantic review does not classify every taught-before-check fact as a hard leak,
because the M and E track definitions explicitly place explanation before
learner action. Both reviews nevertheless agree on the operational result: M
and E cannot be selected unchanged, while Q is the strongest movement direction.

## Moment decisions

### Route 1 — opening choice

Start from Q. Its question contrasts one inspectable result with genuinely
independent review before introducing `boundary`, `bounded helper`, `governed
dispatch`, and `authority`. Keep the scenario immediately under the question so
the opening does not become a cold quiz. Hide both feedback and formal names
until an attempt. Do not reuse E's early `receipt`/`gate` vocabulary or M's
phrase “needs one helper.”

### Route 3 — lifecycle tension

Use Q for the entry and first attempt, then show M's nine-state dual-label map.
This pairing supplies both movement and semantic completeness: Q establishes
the returned → checked → included → decided distinction in ordinary language;
M preserves action persistence, attempt, host result, terminal accounting,
admission, join, gate, dependent planning, closeout, and each non-implication.

Before reuse:

- shorten or balance the answer options so length is not a cue;
- avoid repeating `checked and included` verbatim in the correct option;
- move the upstream prerequisite catalogue into an expandable repository note;
- keep `terminal accounting` and `closeout` in the post-attempt map, not the
  opening;
- retain the exact dependent-work loop and the rule that gate passage is not
  execution or truth.

### Route 7 — transfer reveal

Start from Q's four-link retell. Add the smallest visible or explicitly inherited
premise needed to answer who owns the next move. Remove the lexical echo between
“no exact job file” and “exact saved job ticket,” while preserving the fact that
no persisted action exists. Keep explanation and formal mapping after the start
attempt. Replace any unqualified “route is complete” feedback with a precise
local state such as “Exit ticket saved; this lesson is complete.”

No audition fully satisfies the route brief's request to make the four-part
retell the opening movement. The integrated draft should test whether the retell
can precede the binary start check without weakening the stable question ID and
answer binding.

## What the research does and does not conclude

The research supports one language-direction recommendation for a bounded
integrated audition: Q movement, M lifecycle precision, and the repairs above.
It rejects using E as the baseline and rejects integrating any candidate
verbatim.

It does not establish that beginners understand the guide, that the blend fits
the rendered HTML, that conditional feedback behaves correctly, or that the
complete seven-route voice is coherent. Those require one common full-surface
candidate, rendered interaction checks, human voice approval, and intended-reader
sessions. It also does not close, repair, or supersede the registered Recovery
004 orchestration record.
