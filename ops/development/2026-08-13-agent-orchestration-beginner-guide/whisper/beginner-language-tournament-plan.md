# Beginner-language tournament plan for lesson routes 1–7

## Outcome

Run one governed Whisper tournament that gives every lesson route three
independent language candidates, selects a non-dominated finalist per route,
and integrates the seven selections into one review candidate. The work should
make the class easier to enter without deleting the distinctions it teaches.

Here, a **tournament** means several writers receive the same exact source and
constraints, draft independently, and are compared under the same scorecard. It
does not mean majority voting, choosing the shortest draft, or asking several
agents for interchangeable tone variants.

This plan does not authorize candidate dispatch, HTML mutation, promotion,
commit, push, or deployment. The currently deployed HTML remains the exact
baseline until the later gates in this plan pass.

## Why the intent becomes volatile

The current intent is recorded as frozen after a correction that added more
context to every lesson. The new correction says that the resulting class still
asks beginners to carry too many unfamiliar terms. These are two related
corrections to one transport, so Whisper's convergence guardrail makes the next
intent state `volatile`.

While it is volatile, the next run may update only the correction record, term
policy, surface map, composition plan, and smallest language audition. It must
not generate seven complete route rewrites or alter the HTML. Full candidate
fan-out begins only after the operator approves the audition and a new frozen
intent receipt binds that decision.

## Updated text intent substrate

| Core | Tournament intent |
| --- | --- |
| Resonance | Calm, capable, and conversational. The learner should feel that the system is careful for a reason, not ceremonial or mysteriously autonomous. |
| Relevance | The reader understands ordinary tasks, agents, files, simple JSON, and terminal commands, but should not be expected to know orchestration or governance vocabulary. |
| Trajectory | Show a concrete problem, explain the practical distinction, introduce the formal name, let the learner act, then state what the result still does not prove. |

The governing reader-movement rule is **need before name**. A formal term earns
its place only after the learner has encountered the problem or distinction it
helps name.

## Scope and invariants

### In scope

- Titles, introductions, context primers, scenario copy, answer labels,
  feedback, transitions, repository-anchor explanations, claim-limit wording,
  global learner-facing badges, and the reader glossary.
- The order in which a formal term first appears and the plain-language bridge
  that prepares it.
- Small structural changes inside a lesson when they reduce concept load, such
  as moving a term into an expandable repository detail.

### Out of scope

- Changing lesson IDs, question IDs, correct answers, storage schema, event
  handlers, fixture commands, public source paths, JSON identifiers, lifecycle
  order, or evidence claims.
- Adding live agent calls, shell execution, remote assets, analytics, network
  requests, private repository material, or recovery chronology.
- Treating shorter prose, passing browser checks, or scripted completion as
  evidence of human comprehension.

### Meaning invariants

Every candidate must preserve all of these:

1. One directly inspectable job usually needs one helper, not a team.
2. A coordinated dispatch is justified by real independence, isolation,
   parallelism, or dependency decisions—not by the desire to use more agents.
3. Work returned, a file existing, receipt admission, receipt join, and a gate
   decision are different states.
4. A gate can make later work eligible; it does not create, start, or prove that
   work.
5. An action is a saved work ticket. Saving it proves preparation, not
   execution or compliance.
6. Coordinator, driver, parent/host, and evidence validator retain their current
   public-code ownership boundaries.
7. Fail-closed recovery keeps the supported prefix and refuses to invent the
   missing cause.
8. Fixture validation and compilation do not start agents or prove production
   readiness.
9. The exit ticket is self-recorded and requires human review; non-empty text is
   not comprehension proof.

## Vocabulary policy

### Tiers

| Tier | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| T0 — ordinary language | Use freely: job, file, result, check, start, finish, permission, next step. |
| T1 — necessary formal term | Prepare the need, give a short plain meaning, then show the formal term consistently. |
| T2 — repository-depth term | Keep in code, an expandable repository anchor, or the final glossary unless the current exercise requires it. |
| T3 — authoring-only term | Remove from learner prose: lifecycle bookkeeping language, governance transcript mechanics, run identities, recovery history, and composition metadata. |

meaning.

Code identifiers such as `capability_ref`, `write_scope`,
`forbidden_write_scopes`, and `terminal_without_wait` remain exact. Each must be
paired with a visible plain-language label before the learner is asked to use
it.

Three cross-route consistency rules also apply:

- Use **course path** or **lesson route** for learner navigation and **dispatch
  route** for the runtime object. Do not ask one word, “route,” to carry both
  meanings without a qualifier.
- Reserve **worker finish**, **worker close**, **receipt admission**, **gate
  decision**, and **route closeout** for their exact states. Do not use “closed”
  as a generic synonym for “done.”
- Keep **evidence for one state**, **proof of one bounded claim**, and **whether
  the substantive claim is actually true** separate.

## Tournament design

### Candidate tracks

Three independent track owners are reused across dependency-ordered route
waves. Each track writes only the active routes in that wave. By the end, every
route has M, E, and Q candidates, but later lessons are not drafted against
vocabulary that earlier tournaments have not selected yet.

The route order is mandatory:

1. Routes 1–2 establish coordination and evidence-checkpoint language.
2. Route 3 freezes the lifecycle vocabulary used by later lessons.
3. Routes 4–6 may then run together against that frozen lifecycle ledger.
4. Route 7 runs last and must reuse the selected language from Routes 1–6.

| Track | Construction strategy | Required movement | Main risk to review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M — mechanism first | Begin with the practical distinction or missing step, then name it. | problem → mechanism → formal term → learner action → limit | Can become dry or procedural. |
| E — worked example first | Begin with one small, concrete work request or event history. | example → noticed tension → formal term → general rule → transfer | Can make one example look universal. |
| Q — diagnostic question first | Begin with one question the learner can answer from ordinary reasoning. | question → attempt → explanation → formal term → changed next question | Can leak the answer or feel quiz-first without enough context. |

A glossary-first track is not admitted. Earlier Whisper residue already showed
that it improves scanning while weakening the learning trajectory and
reintroducing catalogue risk.

### Candidate output contract

Each writer returns one pack for the active route wave with the frozen source
and term-policy digests. For every included route, the pack contains:

1. meaning atoms preserved;
2. revised title and one-sentence opening;
3. visible context primer;
4. formal-term reveal and first-use definitions;
5. repository-anchor introduction and “what this does not prove” statement;
6. scenario, question, answer labels, correct feedback, and incorrect feedback;
7. previous/next transition copy;
8. terms introduced, deferred, removed, and retained only as exact code;
9. answer-leak check;
10. word-count and new-term deltas from the source.

Writers produce structured Markdown or YAML candidate packs—not HTML. They may
write only inside the future tournament run directory and may not inspect one
another's outputs before all three packs are frozen.

## Review scorecard

### Hard gates

A route candidate is blocked if any answer is yes:

- Did it remove or reverse a meaning invariant?
- Did it use a formal term before creating a practical need for it?
- Did it reveal the correct answer in the primer or question wording?
- Did it treat admission, join, gate passage, execution, or truth as the same
  state?
- Did it change a code identifier, command, source path, question ID, correct
  answer, interaction contract, or claim ceiling?
- Did it add private material, live-operation instructions, or unsupported
  implementation claims?
- Can the route no longer make sense on its own with only the minimum premise
  it needs from earlier lessons?

### Pareto axes

Reviewers score each axis from 1–5 and provide direct block-level evidence.
Scores support comparison; they do not replace editorial judgment.

| Axis | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Resonance | Does the language sound calm, capable, and natural rather than bureaucratic or childish? |
| Beginner relevance | Can the assumed beginner understand why this distinction matters before learning its name? |
| Earned trajectory | Does each block change what the learner can understand or ask, thereby earning the next block? |
| Semantic fidelity | Are ownership, causal order, evidence state, and non-implications still exact? |
| Interaction fitness | Can the learner answer from visible lesson context without answer leakage? |
| Vocabulary economy | Are only necessary formal terms visible, consistently defined, and reused? |
| Density cost | Does the candidate fit the visual surface without replacing clarity with long prose? |
| Retellability | Can the learner restate input, change, immediate consequence, and later limit? |

The reducer selects from the non-dominated set across these axes. A numerical
total may break an otherwise stable tie only after the reviewers explain why no
lower-scoring hard constraint is being traded away.

## Executable checks and human judgments

### Executable checks

- Rebind all inputs and candidate outputs by SHA-256.
- Parse HTML and validate inline JavaScript syntax.
- Preserve exactly seven lesson IDs and current question IDs, answer values,
  `data-correct` values, timeline states, JSON field keys, and localStorage
  schema.
- Preserve fixture command blocks byte for byte unless a separately sourced
  code update is explicitly selected.
- Preserve seven public repository anchors and seven explicit claim limits.
- Reject remote references, network APIs, private terms, trailing whitespace,
  duplicate IDs, and broken accessible descriptions.
- Report new-formal-term counts, first-use positions, paragraph density, and
  exact duplicate prose as review signals.
- Run the current pointer, keyboard, details, persistence, stale-state recovery,
  reset, clipboard, mobile, zoom, lens, reduced-motion, request, and browser
  error checks against the exact candidate.

### Human editorial judgments

Automation cannot establish that a need was truly prepared, an analogy carries
the right relation, a passage earns the next, or a beginner understands the
lesson. Human reviewers must inspect:

- the first unearned concept in each route;
- whether a plain bridge is genuinely plainer than the formal term;
- whether simplification hides a causal or authority boundary;
- whether examples remain representative rather than becoming rules;
- whether feedback explains the distinction without introducing another stack
  of undefined nouns;
- whether winners from different tracks still sound like one class.

### Intended-reader sessions

For each route, a fresh beginner should be able to:

1. explain the practical problem without using the formal term;
2. recognize the formal term after it is introduced;
3. answer the route's scenario and explain why the other answer fails; and
4. retell what changed and what still does not follow.

At least three independent intended-reader sessions across the complete class
are recommended before making even a limited beginner-comprehension claim.
Three sessions are still a small qualitative sample, not proof of general
usability or teaching effectiveness.

## Definition of done

The tournament is complete only when:

- one exact source baseline and term policy are frozen;
- all three tracks return complete candidates for every route;
- every route has independent semantic and beginner-flow review;
- seven selection receipts preserve candidate provenance and disagreement;
- the integrated candidate passes protected-surface, structural, accessibility,
  browser, privacy, source, and claim-boundary validation;
- a human approves the integrated voice and language;
- learner observations remain separate from scripted browser evidence; and
- remaining uncertainty, including comprehension and transport proof, is named.

## Promotion and deployment boundary

The source HTML is already deployed. Any accepted language change creates a new
candidate with a new digest. Replacing `guided-class.html` or redeploying it
requires a fresh exact-byte inventory, validation, owner acceptance, scoped
deployment dry run, live hash check, browser run, preservation checks for the
other Cyberalchemize routes, and a new deployment receipt. No earlier approval
or receipt stretches over changed bytes.
