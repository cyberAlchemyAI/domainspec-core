## Agent 1 — repository-truth audit and project cards

## 1. Key Findings

- I accept Senge’s three-clinic correction. Fourteen three-minute pitches would consume 42 of 45 minutes before matching. All 14 cards should be asynchronous decision objects; the live session should open with a two-minute display orientation, run at most three rotating six-minute clinics, and reserve the remainder for contribution matching and receipts. The clinic shortlist must be chosen from declared interest, not curator preference.
- The shared browser artifact should store one truth model and expose two orchestrations: self-guided browsing and presenter sequencing. Separate copy would drift. Whisper’s own surface model already separates audience-facing, presenter-facing, and authoring-only content while preserving one intent substrate ([Whisper README](arcanum/spells/whisper/README.md):193-227).
- A card is not a résumé tile. Its minimum decision payload is: current witness, claim ceiling, bounded experiment, owner/contribution/effort/stop/check-in recruitment contract, and primary receipt. This matches the portfolio’s recurring claim-bounded pattern: executable witnesses in Poker/UI Studio/IDE Extension and explicit non-product ceilings in Body-War/Autoads/Tower/Sonar ([Poker README](projects/poker-agent-arena/README.md):9-24; [UI Studio README](arcanum/projects/ui-prototyping-studio/README.md):28-32; [Body-War README](projects/body-war/README.md):3-7; [Autoads README](projects/autoads/README.md):6-12; [Tower Product View](research/tower-system-taxonomy/PRODUCT-VIEW.md):101-119; [Sonar README](projects/sonar-loop/README.md):127-145).
- Veritasium-style misconception→reveal→instrument→boundary→zoom-out is useful only after adding **ask→offer→scope→receipt**: what help is needed, what someone can do, the effort ceiling/stop rule, and what returns to the group.
- Portfolio success should be measured by a **Collaboration Receipt**, not applause or page views: `project + participant + owner + contribution + effort_ceiling + stop_rule + artifact/result + claim_ceiling + check_in + outcome`.

## Agent 2 — participatory service and meeting design

## 1. Key Findings

- **[Revised inference] Solution-independent problem:** Capable makers with unevenly mature projects and useful skills lack a low-risk way to inspect one another’s work, perform one observable act together, and convert that act into a bounded next experiment with a one-week decision. The Co-Op Engine therefore should not present a field of “AI products.” Its honest shape is a maturity ladder: runnable bounded witnesses, governed prototypes, and research/spec bets. This agrees with the accepted portfolio substrate’s distinction among commercial wedges, demo surfaces, prototypes, and research, and with its prohibition on “finished platform” claims (`.arcanum/inventory/queries/2026-07-06-investor-bp-project-portfolio-research/findings.md:75-100`, `:614-645`).

- **[Revised candidate] The 45-minute meeting is a rotating act-first experiment clinic.** A project earns live time by supporting a source-verified observable act, not by having the strongest pitch.

  | Time | Participant action | Browser artifact state | Evidence produced |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0–4 | Host explains bring/offer/listen roles, maturity ladder, and claim ceilings | Room contract + three selected cards | participant role |
  | 4–8 | Silent scan: choose an act, not a favorite brand | cards grouped by `runnable witness / governed prototype / research bet` | act-interest signal |
  | 8–29 | Three × 7-minute clinics: 90-second problem/ceiling; 3-minute observable act; 90-second retell or evidence check; 1-minute ask | presenter mode on one project record | act receipt + comprehension residue |
  | 29–36 | Makers and contributors form pairs/triads around one witnessed friction or opportunity | asks/offers matching view | candidate match |
  | 36–42 | Define a seven-day experiment: contribution, owner, effort ceiling, stop rule, expected evidence | experiment composer | accepted experiment receipt |
  | 42–45 | Read back commitments and next-week review route | follow-up ledger | check-in date + decision owner |

  **Counterexample:** thirteen three-minute summaries consume 39 minutes without showing whether anyone can use or explain anything.  
  **Revision:** all projects remain asynchronously discoverable; exactly three enter the room, each through an observable act. Candidate first set is **Poker Agent Arena, UI Prototyping Studio, and IDE Extension**, but only after Alexander’s authority/source audit confirms their current witnesses and public wording. To avoid making the event appear developer-only, **Agentic Music Studio or TROQAR** should replace one lead when it has a verified, room-sized act such as authoring a musical rule or interpreting a running trace.

- **[Revised candidate] Participant roles are behavioral rather than status-based.**

  - **Project steward:** states the problem, current maturity, mapping limit, and one ask.
  - **Instrument operator:** guides the observable act without narrating a fake outcome.
  - **Contributor:** offers a bounded skill against friction observed during the act.
  - **Reteller:** explains what changed, what remains inference, and what they would try next.
  - **Listener/scout:** may contribute one question, analogy, or future match without pressure to commit.
  - **Receipt keeper:** records the act, evidence/inference boundary, match, experiment, and follow-up owner.

  **Counterexample:** calling everyone a “collaborator” hides the difference between watching, understanding, offering, and committing.  
  **Revision:** preserve these distinct transitions; listening is legitimate participation, while recruitment success requires an explicit accepted experiment.

- **[Revised candidate] The smallest useful project card is a maturity- and act-bounded decision record.**

  **Audience-visible fields:** `display_name`, `domain`, plain-language `problem`, `for_whom`, `maturity_class`, `what_exists_now`, `observable_act`, `what_the_act_can_show`, `mapping_limit`, `claim_ceiling`, `seven_day_experiment`, `skill_ask`, `recruitment_ask`, `next_check_in`.

  **Evidence/authoring fields:** canonical root, source selectors, witness type/date, evidence-versus-inference markers, owner, privacy class, forbidden claims, first-use term bridges, unresolved naming/authority conflicts, and verification status.

  **Machine fields:** stable project/act/experiment IDs, enumerated maturity and receipt states, source digests, and synchronization status.

  This follows the two-lane requirement that human and machine representations have named consumers and a drift-control obligation (`arcanum/disciplines/cards/two-lane-representation.md:27-35`). It also preserves the existing matrix pattern of proof status, missing witness, safe wording, and forbidden wording (`…/findings.md:75-100`).

  **Counterexample:** “IDE Extension—governed agentic development” flattens a host integration into generic AI-governance language and may imply a shipping plugin.  
  **Revision:** describe the concrete act and ceiling: for example, inspect or operate one route-card/workbench interaction, then ask the participant to identify the evidence and next owner. The accepted portfolio substrate explicitly limits related IDE claims to strategy/prototype language and requires a real host fixture (`…/findings.md:266-282`).

- **[Revised candidate] One browser artifact supports self-guided and presenter modes through shared truth plus different pacing.** Whisper requires separate projected copy, spoken copy, notes, interaction prompts, and authoring metadata, with only one visible thought at a time and an operator-approved opening/tension/reveal audition (`arcanum/spells/whisper/README.md:193-223`).

  - **Self-guided mode:** maturity ladder, cards, observable-act preview, evidence/inference toggles, claim ceiling, term bridges, recruitment ask, and asynchronous experiment request.
  - **Presenter mode:** one card and act state at a time, room prompt, timer, presenter-only notes, reveal sequencing, and receipt capture.
  - **Invariant:** mode may change disclosure and controls, never project facts, maturity, source bindings, or claim ceiling.
  - **Blocked state:** unresolved authority or name conflict displays as unresolved and prevents unqualified public copy.

  **Counterexample:** a dense truth card is unusable when projected; a sparse reveal is unintelligible when visited alone.  
  **Revision:** use responsive disclosure over one structured record, not a separate deck and website.

## 2. Gaps or Inconsistencies

- **[Blocking unresolved claims]** The UI Studio dual-root authority mismatch, Tower/MOGT narrative conflict, and display-name conflicts must remain visible record-level blockers. No synthesis should select affected public copy until a canonical root/name and mapping limit are owner-confirmed. The accepted substrate already warns that owned projects do not collapse into one authority surface and that private/public derivatives require explicit scrubbing (`…/findings.md:520-526`, `:550-563`).

- **[Revised candidate] First-clinic selection rule:** choose three projects only when each passes all six gates:

  1. canonical root and display name verified;
  2. public-safe claim ceiling approved;
  3. room-sized observable act exists;
  4. act produces evidence or a meaningful failure within three minutes;
  5. a concrete seven-day skill ask exists;
  6. the trio spans at least two participant domains and at least two maturity classes.

  Poker/UI Studio/IDE Extension are **candidate**, not confirmed, leads. If the trio fails domain breadth, substitute Music Studio or TROQAR only after equivalent verification.

- **[Revised candidate] Success receipt:** success is not attendance, applause, CTA clicks, or even a match. Use a nested receipt:

  ```text
  act_receipt:
    project_id, participant_role, observable_action,
    observed_result, evidence_or_inference, mapping_limit, retell
  experiment_receipt:
    contributor, contribution, owner, effort_ceiling,
    stop_rule, expected_witness, check_in
  follow_up_receipt:
    status [not_started|active|completed|stopped|inconclusive],
    evidence_link, owner_decision [continue|revise|stop|retest],
    lesson
  ```

  This preserves the portfolio’s receipt discipline: bounded artifact, owner decision, privacy/reuse boundary, and no claim that a receipt proves truth (`…/findings.md:38-48`, `:124-147`).

- **[Candidate] Veritasium inside the live transport:**

  - **Misconception:** “This is a gallery of finished AI products, and collaboration means joining one.”
  - **Dissonance:** the cards reveal different maturity levels, missing witnesses, and mapping limits.
  - **Reveal:** the unit of collaboration is one observable act and its next falsifiable experiment.
  - **Concrete instrument:** perform a project-specific act—play a hand, annotate a UI, operate a route card, retell an explanation, map a metric chain, or author a musical rule.
  - **Honest boundary:** the act can expose mechanics, understanding, or friction; it cannot establish market demand, generalized efficacy, or production readiness. The Craft teaching package’s browser suite proved mechanics/layout, not teaching effectiveness (`.arcanum/inventory/entries/craft-causal-class-total-refresh-review-strategy-result-synthesis.md:100-123`).
  - **Zoom-out:** inspectable acts plus owner-routed follow-up can turn a mixed portfolio into a learning system.

  **Recruitment extension:** Veritasium ends with an onward route but does not negotiate role fit, contribution size, consent, stop rules, or follow-up ownership (`arcanum/spells/whisper/presets/veritasium.md:46-69`, `:80-88`). Add `ask → offer → scope → receipt` after the explanatory arc.

- **Reader profiles remain distinct:**

  1. **Technical builder without framework context:** needs an executable or inspectable mechanism, interfaces, current witness, mapping limit, and exact build/test ask. Terms such as “receipt,” “promotion gate,” or “agentic” appear only after the concrete act creates a need for them.
  2. **Writer, musician, runner, or other domain practitioner building with AI:** needs a recognizable craft act, the location of human judgment, what the system can and cannot infer, and a contribution route that does not reduce domain expertise to prompt writing.

## 1. Key Findings

- **Meadows’s service boundary wins.** Discovery is not the service outcome. The relevant chain is: latent interest → legible need/offer → candidate pair → bounded proposal → mutual commitment → seven-day run → receipt → keep/revise/stop. Observed marketplaces support the early links, while implementation-intention evidence supports converting intent into a specified action. None establishes success without next-week execution evidence. ([GitHub, “Creating a Project Pod,” accessed 2026-07-25](https://docs.github.com/en/nonprofit/project-pods/creating-a-volunteering-project); [Gollwitzer & Sheeran, “Implementation Intentions and Goal Achievement,” 2006](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0065260106380021))

- **The opportunity must be the unit of recruitment, with a four-way decision.** GitHub Project Pods requires clear scope, impact, skills, timeframe, tasks, and deliverables; unclear goals or required skills trigger revision. Devpost makes missing roles and desired skills explicit. Co-Op should therefore ask each person to choose: join one named experiment; offer one bounded contribution; follow without committing; or explicitly decline. A project profile or “interested” reaction is insufficient. ([GitHub, “Project Pods FAQ,” accessed 2026-07-25](https://docs.github.com/en/nonprofit/project-pods/frequently-asked-questions); [Devpost, “How to find teammates,” published 2025, accessed 2026-07-25](https://help.devpost.team/article/247-how-to-find-teammates))

- **Exchange-first and clinic-first are distinct mechanisms.** A structured exchange lowers repeated explanation and preserves value for asynchronous or passive participants. A clinic supplies negotiation bandwidth for ambiguous, trust-sensitive cases. The hybrid is justified only when exchange signals allocate scarce clinic time—for example, multiple offers, conflicting interpretations, or a blocked trust/privacy question. Defaulting every project into a live clinic recreates the “more projects → more explanation → more spectatorship” loop.

- **Demo Day remains a poor default comparator for collaboration formation.** YC Demo Day broadcasts curated pitches to a prequalified investor/media audience of roughly 1,000, with an accompanying website for absent invitees. Its mechanism is attention plus investor follow-up, not bilateral collaborator commitment. Copying its polish without audience qualification, clear roles, or receipts risks applause being mistaken for action. ([Y Combinator, “Demo Day FAQ,” accessed 2026-07-25](https://www.ycombinator.com/demoday/faq?rd=1))

- **The control variables are part of the product.** Owner response time, real availability, public/private boundaries, reuse rights, next-week handling, and kill criteria determine whether a candidate match can become a safe experiment. They cannot remain facilitation details. Observed GitHub guidance explicitly requires responsive owners, communicates scope changes through the shared workspace, and preserves partial work for successor teams. ([GitHub, “Creating a Project Pod,” accessed 2026-07-25](https://docs.github.com/en/nonprofit/project-pods/creating-a-volunteering-project))

### Revised alternative: a gated **Seven-Day Experiment Exchange**

This is exchange-first. A clinic is invoked only from exchange evidence.

**1. Intake creates two compatible card types.**

An experiment card states: named problem/user; current artifact or evidence; one seven-day experiment; bounded contribution sought; useful skills/domain knowledge; owner response SLA; actual availability; success evidence; stop condition; and public/private/reuse fields.

A capacity card states: contribution offered; maximum time; relevant evidence; availability; learning interest; privacy limit; and whether the offer is public or facilitator-only.

Cards must avoid confidential prose in public fields. A private negotiation field may name sensitive context, access restrictions, or non-reusable artifacts. The experiment cannot enter matching until the owner declares what outputs may be retained, shared, or reused.

**2. Exchange signals allocate live attention.**

Participants browse and select exactly one response:

- join this named experiment;
- offer this bounded contribution;
- follow without committing;
- decline, optionally with a reason.

“Interested,” applause, page visits, and attendance remain engagement signals only. A clinic slot is allocated when cards show promising but unresolved complementarity: multiple bounded offers, disagreement about scope, missing domain translation, privacy/access negotiation, or an unclear success test. Cards with no legible offer return to revision rather than consuming presentation time.

**3. A clinic may produce a proposal, not a match.**

The clinic output is a bilateral proposal naming both people, experiment, artifact, timebox, access boundary, meeting time, owner response expectation, and receipt date. Either side can decline without penalty. A one-sided selection is `candidate_pair`, not `matched`.

**4. Mutual commitment requires reciprocal confirmation.**

Both parties confirm availability and the exact bounded action. The commitment uses a concrete implementation intention: if it is the agreed time/place, we will perform the named action on the named artifact and record the stated evidence. The classic 94-test meta-analysis reports a medium-to-large goal-attainment effect; a newer 642-test review finds stronger effects for contingent if-then plans, motivated participants, and rehearsal. Transfer to Co-Op is an inference requiring validation. ([Keller et al., “The when and how of planning,” 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563))

**5. The seven-day receipt is the minimum outcome evidence.**

Every bilateral proposal must resolve to:

- `ran`: action occurred; attach bounded evidence and learning;
- `not_started`: mutual commitment existed but action did not begin;
- `blocked`: name the control variable—owner response, availability, access, privacy/reuse, dependency, or scope;
- `declined`: one party declined before mutual commitment;
- `revised`: experiment was renegotiated with a new receipt date;
- `stopped`: kill criterion was met.

After the receipt, both parties choose `keep`, `revise`, or `stop`. Two meetings without a run receipt trigger `stop` or a fresh, smaller experiment; conversation cannot roll forward indefinitely as evidence of collaboration.

**6. Listener value is an explicit output.**

Followers receive a redacted card history: question, bounded experiment, decision, and permitted learning. They do not receive private fields or access by default. Their value is assessed through later useful actions—following a result, offering a relevant contribution, reusing an allowed pattern—not mere view count.

## AI Explanation Evidence Applied

Google PAIR advises starting from users’ existing mental models, explaining benefits rather than technology, onboarding in stages, clearly stating capabilities and limits, and offering low-risk reversible experimentation. It warns that anthropomorphic first-person conversational voice can create unrealistic expectations. Its explainability pattern recommends explaining for the current decision rather than completeness, with progressive disclosure. ([Google PAIR, “Mental Models,” accessed 2026-07-25](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/chapter/mental-models/); [Google PAIR, “Explainability + Trust,” accessed 2026-07-25](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/chapter/explainability-trust/); [Google PAIR, “Explain for understanding, not completeness,” accessed 2026-07-25](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/patterns))

Applied card order:

1. What changes for whom?
2. What bounded human contribution is requested?
3. One concrete input → system behavior → output example.
4. Where does a human decide or override?
5. What can fail or remain uncertain?
6. What data/access boundary applies?
7. What reversible experiment tests usefulness?
8. Only then: model, stack, or architecture detail.

“AI copilot,” “intelligent assistant,” and generic chatbot narration should not substitute for a causal account. Domain practitioners must be able to decide whether to contribute without decoding model jargon.

## 3. Gaps or Inconsistencies

- Operator and vendor sources demonstrate mechanisms, not comparative causal effectiveness.
- No searched study directly tests this complete seven-day boundary in a 45-minute mixed Co-Op setting.
- The right owner response SLA, receipt burden, and two-meeting kill threshold remain hypotheses.
- Public/private separation can protect participants while making cards too thin to match; the minimum safe disclosure requires testing.
- Explicit decline may improve denominator integrity but feel socially costly unless the interface normalizes it.
- Listener value has a proposed evidence model but no established baseline.
- A concierge may conceal weak card structure by manually rescuing matches; trials must record facilitator interventions.

## 4. Local Tensions

- **Legibility versus activation energy:** more mandatory fields reduce vague asks but may suppress early or uncertain projects.
- **Open participation versus evidence:** credentials can exclude capable adjacent practitioners; no evidence permits enthusiasm to masquerade as capacity.
- **Privacy versus reuse:** useful receipts may contain the very context that cannot be published.
- **Clinic access versus status:** signal-triggered clinics are defensible, but popular projects may monopolize attention unless blocked or neglected cards receive a separate route.
- **Project continuity versus kill discipline:** stopping after two meeting-only cycles protects the service from conversational drift but may terminate relationships whose value matures slowly.

## 5. Questions for Synthesis

1. Which fields are public, facilitator-only, bilateral after consent, or never retained?
2. What owner response SLA and weekly availability minimum admit an experiment?
3. Who validates that a contribution is genuinely bounded and executable within seven days?
4. Is explicit decline visible to the owner, facilitator-only, or aggregated?
5. How are followers notified and how is later listener value attributed without surveillance?
6. What receipt evidence is enough for `ran`, and who may inspect it?
7. When does a blocked experiment receive another clinic versus stop?
8. How will trials distinguish card quality from concierge rescue?

Dissent remaining: Meadows is right that exchange signals must gate clinic use and that success begins at reciprocal commitment and is proven only by next-week receipts. I still resist treating the exchange as sufficient infrastructure. Observed precedent suggests some valuable complements cannot be made legible before a short human conversation, especially across domain vocabularies. The clinic should therefore remain a governed exception triggered by evidence, not be eliminated. I also regard the two-meeting kill rule as a strong trial default, not yet a universal truth; `blocked` receipts should reveal whether certain access-heavy experiments need a different cadence.

## Agent 4 — systems reframing and feedback-loop design

## 1. Key Findings

- Christensen’s precedent evidence confirms the central reframing: the discoverable unit should be a bounded **opportunity/experiment**, not a presenter or portfolio item. GitHub Project Pods’ required fields—goal, scope, timeframe, repository/artifact, skills, tasks, deliverables, and owner response—supply an externally observed structure for what my earlier model derived internally. Locally, the same discipline appears in `goal`: routes name owner, technique, inputs, expected receipt, and gate, and completion remains auditable ([goal/README.md:84](/home/vrondelli/projects/domainspec-core/arcanum/spells/goal/README.md:84), [goal/README.md:89](/home/vrondelli/projects/domainspec-core/arcanum/spells/goal/README.md:89), [goal/README.md:146](/home/vrondelli/projects/domainspec-core/arcanum/spells/goal/README.md:146)). The Co-Op application remains a proposal, not a proven local format.

- The strongest family is now a precedent-informed **Experiment Exchange**: persistent experiment cards plus capacity cards, live energy selection, bounded clinics, bilateral appointments, and next-week receipts. This reconciles Open Space’s rapid energy discovery with Devpost-style persistence while explicitly controlling their known weaknesses: ephemeral follow-through, browsing cost, and stale asks. Demo Day remains a poor primary analogue because it optimizes legibility for a prequalified investment decision, not collaborator formation.

- An AI matcher is premature. Christensen’s talent-marketplace evidence shows that matching becomes useful only after structured opportunity and capacity data exists. The first system should expose deterministic filters and human-readable reasons—skill overlap, availability, desired learning, boundary compatibility—while collecting enough observed selections and refusals to learn whether recommendation is warranted.

- A commitment must specify when, where, and how, consistent with implementation-intention evidence, but that evidence does **not** establish Co-Op follow-through. Treat the bilateral appointment as an intermediate state; only the next-week receipt establishes whether collaboration started.

- The page remains one browser artifact, but it is no longer fundamentally a presentation. It becomes a **persistent exchange and live controller** with modes over shared records. Whisper supports this separation: its artifact lifecycle assigns owners and gates to states ([whisper/README.md:60](/home/vrondelli/projects/domainspec-core/arcanum/spells/whisper/README.md:60)), while its live contract separates projected, spoken, presenter, interaction, and authoring surfaces and blocks unearned jargon ([whisper/README.md:193](/home/vrondelli/projects/domainspec-core/arcanum/spells/whisper/README.md:193), [whisper/README.md:211](/home/vrondelli/projects/domainspec-core/arcanum/spells/whisper/README.md:211)). Google PAIR strengthens the design constraint: explain the user’s problem and mental model first, disclose AI capabilities and limits, stage complexity, and preserve reversible experimentation without anthropomorphic assistant language.

## 2. System Model

### Inputs

**Experiment card**

- plain-language problem and desired change;
- current witness or artifact;
- one uncertainty testable within seven days;
- scope and explicit non-scope;
- tasks or contribution options;
- needed skills/domain knowledge;
- expected deliverable or observation;
- owner and response deadline;
- repository/artifact link where safe;
- privacy, reuse, and AI capability/limit boundaries.

**Capacity card**

- skill or domain practice;
- contribution offered;
- desired learning;
- available time and appointment windows;
- AI comfort and preferred degree of assistance;
- prohibited work/data;
- modes: build, review, advise, observe, refer.

**Control inputs**

- 45-minute ceiling and mixed participation: bring a project, offer a skill, or listen ([dispatch-sheet.json:5](/home/vrondelli/projects/domainspec-core/ops/development/2026-07-25-co-op-engine-portfolio-research/dispatch-sheet.json:5));
- technical builders without internal-framework context and domain practitioners who want to build with AI ([dispatch-sheet.json:6](/home/vrondelli/projects/domainspec-core/ops/development/2026-07-25-co-op-engine-portfolio-research/dispatch-sheet.json:6));
- one browser artifact for self-guided and live use, with surfaces kept separate ([dispatch-sheet.json:6](/home/vrondelli/projects/domainspec-core/ops/development/2026-07-25-co-op-engine-portfolio-research/dispatch-sheet.json:6)).

### States and transitions

`draft → eligible → energy-marked → clinic-selected → candidate pairing → bilateral appointment → started/not-started → receipt → continue/revise/close/stale`

Transition gates:

1. **Draft → eligible:** owner, scope, seven-day uncertainty, contribution, boundary, and current witness are present.
2. **Eligible → energy-marked:** another participant marks `can help`, `want to learn`, `need clarification`, or `refer`.
3. **Energy-marked → clinic-selected:** live interest plus unresolved fit justifies scarce clinic time.
4. **Clinic → candidate pairing:** parties can name a plausible contribution.
5. **Pairing → appointment:** both accept a when/where/how commitment and stop condition.
6. **Appointment → receipt:** next week records action and evidence, including non-starts.
7. **No response → stale:** ask leaves active discovery after a declared expiry; owner must refresh it.

### 45-minute control loop

- **0–5:** orient around one decision: “Which seven-day experiment, if any, will you join or help?”
- **5–12:** silent scan of eligible experiment and capacity cards; participants place energy marks.
- **12–29:** two or three energy-selected clinics. Each resolves problem, capability/limit boundary, contribution, and smallest experiment—not project history.
- **29–39:** candidate pairs negotiate bilateral appointments: who, what, when, where, how, stop condition.
- **39–45:** public read-back of appointments and receipt dates; unresolved interest becomes follow/referral, not implied commitment.

### Bottlenecks and delays

- **Eligibility bottleneck:** owners may resist reducing projects to one uncertainty.
- **Attention bottleneck:** persistent cards reduce presentation time but can create Devpost-like browsing load.
- **Translation bottleneck:** technical labels can conceal the craft decision.
- **Trust bottleneck:** an ask without a witness or capability limit invites inflated expectations.
- **Bilateral bottleneck:** energy marks are cheap; mutual scope acceptance is scarce.
- **Feedback delay:** the week between appointment and receipt permits silent attrition.
- **Maintenance bottleneck:** persistent cards become misleading unless expiry is enforced.

### Failure loops and countermeasures

- More projects → more browsing → weaker selection → fewer appointments.  
  **Countermeasure:** eligibility gate, expiry, filters, and only energy-selected clinics.

- More polish → more spectatorship → less participant agency → perceived need for more polish.  
  **Countermeasure:** no project walkthrough by default; the live surface begins with available actions.

- Vague cards → poor pairings → failed starts → pressure for opaque AI matching.  
  **Countermeasure:** improve structured fields and observe human decisions before automating recommendations.

- Anthropomorphic AI language → inflated capability expectations → unsafe experiment → distrust.  
  **Countermeasure:** state task, capability, limit, human judgment, and reversible first action; never describe the interface as a teammate that “understands.”

- Only successful experiments return → matching rules learn from survivor bias.  
  **Countermeasure:** `not_started`, `declined`, `blocked`, and `inconclusive` are valid receipts.

- Stale asks remain visible → wasted contact attempts → lower exchange trust.  
  **Countermeasure:** response deadline, auto-stale state, explicit owner refresh.

### Success evidence and next-week receipt

Do not count page views, energy marks, clinic attendance, or appointments as completed recruitment. Measure:

- eligible-card-to-bilateral-appointment conversion;
- time from relevant discovery to appointment;
- domain/technical cross-role pairings;
- owner response time and stale-card rate;
- next-week receipt return rate;
- proportion started, blocked, declined, and completed;
- whether both audience profiles can retell invitation → experiment → contribution → later boundary, consistent with Whisper’s retell-chain criterion ([whisper/README.md:166](/home/vrondelli/projects/domainspec-core/arcanum/spells/whisper/README.md:166)).

Receipt:

`experiment_id; appointment_id; roles; promised contribution; when/where/how; observed action; witness; outcome={started, completed, blocked, declined, not_started, inconclusive}; reason; capability/limit learned; owner decision={continue, revise, close}; next ask; consent/publicity boundary`

The repository’s narrow-pilot precedent supports explicit evidence, owner, reuse boundary, and next plan ([PUBLIC-OFFERING.md:56](/home/vrondelli/projects/domainspec-core/projects/ontologize/PUBLIC-OFFERING.md:56), [PUBLIC-OFFERING.md:61](/home/vrondelli/projects/domainspec-core/projects/ontologize/PUBLIC-OFFERING.md:61)). A receipt is evidence of action, not proof that the product or matching mechanism works.

## 3. Alternative Families

### A. Experiment Exchange — recommended baseline

Persistent experiment and capacity cards are the core interaction. Human-readable filters aid discovery; live energy marks allocate clinic time; bilateral appointments close recruitment; receipts update persistent state.

Artifact roles:

- before: **self-guided recruiter**;
- live: **live controller**;
- after: **post-meeting record**;
- never primarily: pitch deck.

Minimum recruitment decision for either audience: `join`, `offer bounded help`, `follow/refer`, or `decline for named fit/boundary reason`.

For a technical builder, the card must expose artifact, interface or system boundary, missing witness, and concrete task. For a domain practitioner, it must expose the craft problem, the human judgment retained, what AI can and cannot do, and a contribution stated without framework jargon.

### B. Clinic-Only Cohort

A facilitator preselects two or three eligible experiments. Participants diagnose and scope them live; no open persistent marketplace exists beyond a short pre-read and final record.

Artifact roles:

- before: **pre-read**;
- live: **live controller**;
- after: **post-meeting record**.

This has lower browsing and maintenance cost and stronger facilitation. It is preferable for a small, recurring, high-trust group or when most cards are immature.

Weakness exposed by precedent: it sacrifices Project Pods/Devpost-style asynchronous discovery and privileges those available live. Preserve as a bounded comparison condition, not the default.

### C. Exchange without clinics

Cards, filters, and bilateral appointments operate asynchronously; the meeting is only a brief closeout.

This is genuinely distinct and may win when cards become mature. For the first iterations, however, it removes the Open-Space-like signal that reveals live energy and loses a venue for resolving cross-domain ambiguity. Keep as future work after observed card quality improves.

### Explicit eliminations

- **Portfolio gallery/demo day:** block as primary mechanism. Demo Day’s audience and decision are investment-oriented; legibility does not create bilateral collaborator scope.
- **Pure Open Space:** block. It reveals energy but lacks persistent opportunities, owner response, expiry, and receipt.
- **Devpost-like directory alone:** block. Persistence without expiry, bounded roles, or live prioritization creates browsing and stale-ask costs.
- **AI talent matcher:** block until structured opportunity/capacity data and observed match outcomes exist.
- **Unstructured networking or rotating project updates:** block because neither requires contribution, appointment, boundary, nor receipt.
- **Implementation-intention prompt alone:** analogy only. When/where/how may strengthen an appointment; it does not prove action.

## 4. Gaps or Inconsistencies

- No observed Co-Op baseline exists for room size, role ratio, card completion, appointments, or receipts.
- Project Pods establishes useful opportunity fields, not that this exact composite works in a 45-minute mixed-audience meeting.
- External sources support individual mechanisms; their combination remains unvalidated.
- “Listener” lacks a defined success outcome. Candidate actions are retell, referral, critique, or follow, but none is yet evidenced locally.
- Card expiry duration, clinic count, and minimum energy threshold are unknown.
- Seven days is a design constraint, not an evidenced optimum.
- The requirement for one page may encourage excess density unless live mode is a strict projection over shared state rather than the entire self-guided surface.
- Mental-model-first explanation requires actual audience research; PAIR guidance does not tell us these participants’ current models.
