# Whisper Result — Co-Op Project Showcase

- Transport: `browser_showcase_recruitment_page` extension
- Objective: turn fourteen unfinished projects into understandable,
  trustworthy choices and a next human conversation.
- Target public: technical builders and domain practitioners—especially
  writers, musicians, designers, researchers, players, educators, and
  operators—who want to work with AI and build their own things.
- Draft status: `pass` for local implementation.
- Publication status: not admitted.

## Text intent substrate

### Resonance core

Curious, candid, energetic, and consequential. The reader should feel invited
into real unfinished work, not recruited by hype. Forbidden feel: corporate
innovation theater, generic AI optimism, fake intimacy, or a job board.

### Relevance core

Readers know their craft but may not know the system vocabulary. Start from
felt problems and native acts—rewrite, listen, play, run, annotate, inspect,
teach, test—before naming technical mechanisms. Every ambitious vision sits
beside what exists and what does not.

### Trajectory core

1. Hook: projects rarely fail for lack of ideas; they become too hard to enter.
2. Reveal: the page makes unfinished work legible enough for one useful
   conversation.
3. Choice: begin from what you want to do or from the complete project map.
4. Opening: each project asks one memorable question.
5. Depth: vision → now → limit → next experiment → possible contribution.
6. Action: take a bounded offer to the existing 45-minute Co-Op meeting.
7. Ending: bring a skill, a question, or attention; a human decides what
   happens next.

## Candidate decision

- Project-index-first remains the complete control.
- Decision-lens-first remains the alternate entrance.
- Selected composition: dual entrance over one record spine. This does not
  claim either entrance is superior.

## Opening copy

Eyebrow: `THE CO-OP ENGINE · PROJECT MAP / LIVE PRESENTATION`

Headline:

> Projects do not need more spectators. They need the right next
> conversation.

Lead:

> Fourteen experiments across writing, music, games, tools, learning and
> trustworthy AI. See what exists, what is still a bet, and where your craft
> could matter.

Reveal:

> The hard part is rarely finding talented people. It is making unfinished
> work legible enough that someone can say, “I understand the next move—and I
> can help.”

Meeting boundary:

> The Co-Op Engine is the existing 45-minute weekly meeting. This page is its
> shared map: useful before the meeting, readable on a screen during it, and
> honest after everyone goes home. It does not match people automatically.

## Meta-narration sequence

- `Orienting`: turning the portfolio into one understandable promise.
- `Choosing`: letting the visitor begin from a craft or see the whole map.
- `Opening`: showing the project’s ambition beside its present proof.
- `Testing`: separating the next small experiment from a grand product claim.
- `Inviting`: turning interest into a non-binding human conversation.

> You do not need to join a team on the spot. Bring one project, one skill, one
> question, or simply your attention. A useful offer is small enough to
> discuss: “For [project], I could help test [uncertainty] by doing [bounded
> act]. Who should I speak with?”

Button labels:

- `Find a project by what I do`
- `See all fourteen projects`
- `Present this page`
- `Open the project`
- `Copy a contribution prompt`

Boundary:

> Possible contribution, not an open role. Confirm current welcome, scope, and
> ownership with a human at Co-Op.

## Validation

- Ambition is adjacent to present proof and limitation.
- Domain acts remain specific rather than becoming generic AI-assistant copy.
- First-use wording avoids or explains internal jargon.
- The page names who acts: visitor, facilitator, project owner, or human
  contributor.
- No phrase implies autonomous matching, inferred consent, delivery,
  commitment, or outcome.
- Meta-narration is functional and region-specific rather than decorative.
- Learning residue: the strongest shared voice is `curiosity + mechanism +
  honest boundary + bounded invitation`.
