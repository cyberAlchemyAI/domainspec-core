How I got here

I create systems that help people and AI agents preserve meaning as ideas become software. I began DomainSpec to make domain intent typed, connected, and testable—linking concepts and relationships to specifications, obligations, tests, observability, and implementation.

Building it through real project work changed the architecture. Feedback flowed upstream; failures became evidence; and unclear ownership became a design problem. The result grew beyond a specification framework into a way of keeping intent, execution, and evidence in contact without hiding uncertainty.

What I do

I turn complex domain intent into traceable structures and workflows that people and agents can inspect, test, and revise.

MODEL

Express domain meaning as typed concepts, relations, and obligations

CONNECT

Trace decisions through artifacts, implementation, and evidence

PROVE

Keep claims within tests, receipts, and bounded validation

LEARN

Preserve drift and failure so the next correction starts informed

What I am building now

With Victor Boscaro, I am developing CyberAlchemy v2, a collaborative synthesis of this work. It asks a deeper question than how to coordinate agents: how should knowledge become admissible, who may change shared truth, and what evidence earns the right to shape future work?

The architecture keeps responsibilities distinct. DomainSpec owns private typed intent. Arcanum owns the public, reusable methods for bounded work. Saturn names the private convergence loop intended to compare reality with intent. CyberAlchemy v2 is the private authority spine for vocabulary, source routing, owner routes, and promotion gates.

Today, the repository contains committed mechanisms, formal corrections, governance models, DomainSpec v2 work, and bounded prototypes. It is not a finished or deployed three-pillar platform. But it already expresses the principle that guides my work: make intent explicit, assign responsibility clearly, preserve what failed, and let proof set the ceiling.
