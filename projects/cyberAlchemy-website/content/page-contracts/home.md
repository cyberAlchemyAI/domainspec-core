that verifies implementation or operation.

## Allowed Claims

- CyberAlchemy is a method for turning vague intent and discovered evidence into
  governed artifacts.
- The method keeps objective, output artifact, discovery, tension, and route
  visible.
- The work should leave sources, decisions, limits, and next steps visible.
- The Home page may invite readers to explore the Method or How It Works.
- The Home page may invite a bounded pilot/demo only if phrased as an offer, not
  as proof of existing broad capability.

## Forbidden Claims

- CyberAlchemy is a shipped end-to-end runtime.
- CyberAlchemy is a universal product capability.
- CyberAlchemy is a security system or security guarantee.
- CyberAlchemy autonomously manages a company or replaces decision owners.
- CyberAlchemy proves all agent behavior.
- CyberAlchemy has a complete operational ontology runtime unless separately
  source-backed.
- Raw private/session/dispatch details, local paths, agent names, token budgets,
  private project names, or owner handoffs.
- HTML/PDF or analogy material as factual authority.
- Ontology-heavy or architecture-heavy first-screen explanation.
- "AI magic" or novelty-inflation language.

## Public-Boundary Notes

- Use public/canonical source anchors for factual claims.
- Translate private/session-derived strategy into public-safe language.
- Keep proof hints simple: sources, decisions, evidence, limits, next route.
- Do not cite private strategy/fusion/session files in public copy.
- Do not include repository-local paths in public copy.

## Required Body Parts

1. `opening`
   - Name the reader job in one plain sentence.
2. `orientation`
   - Explain CyberAlchemy in the smallest relevant frame: a
     capability-foundry method.
3. `evidence-backed body`
   - 2-4 sections:
     - problem or pressure
     - transformation
     - how the method keeps evidence/limits visible
     - where to go next
4. `boundary`
   - Say what is a method claim, what is an offer, and what is not being
     claimed.
5. `next action`
   - Route to Method or How It Works, with optional bounded pilot/demo CTA.
