## Synthesizer Result

The revised synthesis frames CyberAlchemy as a capability-foundry method: a
governed way to turn vague intent, scattered evidence, and system pressure into
useful artifacts.

Recommended site posture:

- Home is an offer page, not an architecture page.
- Public copy leads with problem, transformation, audience, proof hint, and CTA.
- Method, evidence, and claim discipline are stronger first-public claims than
  broad product/runtime claims.
- Architecture appears later, after value and trust are established.
- HTML/PDF artifacts are form evidence unless backed by canonical sources.
- Private/session material can guide strategy, but must be abstracted,
  anonymized, and re-anchored before publication.
- Site-wide trust rules must enforce authority type, claim type, source anchor,
  public-boundary check, and built/designed/planned/open status.

## Reviewer Returns

### Public-Boundary Gate

Verdict: `ACCEPT_WITH_EDITS`.

Required edits adopted:

- Home/capability foundry needs public-copy abstraction and re-anchoring.
- One-line offer is structure, not CyberAlchemy authority.
- Stages versus substrate is `Adapt`, not raw publish-now.
- Claim <= proof public wording must not cite private strategy/fusion files.
- Certified versus assumed becomes decision-boundary discipline.
- Structured critique loops must not expose dispatch mechanics.
- Private/project examples must be anonymized or explicitly approved.
- PDF facts are deferred until extraction and audit.

Sentence to preserve:

Public pages may use private/session/project material as strategy evidence, but
public copy must be re-anchored, abstracted, anonymized, and constrained by
claim <= proof.

### Claim-Proof Gate

Verdict: `ACCEPT_WITH_EDITS`.

Required edits adopted:

- Use "capability-foundry method," not shipped-product language.
- Publish-now verdicts must name authority type and claim type.
- `Built` means verified by owned source evidence as implemented or operating;
  documentation alone is not enough.
- Evidence/receipts are method discipline unless implementation is specifically
  anchored.
- Route grammar validates route shape and handoffs; it does not certify
  downstream execution.
- Inventory captures source-backed evidence; it does not prove promoted
  semantic authority.

Sentence to preserve:

CyberAlchemy may be published now as a method and trust discipline for
transforming intent into evidenced artifacts, but not as a shipped end-to-end
runtime, security system, or universal product capability unless each claim is
separately source-backed.

### Visitor-Clarity Gate

Verdict: `ACCEPT_WITH_EDITS`.

Required edits adopted:

- Homepage must be offer-first: problem, transformation, who it helps, proof
  hint, CTA.
- Architecture comes after offer, method, and trust.
- Use friendlier public nav such as `Trust` or `How Claims Are Checked`.
- Built/designed/planned/open and claim <= proof are site-wide trust labels,
  not necessarily standalone destinations.
- Residue needs a plain example before becoming an advanced concept.
- Every page needs a CTA or next read.

Sentence to preserve:

The first site should earn visitor trust by showing how messy intent becomes a
useful artifact, then revealing evidence, claim limits, and architecture only as
the visitor needs them.
