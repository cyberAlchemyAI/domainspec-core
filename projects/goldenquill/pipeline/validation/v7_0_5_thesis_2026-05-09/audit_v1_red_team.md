### Conclusion  

- **Blocking:** **true** – the amendment, as written, contains **high‑severity cross‑org leakage paths** that directly violate Law 3 (Org Isolation). These must be eliminated before the amendment can be locked.  
- **Verdict:** **ESCALATE**  

  The high‑severity findings (RT‑01, RT‑02, RT‑03) expose **P0** architectural defects that cannot be patched merely by superficial wording; they require **runtime enforcement guarantees** (namespace‑scoped vector stores, mandatory pre‑checks, continuous integrity verification). Implementing those controls changes the underlying execution model of Scout, Logician, and the vector‑store wrapper, which goes beyond “text‑level fixes”. Consequently the amendment must be **escalated** to the architecture council for a redesign of the isolation mechanisms before it proceeds to the Thesis round.  

--- 

**Summary of required architectural actions** (to guide the redesign):  

1. **Hard‑enforced org‑namespace isolation** for *all* persistent stores (FAISS/Chroma vectors, file‑system paths, Pydantic‑AI tools).  
2. **Logician must raise and abort** on any cross‑org path before any I/O, not just log a failure code.  
3. **Per‑read hash verification** for every Funder Intel document; make staleness evaluation part of the read pipeline.  
4. **Operator‑signed consent‑tier mutation** with immutable audit trail.  
5. **Sanitization of cross‑reference pointers** in Channel G to prevent indirect leakage.  

Addressing these points will close the P0 breach surfaces and bring the amendment into compliance with the Five Operating Laws and the Cross‑Family Rigor mandate.
