# GoldenQuill Promotion Governance

## Executive Summary

GoldenQuill should treat grant writing as a traceable learning system, not only
as a document-production process. A grant run produces valuable signals before
and after submission: discovery quality, eligibility fit, gate results, reviewer
feedback, award or decline notices, partial awards, reporting performance, and
relationship movement.

The proposed promotion governance layer lets GoldenQuill learn from those
signals without turning a draft, dashboard number, staff memory, or single
review comment into reusable authority too early.

## Core Idea

Every grant run should be represented as a clear chain:

```text
grant run
  -> source-backed evidence
  -> measured outcome or learning signal
  -> reviewable learning candidate
  -> privacy and governance review
  -> owner decision
  -> approved reuse, rejection, retirement, contradiction, or private residue
```

This creates a disciplined way to answer:

- What happened in the grant run?
- In what order did it happen?
- Which review gate allowed or blocked the next step?
- What evidence supports the outcome?
- What can be learned from the outcome?
- What may be reused safely?
- What must stay private or unapproved?

## Why This Matters

Grant results are noisy. A win does not prove every proposal claim was strong.
A rejection does not prove the writing failed. A portal status does not prove a
final agency decision. Reviewer feedback may be useful, but it can also be
private, context-specific, or incomplete.

GoldenQuill needs a way to learn from real-world evidence while preserving
source truth, privacy boundaries, and human accountability.

## Recommended Positioning

For the client, this is best framed as:

> GoldenQuill will not merely help write grants. It will help the organization
> build a governed learning loop around grant strategy, proposal quality,
> funder fit, evidence gaps, and post-submission outcomes.

## Expected Benefits

- Better tracking of how far each grant actually progressed.
- More useful learning from wins, losses, partial awards, and reviewer comments.
- Safer reuse of prior grant lessons.
- Stronger separation between private client feedback and reusable general
  knowledge.
- More meaningful KPIs than win rate alone.
- Clearer accountability for what is approved, rejected, retired, or contradicted.
