## Executive Summary

The Decentralized Living Library of Fungi package presents a governed,
multi-node fungal biodiversity and applied mycology network. Its core thesis is
that the strongest grant strategy is not a single farm, festival, lab, or
commercial mushroom brand, but a distributed public-benefit infrastructure for
preserving, verifying, studying, and making useful culturally and ecologically
important fungi.

The package connects four main capacities:

- Pennsylvania public/co-op/culture capacity through William Padilla-Brown,
  MycoSymbiotics, MycoSymbiotics.coop, MycoFest, grower education, cultivation,
  workshops, and public legitimacy.
- Field-methods and soil microbiome capacity through Dietrich Epp Schmidt,
  focused on mycorrhizal ecology, regenerative agroforestry, field protocols,
  data discipline, and conservation-practice evidence.
- A prospective Houston Research Node through Dr. Venkatesh Balan and the
  University of Houston, framed around fungal systems research, bioprocess
  questions, lab methods, student projects, comparative protocols, and sponsored
  research.
- Funding and operating-system capacity through Josh Inklovich, FIT Farms, and
  Golden Quill/Tilth, including funder matching, proposal production, evidence
  vaulting, partner memory, routing discipline, and quality control.

The fundable opportunity is to build a living fungal library with accession
governance, DNA-vouchering, public education, field evidence, research-node
collaboration, and durable financial support. The package is strongest when it
is presented as infrastructure for biodiversity, biological collections,
soil-health research, specialty crop development, public science, and long-term
stewardship.

## Core Project Lanes

The project is split into seven fundable lanes:

1. Core living library / accession archive
   - Sequence-vouchered cultivated and specialist fungal collection.
   - Needs governance, access policy, redundancy, database capacity, and public
     collection logic.
   - First ask: `$150K` bridge; `$450K` three-year curator/database/backup ask.

2. AMF / INVAM-adjacent support
   - Permission-safe support concept for AMF stewardship, public awareness,
     vouchering, and backup capacity.
   - Must remain concept-only unless KU/INVAM authorization exists.

3. Soil microbiome + agroforestry field network
   - Sentinel plots, mycorrhizal data, truffle/tree systems, conservation
     practice evidence, and field protocols.
   - First ask: `$50K-$300K` field protocol/demo package.

4. Mushroom specialty crop + co-op
   - Grower aggregation, education, value-added product infrastructure, market
     development, and cooperative benefit.
   - First ask: `$100K-$500K` local food/co-op implementation.

5. Houston Research Node / fungal systems research
   - University-adjacent research route for fungal cultivation, bioprocess
     questions, lab methods, student projects, comparative protocols, and
     sponsored research.
   - First ask: `$100K-$750K` Houston research-node pilot.
   - Important correction: this is not an automation-first lane. Automation,
     sensors, or controlled-environment systems are optional tools only when
     they serve fungal systems research.

6. MycoFest public science
   - Accession desk, education, storytelling, donor campaign, youth/community
     science, and sponsor visibility.
   - First ask: `$25K-$150K` public-science and campaign package.

7. Endowment / donor circle
   - Permanent support for curation, host cultures, database, insurance,
     student fellows, and long-term stewardship.
   - First ask: `$1M` initial target; `$5M` durability target.

## Entity And Partner Routing

The project depends on keeping lanes clean:

- Public collection assets need a mission-locked home, such as a nonprofit,
  fiscal sponsor, public-benefit cooperative program, university route, or
  similarly governed structure.
- MycoSymbiotics private IP, formulas, commercial relationships, and product
  methods should stay in a private or licensed-services lane unless
  intentionally contributed.
- Co-op grants should fund member benefit, shared infrastructure, field
  demonstrations, education, and market development.
- UH/Balan work should move through confirmed university or sponsored-research
  structures with clear data, publication, student, and IP terms.
- Josh/FIT Farms/Golden Quill can operate as the funding architecture and
  integration layer, but partner commitments must not be represented before
  written confirmation.
- KU/INVAM can be referenced only as factual public context or as an authorized
  route if written permission exists.

## Non-Negotiable Boundaries

The package repeatedly flags these limits:

- Do not imply KU/INVAM partnership, strain access, MTA, services, or name-use
  authorization.
- Do not claim any partner is committed without written confirmation.
- Do not frame Dr. Balan or the University of Houston as an automation module;
  frame them as the Houston Research Node.
- Do not commingle public collection assets with private MycoSymbiotics,
  FIT Farms, or co-op revenue assets.
- Do not make health, ecological miracle, endorsement, or partner-status claims
  without scientific, legal, and relationship review.
- Do not send a broad spreadsheet to funders before selecting a clean ask,
  entity route, owner, and go/no-go status.
