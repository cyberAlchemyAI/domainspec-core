## Agent 1 — Sunstein, Cass

# Brazilian Legal-Doctrine Issue Map — 16 July 2026

## Status key and boundary

- **[B] Binding authority:** enacted legislation or regulation.
- **[R] Regulator interpretation:** current official administrative guidance.
- **[I] Defensible inference:** application to this proposed fact pattern.
- **[U] Unknown:** no located authority decides this exact model.
- **[Q] Counsel question:** decision required before taking money.

This is primary-source issue spotting, not a conclusion that the product is legal or illegal.

## Central classification tension

**[B]** The Civil Code recognizes aleatory contracts involving future things or existing things exposed to risk (arts. 458–461). But that private-law category does not displace consumer, promotion, lottery, gambling, payment, or securities rules.

**[B]** The Contraventions Law defines a game of chance as one where gain and loss depend mainly on luck (art. 50), and “lottery” broadly as an operation using tickets, symbols, or analogous means where obtaining money or goods depends on a draw (art. 51). The STF has not resolved Theme 924 on the constitutional reception of art. 50; as of 16 July 2026, the case had been removed from an April calendar and was listed for August 2026.

**[R]** SPA describes commercial promotion as the **free** distribution of prizes for advertising. Its authorized modalities do not naturally match the proposed paid instant RNG: an “operation analogous to vale-brinde” is instant but may not select the winner randomly; a promotional draw must remain linked to federal lottery results.

**[I]** Therefore, “every buyer receives a card,” finite pre-owned inventory, and no cash-out support an aleatory-sale argument, but do not settle it. Charging specifically for the randomized allocation also prevents assuming the safer “free promotional prize” route.

**[Q]** Brazilian consumer, regulatory, and criminal counsel must classify the exact operational witness—not the label “pack,” “NFT,” or “sale”—against ordinary aleatory sale, commercial promotion, unauthorized lottery/game of chance, and fixed-odds betting.

## Issue map beyond classification

### Consumer and marketplace responsibility

**[B]** Offers bind; omitted material facts can mislead; service and product defects can create strict/solidary liability. Odds, remaining inventory, price, all fees, shipping, grade/condition, valuation basis, title, redemption deadlines, and remedies are material.

**[R]** STJ REsp 1.836.349 exempted a platform acting only as classifieds, without transaction intermediation, while expressly distinguishing true intermediaries. **[I]** This platform’s proposed RNG, payment, approval, and fulfillment functions point away from that exemption.

### Payment, virtual assets, and AML/KYC

**[B]** Law 12.865 defines payment accounts and electronic money. No internal balance reduces this perimeter, but checkout and creator settlement must use an authorized provider. If the NFT is a covered virtual asset, Law 14.478, Law 9.613, and Resolution 520 bring authorization, customer identification, transaction monitoring, governance, segregation, and reporting requirements. **[U]** If the claim is excluded product access, platform statutory KYC duties require separate analysis; the PSP will still impose onboarding/fraud controls.

### Tax, fiscal documents, and invoicing

**[B]** LC 214/2025, as amended in 2026, defines a digital platform by control over collection, payment, terms, or delivery and provides platform information, document, split-payment, and potential IBS/CBS responsibility rules. Covered virtual-asset services receive specific IBS/CBS treatment. Receita’s DeCripto surface was current on 16 April 2026. SPA’s current guidance requires 20% prize withholding for authorized commercial promotions and proof of prize ownership.

**[Q]** Fix whether each amount is sale revenue, marketplace commission, custody/shipping service, promotional prize, or virtual-asset service; identify invoice issuer, tax domicile, title-transfer time, creator payout treatment, and whether DeCripto applies.

### Jurisdiction

**[B]** CDC art. 101 permits supplier-liability proceedings at the consumer’s domicile. Law 15.211 applies to relevant technology products/services directed to or probably accessed by minors in Brazil regardless of provider location. LCP art. 2 applies to contraventions practiced in Brazil. Cross-border NFT transfers may implicate BCB Resolution 521. **[Q]** Foreign creators, foreign custodian, offshore contracts, self-custodied wallets, imports, and delivery to multiple states/countries each need a separate launch territory analysis.

## Hard-stop conditions

1. No payment until counsel delivers a written classification for the complete transaction and failure paths.
2. If commercial-promotion treatment applies, do not launch before SPA authorization and confirmation that the RNG fits an allowed modality. Current SPA rules also impose legal-entity eligibility, prize-ownership proof, a 5% operational-revenue prize ceiling, no cash conversion, and rejection of plans that stimulate gambling.
3. No “opened packs are non-refundable” rule until counsel specifies CDC art. 49 treatment before and after allocation, reveal, shipment, and delivery.
4. No transferable NFT, wallet custody, NFT marketplace, or external transfers until BCB/CVM perimeter opinions and any licensed-provider route are recorded.
5. No money-like balance, reusable credit, cash-out, or buyback without payment counsel and written PSP approval.
6. No creator listing before identity, legal capacity, title, import/fiscal provenance, authenticity, custody allocation, invoice role, and tax domicile are verified.
7. No minor-access launch or collectible-targeted advertising until Law 15.211/Decree 12.880 applicability, age assurance, and advertising treatment are approved.
8. No multi-jurisdiction launch; begin only in the territory expressly covered by the opinions and PSP/custodian contracts.

## Exact counsel and PSP brief

Counsel must answer, with assumptions and change triggers:

1. What does the buyer purchase at checkout: a specific card, a claim, or a chance?
2. Is receiving some card sufficient to avoid “gain/loss,” “prize,” or “lottery” treatment?

5. Who is seller, supplier, promoter, merchant of record, token issuer, custodian principal, invoice issuer, and redemption obligor?

7. What refunds and reversals apply at each state, especially under CDC art. 49?
8. Does each token variant fall under Law 14.478/Resolution 520, the product-access exclusion, or CVM jurisdiction?
9. What AML/KYC, DeCripto, invoice, withholding, IBS/CBS, state/local tax, and import duties apply?
10. Which age gate, marketing restrictions, forum, governing law, and allowed territory are required?

The evidence packet should include UI copy, terms, complete money flow, title/custody flow, token rights and transfer controls, RNG lifecycle, refund/chargeback paths, creator/platform contracts, invoice examples, and one successful and one failed-delivery witness.

## Agent 2 — Acemoglu, Daron

# Explorer Report — Adversarial Transaction-Scenario Model

## 1. Status legend and central finding

- **[O] Obligation/authority:** supported by an official source.
- **[I] Inference:** application to this proposed product.
- **[U] Unknown:** facts or regulator interpretation not yet established.
- **[C] Counsel/authority decision:** requires a written professional or regulator determination.

**Central finding:** “card-only settlement” does not create a safe harbor. A buyer still risks money for an outcome selected principally by chance, and the physical card has economic value. The strongest ordinary-sale candidate is a free, non-binding allocation revealed **before** payment, followed by an identified-card purchase. Charging or reserving buyer funds before randomness remains materially closer to a paid randomized transaction.

“No money ever leaves the system” is not viable as an absolute rule. Online withdrawal, failed delivery, defects, refused performance, fraud, and chargebacks can require monetary reversal or refund. CDC arts. 18 and 35 include restitution among consumer remedies; art. 49 requires return of amounts paid when withdrawal applies. **[O/C]**

## 2. Fact-pattern decision tree

1. **Does money, credit, a deposit, fee, or other economic commitment occur before the card is fixed?**
   - **Yes:** consideration precedes chance. Continue to 2.
   - **No:** if allocation is free, immediately revealed, non-binding, and rejectable without fee, penalty, lost credit, or suppressed future access, the later sale is for an identified card. **[I/C]**

2. **Is randomness merely an advertising bonus attached to another genuine purchase?**
   - **Yes:** this becomes more promotion-like. SPA defines commercial promotion as free prize distribution to increase sales and lists sorteio, vale-brinde, concurso and analogous forms; authorization is exclusive to SPA. **[O/C]**
   - **No; the uncertain card is the purchased object:** candidate aleatory sale, but not automatically lawful. Civil Code arts. 458–461 recognize aleatory contracts; that does not displace gambling, promotion, consumer, payment, or betting law. **[O/I/C]**

3. **Does value gain/loss depend principally on RNG?**
   - **Yes:** Decree-Law 3.688/1941 art. 50 defines a game of chance by gain/loss depending exclusively or principally on chance. Law 14.790/2023 defines an “aposta” as placing value at risk expecting a prize, and online game as an RNG-determined future event. The pack does not neatly satisfy every fixed-odds feature, but the overlap requires written classification. **[O/C]**
   - Receiving some card does not answer this where card values differ materially. **[I]**

4. **What does the token do?**
   - **Non-transferable exact-card claim:** stronger candidate for Law 14.478 art. 3(III)’s exclusion for instruments granting access to specified products/services. **[I/C]**
   - **Transferable NFT/public wallet/secondary-market fees or appreciation marketing:** increases virtual-asset and CVM analysis. Law 14.478 regulates transferable digital value used for payment/investment and services including transfer/custody; BCB Resolution 520/2025 now regulates authorized VASPs. CVM says asset-backed tokens/NFTs may or may not be securities depending on rights and collective-investment features. **[O/C]**

5. **Who controls the transaction in substance?**
   - If platform sets rules/price, runs RNG, approves inventory, takes checkout, controls claims, handles disputes or delivery, “creator is seller” is not sufficient to remove platform supplier exposure. **[I/C]**
   - A pure listings/search provider with no intermediation has narrower STJ exposure; this product is materially more controlled than that precedent. **[O/I]**

6. **Who owns the exact card, and when does buyer title arise?**
   - Civil Code art. 481 makes sale an obligation to transfer ownership; arts. 1,226/1,267 generally require tradition for movable-property rights, including constructive forms where a third-party holder owes restitution. NFT transfer alone does not establish physical title. **[O/I/C]**
   - Custody, title, redemption right, possession, grade and authenticity must be separate fields.

## 4. Money-flow disagreement: nominal roles versus reality

The contract label must be reconciled with these ledgers:

```text
buyer authorization → PSP/acquirer → legal merchant
→ platform fee → creator/provider payout
→ tax/invoice → custodian/carrier payment
→ refund/withdrawal/chargeback/failed-delivery reversal
```

BCB’s marketplace FAQ distinguishes: a marketplace receiving and repassing payments to sellers after extracting remuneration can be a **subcredenciador**; where the buyer’s legal relationship is directly with the platform, the platform is the final receiving merchant. **[O]**

Points likely to defeat nominal roles:

- creator is paid before delivery or expiry of withdrawal/chargeback exposure;
- platform determines the buyer refund but creator contract allocates the loss elsewhere;
- PSP identifies platform, while invoice/terms identify creator;
- custodian cannot refund, yet terms call it redemption obligor;
- NFT is transferred externally while platform retains freeze/burn/redemption control;
- chargeback occurs after the card/NFT has been transferred;
- failed delivery leaves neither money reserve nor live claim;
- “no cash-out” is confused with legally required refund.

These are precisely where settlement evidence may contradict the nominal transaction model.

## Agent 3 — Simon, Herbert

# Simon Explorer Report — Payment Architecture and Fund Flow

**Currentness:** 16 July 2026.<br>
**Scope:** Brazilian payment architecture issue-spotting, not legal or tax advice.

## Executive determination

“No customer cash-out” is a useful boundary, but “money never leaves the system” is false. Money must leave through processor fees, creator/provider payouts, platform revenue, taxes, custody, insurance, shipping, refunds, and chargebacks.

Two coherent architectures exist:

1. **Platform-as-seller:** the buyer contracts directly with the platform; the platform owns or assumes the card obligation and pays creators/custodian/carrier as vendors.
2. **Marketplace:** the buyer contracts with an identified creator/seller; a provider-approved marketplace payment product onboards sellers and performs settlement/split payments.

Reject the hybrid in which terms call the creator the seller while the platform receives all customer money, controls the descriptor/refunds, and later repays creators from an ordinary bank account. Banco Central says a marketplace that receives and passes payments to sellers after deducting its fee may act as a **subcredenciador**; conversely, when the payer’s legal relationship is directly with the platform, the platform is the receiving end user even if partners fulfill the product. Current rules require subcredenciadores acting as receiver or payer to participate in centralized settlement. Since December 2025, BCB rules also require closure of accounts used for unauthorized payment services or “contas-bolsão.”

## Evidence labels

- **[R]** statutory or regulator rule.
- **[N]** network or PSP policy.
- **[I]** product/solvency inference.
- **[U]** unresolved counsel or provider question.

## Fund-flow candidates

### A. Platform is the seller

```text
Buyer
  → licensed acquirer/PSP authorizes payment
  → PSP settles the platform receivable
  → provider fee + agreed risk reserve are retained
  → platform business account receives available balance
  → platform pays creator/vendor, custodian, carrier, insurer and taxes

Refund: platform → PSP → original payment instrument
Chargeback: issuer/network → acquirer/PSP debits platform
Physical redemption: custodian → carrier → buyer
```

- **[R]** This avoids subcredenciamento only if the legal and factual buyer relationship is genuinely with the platform.
- **[I]** Platform should acquire/secure title and custody before sale and become the clear delivery/remedy obligor.
- **[I]** Creator payment is procurement/vendor expense, not withdrawal of customer wallet funds.
- **[U]** Tax counsel must determine when title and taxable supply occur and what fiscal document describes the randomized allocation.

### B. Creator is the seller; platform is marketplace

```text
Buyer
  → PSP-hosted seller/marketplace checkout
  → acquirer and centralized settlement
  → creator connected account receives approved net amount
  → platform receives disclosed fee
  → provider/contractual reserve remains available for reversals

Refund/chargeback:
  → charged account is debited
  → platform backstop applies if the PSP contract assigns negative-balance risk
```

- **[R]** Receiving and repassing money to multiple sellers can make the platform a subcredenciador.
- **[N]** In Stripe’s example, direct charges place the charge and initial dispute on the connected seller; destination/separate charges place refunds, disputes, fees, and negative-balance exposure on the platform.
- **[I]** A provider-controlled connected-account flow is safer than a pooled platform bank account.
- **[U]** Consumer/platform liability still depends on the platform’s actual role in pack approval, randomness, custody, disclosure, and remedy—not only the contract label.

|---|---|
| Checkout | **[R]** CDC arts. 30–31 and Decree 7.962 require the real supplier, total price/fees, material terms, fulfillment, and cancellation path to be clear before acceptance. **[N]** PSP must underwrite the actual pack, jackpot, custody, and NFT flow. |
| Authorization | **[N]** Visa’s April 2026 rules have specific NFT transaction coding and enhanced high-integrity/gambling controls. Do not process under a generic collectibles MCC without written acquirer classification. |
| Settlement | **[R]** BCB Resolution 150’s factual test controls marketplace/subcredenciador treatment. Resolution 494 requires payment institutions to obtain authorization before beginning payment services. |
| Creator payout | **[I]** Never pay out proceeds that are still needed for refunds, disputes, delivery, or custodian failure. Release timing and rolling reserve require provider approval. |
| Refund | **[R]** CDC art. 49 gives a seven-day distance-sale withdrawal right; Decree 7.962 requires immediate notice to the financial institution/card administrator for non-posting or reversal. **[U]** Counsel must determine the exact consequence of reveal, token transfer, redemption, and return of the physical card. |
| Chargeback | **[N]** Card-network disputes exist independently of the product’s refund wording. Visa and Mastercard recognize non-receipt and not-as-described/damaged merchandise disputes. Blockchain delivery state does not override issuer/network evidence rules. |
| Loss/damage | **[R/I]** Consumer remedies remain against the responsible supplier. Maintain two different protections: cash reserve for purchase-price reversals and insurance/bond for the physical card’s replacement value. |
| Custodian default | **[I]** A custodian default can produce simultaneous non-delivery claims. Contractual indemnity is insufficient unless backed by insurance, reserve, evidence access, and an operational replacement/refund route. |
| Tax/invoice | **[R]** Physical goods require the applicable NF-e/NFC-e chain; intermediation may create a separate service-document obligation. The 2026 IBS/CBS transition also requires electronic fiscal documents. **[U]** Decide whether creator or platform is the seller before determining invoice issuer, timing, NCM/ICMS, commission treatment, and custody transfers. |

## Wallet, stored-value and NFT boundary

- A one-time payment followed by a card claim is not an internal wallet.
- **[R/I]** A reusable BRL balance or cross-seller credit recorded for future purchases is likely a prepaid payment-account/e-money problem. Law 12.865 defines electronic money and segregates funds held in payment accounts; Resolution 96 imposes identification, operation, and closure rules. Do not create such a balance.
- Refunds go to the original payment method, not platform credit, unless counsel and the PSP approve an optional lawful alternative.
- A “bonus” usable across sellers can reproduce stored-value risk even if called points.
- A non-transferable claim to one specified card may support an argument for Law 14.478’s product/service-access exclusion, but this is **[U]**, not a safe harbor.
- Transferable NFTs, platform custody, wallet-to-wallet transfer, or secondary trading can bring BCB virtual-asset rules and CVM functional classification into scope. Resolution BCB 520 has applied since 2 February 2026; CVM Opinion 40 says an asset-referenced token/NFT may or may not be a security depending on its rights and offering.
- **[N]** Stripe currently prohibits games of chance/sweepstakes with a material prize and secondary NFT sales, while treating first-party NFT sales, stored value, high-value goods, and payment aggregation as restricted. A legal theory that the pack is an ordinary aleatory sale does not force a PSP to accept it.

## Minimum PSP/acquirer question set

Require written answers tied to the complete product flow:

1. Do you classify this as ordinary collectibles commerce, gambling/sweepstakes, commercial promotion, high-integrity risk, NFT sale, or another category?
2. What MCC, transaction indicators, descriptor, and card-network registration apply?
3. Is the platform or each creator the merchant of record and user-final receiver?
4. Must we use direct charges, destination charges, split settlement, or another approved model?
5. Does the flow make the platform a subcredenciador, and who performs centralized settlement?
6. Which entity is debited for refunds, chargebacks, dispute fees, fraud, and negative balances?
7. What reserve, payout delay, volume cap, collateral, or rolling hold is required?
8. What KYB/KYC, beneficial-owner, sanctions, seller-bank-account, and custodian verification is required?
9. Are paid random allocation, material jackpots, first-party NFT minting, external NFT transfers, or secondary trading allowed?
10. What evidence wins non-receipt, damaged/not-as-described, unauthorized-payment, and duplicate-charge disputes?
11. How must Article 49 cancellations be executed before reveal, after reveal, after token transfer, and after shipping?
12. Can creator payout occur before physical delivery, and how is it recovered after seller or custodian default?
13. Are Pix and cards both permitted for this category, and do their refund/fraud controls differ?
14. What fiscal-document and transaction-reconciliation fields must accompany each payment, claim, shipment, refund, and payout?
15. Which changes—jackpot size, odds, NFT transferability, seller type, country, or custody model—require renewed underwriting?

## Pre-money stop conditions

Do not accept even pilot money until:

1. Brazilian counsel fixes the seller/promoter, sale object, title-transfer moment, Article 49 treatment, SPA position, and NFT classification.
2. A licensed PSP/acquirer approves the actual screenshots, terms, odds, jackpot, token, custody, fulfillment, and refund flow in writing.
3. Merchant/subcredenciador status and settlement architecture are documented.
4. Every creator is KYB-approved and paid only through the approved settlement path.
5. Tax counsel fixes invoice issuer, description, timing, and 2026 tax-transition treatment.
6. Refund, chargeback, creator-default, card-loss, and custodian-default reserves are funded and stress-tested.
7. No reusable balance, cash-out, buyback, pooled third-party bank account, or unapproved NFT secondary market exists.
8. Delivery evidence satisfies both custody protocol and network dispute requirements.
9. Test transactions use the approved MCC and production-equivalent flow; no generic e-commerce account is used to “see if it passes.”

## Explicit disagreement findings

**Sunstein tension:** A legally arguable “ordinary aleatory sale” path is not sufficient. Stripe can still reject the business, and Visa can require special coding, registration, monitoring, or gambling treatment. Legal clearance and PSP acceptance are separate gates.

**Acemoglu tension:** Nominal roles collapse under actual money control. If the platform controls checkout, statement descriptor, proceeds, fee extraction, refund decision, chargeback response, and delayed seller payout, calling the creator “merchant” does not make the platform a neutral software intermediary.
