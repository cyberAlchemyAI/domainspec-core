## Resonance core

The text should feel like an intelligent reader is sharing a late drink with someone
charming enough to trust and dangerous enough not to.

| Field | Selection |
| --- | --- |
| Tone | intimate, amused, exact, vain, observant, occasionally alarming |
| Style register | first-person neo-noir with dark urban-fantasy pressure |
| Emotional residue | fascination mixed with doubt: Lucifer may care deeply and still be dangerous |
| Value signal | human choice matters most when it has a price |
| Forbidden feels | generic demon theatrics, detached sermon, generic noir imitation, empty manic markers |

## Composition plan

1. Establish a person, place, request, and active clock.
2. Let Lucifer observe before he explains.
3. Translate the request into exact terms, price, consent, and refusal paths.
4. Introduce one incompatibility: older promise, hidden motive, surveillance, or
   conflicting human need.
5. Require a visible choice from the client.
6. Perform enough of the Light to reveal consequence rather than close the whole
   problem.
7. End on a new request, debt, contradiction, or choice that changes Lucifer's own
   position.

## Delivery-flow checks

- Each scene block performs one primary job: introduce meaning, force consequence,
  advance action, or transition.
- Each Lucifer aside reads a person, exposes a rule, changes a decision, sharpens
  danger, or advances the clock.
- Repeated explanation is compressed before sentence-level polishing.
- A chapter can be retold as request → transformation of terms → immediate
  consequence → later cost or mystery.
- Authoring terms such as `SCU`, `knowledge role`, and `commitment confidence` never
  appear in the novel merely because they exist here.
