# Lucifer Voice Guide

## Core register

Lucifer narrates as though telling one intelligent reader a secret over a drink. He is intimate, amused, exact, vain, observant, and occasionally alarming. He should not sound like a generic noir detective, a stage demon, or an author delivering doctrine through a character mask.

## The tether rule

An internal aside or direct-reader interjection must perform at least one live job:

1. **Read a person** — expose what Lucifer notices about fear, hope, love, stillness, or choice.
2. **Expose a rule** — clarify contracts, limits, price, timing, or preparation.
3. **Change a decision** — make Lucifer or another character act differently.
4. **Sharpen danger** — reinterpret an object, route, pursuer, or apparent coincidence.
5. **Advance the clock** — remind the reader what time or access is being lost.

If an aside performs none of these jobs, tether it, move it to a quieter scene, or cut it.

## The reader-discovery rule

**Make the mechanics explicit and the meaning discoverable.**

State what the reader must understand to follow agency, consent, causality, time, and consequences. Let theme, emotion, attitude, and mythology arrive through an evidence → gap → choice → payoff sequence:

1. Give the reader concrete evidence.
2. Leave a clean gap for interpretation.
3. Let a character make a consequential choice.
4. Pay the meaning off through a later object, action, image, or callback.

When a scene has already demonstrated its meaning, test cutting the next sentence that explains it. If the meaning matters but arrives too early, leave the question or parable unfinished and let the story answer it later. A gap without a traceable payoff is obscurity, not discovery.

## Keep

- Short reactions caused by immediate failure or surprise: “Tough audience.”
- Precise observations that reveal competence: privacy is timing; love is visible in where someone looks.
- Aphorisms that close a live turn: impossible requests versus incompatible ones.
- Mundane/supernatural ambiguity attached to evidence: “Entirely ordinary technology. Probably.”

## Tether or compress

- General declarations about loving humanity when no present human action triggers them.
- Religious commentary after Mara has already forced the scene back to Elena.
- Historical backstory inserted during pursuit.
- Jokes that relieve danger without acknowledging the remaining cost or time.

## Avoid

- Repeated laughter markers or capitals used as a substitute for instability.
- Interjections that merely repeat the chapter's thesis.
- Omniscient claims unsupported by Lucifer's observable access.
- Approval language that converts Mara's autonomous act into Lucifer's achievement.
- Treating Lucifer's mythology as neutral biblical fact.

## Character relationship

Lucifer can be patronizing, but the narrative should know when he is being patronizing. His fascination with Mara grows because she chooses under pressure. Admiration works best when he notices that she acts without needing his permission.

## Pacing test

During revision, read only the scene action and dialogue. Then restore each aside one at a time. If removing an aside makes no difference to character, rule, danger, choice, or time, it has not earned the interruption.
