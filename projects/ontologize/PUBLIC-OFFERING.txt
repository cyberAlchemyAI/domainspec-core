# CyberAlchemy - Public Offering

> Shareable draft. CyberAlchemy is a working name. This is not a priced contract or a promise of
> autonomous decision-making; it is an offer for a scoped founding pilot.

## One-line offer

CyberAlchemy helps operations-heavy companies turn dashboards they only half-trust, scattered team
knowledge, and early AI-agent work into auditable business intelligence and a governed learning loop.

## The problem

Most companies already produce plenty of data. They have dashboards, spreadsheets, CRM exports,
operator notes, status reports, and sometimes AI agents doing pieces of work.

The issue is that the company often cannot answer the questions that matter most:

- Which numbers are actually defensible?
- What did this metric count, divide by, or quietly leave out?
- Which lesson from one run is safe to reuse in the next one?
- What information is private, internal, or shareable?
- Who is allowed to promote a local observation into company knowledge?
- If an AI agent produced or summarized something, what governed its use of evidence?

When those questions stay implicit, dashboards become performance theater, knowledge stays trapped in
people's heads, and agents become more output to supervise instead of infrastructure the business can
trust.

## What CyberAlchemy installs

CyberAlchemy installs a lightweight operating layer over the tools a company already uses. The first
pilot focuses on one business slice, one priority metric, and one governed learning loop.

The layer has four parts:

1. **Evidence-linked business intelligence**  
   A priority KPI is rebuilt so the company can see its denominator, source events, evidence
   references, interpretation limits, and confidence grade. The goal is not another dashboard; it is a
   number the business can defend.

2. **Operational ontology**  
   We map the company's real operating structure: who it serves, what value mechanisms drive outcomes,
   which vocabulary different teams use, and which evidence schemas make sense for each context. This
   prevents one generic metric from erasing important business differences.

3. **Data boundary register**  
   We define which fields and signals are private, internal, shareable, or restricted. Unknown data
   fails closed. This gives human operators and AI agents the same boundary map before anything becomes
   reusable.

4. **Promotion graph**  
   Runs become source-backed events, evidence, candidate lessons, review decisions, and either promoted
   knowledge, rejected claims, private residue, or retired assumptions. Nothing becomes company truth
   just because it appeared in a report.

## What the first pilot proves

The founding pilot is intentionally narrow. It should prove whether the pattern works on real company
data before anyone pretends it is a broad transformation program.

By the end of a successful pilot, the company should have:

- one priority metric traced from raw events to an auditable business observation,
- one agreed definition of what that metric counts and does not count,
- one confidence grade backed by non-empty evidence references,
- one explicit owner for approving reusable lessons,
- one privacy and reuse boundary for the data involved,
- one governed path from operational run to reviewed learning,
- one current-to-target plan for extending the system after the pilot.

The pilot does not sell autonomous management, magic dashboards, or "AI strategy" theater. It sells a
small piece of business intelligence the company can inspect, challenge, and improve.

## Who this is for

CyberAlchemy is a fit for companies that:

- serve more than one customer segment, lane, product line, or operating context,
- have dashboards or reports that leaders use but do not fully trust,
- run repeatable operational cycles such as shipments, orders, production lots, claims, sales motions,
  service tickets, grants, or delivery projects,
- are experimenting with AI agents or AI-assisted work and need governance before scaling it,
- have an operations, data, or AI lead who can work with an executive sponsor.

Good first verticals include logistics, manufacturing, ops-heavy services, sales operations, grant or
bid intelligence, and other workflow-heavy businesses where outcomes can be tied back to source
events.

## Engagement shape

The first engagement follows four stages:

1. **Assessment**  
   Identify the operating slice, the priority metric, source systems, data boundaries, existing
   dashboards, and any AI-agent workflows already in use.

2. **Design**  
   Define the ontology cards, evidence schema, confidence grading rules, owner decisions, and pilot
   promotion graph.

3. **Pilot**  
   Build the thin slice: one metric, one evidence chain, one governed learning loop, and a clear review
   process on the client's real data.

4. **Handoff**  
   Deliver the working pilot package, owner decisions, risk register, and current-to-target transition
   plan.

## What makes it different

Most analytics work asks, "What should the dashboard show?"

CyberAlchemy asks a more operational question: "What would make this number trustworthy enough to
learn from, reuse, and eventually route agent work around?"

That changes the work. The dashboard is only the visible layer. Under it are the definitions,
evidence references, privacy boundaries, review gates, and owner decisions that make the intelligence
durable.

## Founding pilot invitation

I am looking for founding pilot partners with real operational data, an important metric they want to
trust more, and a willingness to examine how company knowledge gets created.

The best first conversation is simple:

- What is one number your team uses but does not fully trust?
- What real-world event should that number be grounded in?
- What decision would improve if the number became auditable?
- Where are AI agents or AI-assisted workflows starting to touch the process?

If those answers are clear enough, the next step is a short assessment and a scoped pilot proposal.
