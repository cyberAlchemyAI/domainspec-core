### 2. Augmentation-move vocabulary

Prefer verbs describing observable human work and inspectable system outputs:

| Move | Recognizable human act | Bounded system contribution | Required retained judgment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orient | Name the objective and current situation | Gather relevant context; expose known gaps | Decide what problem is actually being solved |
| Find | Look for relevant material | Retrieve candidate sources or records | Judge relevance, credibility, and sufficiency |
| Compare | Examine alternatives | Align differences, conflicts, and trade-offs | Choose criteria and interpret consequences |
| Externalize | Put an idea into inspectable form | Draft a map, option, plan, or artifact | Decide whether the representation preserves intent |
| Structure | Organize material for use | Group, sequence, index, or link items | Approve the organizing distinctions |
| Translate | Explain across vocabularies or domains | Propose mappings and identify limits | Decide whether meaning survives the mapping |
| Surface | Notice a gap, risk, contradiction, or dependency | Flag evidence and show why it triggered | Determine significance and response |
| Prepare | Make a possible action reviewable | Draft parameters, files, commands, or handoff | Authorize or reject the proposed action |
| Route | Send work to its proper owner | Identify eligible route and package context | Select the owner and accept the handoff |
| Test | Compare an artifact with a declared expectation | Run checks and preserve observations | Interpret failures and accept/reject evidence |
| Remember | Preserve context for later retrieval | Store source-bound trace and version | Decide admission, correction, expiry, and reuse |
| Hand off | Transfer responsibility with context | Package status, evidence, gaps, and next options | Receiving human explicitly accepts responsibility |
| Recover | Return from error or changed context | Expose prior state, residue, and reversible choices | Choose rollback, correction, continuation, or stop |

Avoid identity metaphors such as “the AI understands,” “knows,” “decides,” or “partners.” Name the operation and artifact instead.

### 3. Operational boundaries

- Recommendation: may rank or present options with inputs, criteria, uncertainty, and alternatives visible. It must not become a decision merely through ranking.
- Preparation: may draft an action and its expected effects. No durable or external effect occurs before the named approval gate.
- Routing: may identify an eligible owner or workflow. Routing does not mean acceptance, completion, promotion, or authority transfer.
- Execution: requires an explicitly owned permission, bounded target, visible result, failure behavior, and recovery path. No inferred consent or silent scope expansion.
- Memory: stores retrievable, source/version-bound context. It does not convert prior text into truth, approval, user preference, or evergreen instruction.
- Testing: records observed behavior against a declared expectation. A passing test proves only its exact fixture, environment, and assertion—not usefulness, comprehension, safety, or production readiness.
- Handoff: packages artifact, status, evidence, unresolved issues, and requested next action. A written handoff is not resolved until the receiver accepts it.
- Adaptation: any learning or personalization requires explicit scope, reviewability, correction, and reset. The current substrate should make no portfolio-wide adaptation claim.

These boundaries operationalize Horvitz’s mixed initiative, Amershi’s correction/dismissal/control guidance, Parasuraman et al.’s function allocation, and NIST’s role differentiation. They remain candidate product rules until admitted by each owner.

### 4. Retained-human-judgment and denied-substitution rules

Retain with a named human/domain owner:

1. Problem definition, desired outcome, and trade-off priorities.
2. Whether sources are credible and adequate for the decision.
3. Domain meaning: story canon, musical quality, safety, fairness, accessibility, product truth, metric interpretation, or research validity.
4. Consent, privacy classification, publication, and reuse.
5. Approval of durable execution or external effects.
6. Whether a test result is decision-relevant and what failure means.
7. Admission or promotion of memory, ontology, definitions, lessons, and status.
8. Acceptance of handoff and responsibility for continuation.
9. Stop, rollback, or exception decisions.
10. Claims about maturity, efficacy, audience understanding, and production readiness.

Denied substitutions:

- Retrieval must not substitute for evidence review.
- A recommendation must not substitute for a decision.
- A drafted command must not substitute for authorization.
- A route suggestion must not substitute for owner acceptance.
- Stored context must not substitute for truth or consent.
- A test pass must not substitute for domain validation.
- A receipt must not substitute for approval.
- Polished copy or UI must not substitute for implemented capability.
- Human review language must not substitute for an effective control mechanism.
- Assistance must not substitute for domain expertise where consequences depend on that expertise.

## 6. Failure cases and kill conditions

- **Status prestige:** mature entries dominate order or visual weight. Keep relevance/order separate from maturity.
